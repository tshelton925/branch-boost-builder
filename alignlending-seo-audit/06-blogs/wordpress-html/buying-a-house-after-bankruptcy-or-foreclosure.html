Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 17, 2026

If a bankruptcy, foreclosure, short sale or deed in lieu is behind you, you can usually buy again after a waiting period. It runs from 12 months into a Chapter 13 plan for FHA, VA and USDA to 7 years after a foreclosure for a conventional loan. In Michigan, the redemption period can move your start date.

Key takeaways

  • Chapter 13 is not a full stop: FHA, VA and USDA can allow a purchase after 12 months of on-time plan payments, with court or trustee approval.
  • Chapter 7 waits differ: about 2 years from discharge for FHA and VA, 3 for USDA, 4 for Fannie Mae.
  • Conventional waits run longest after a foreclosure: 7 years for Fannie Mae, or 3 with extenuating circumstances.
  • Michigan's redemption period can move the date: the sheriff's deed takes effect only when it ends, most often after 6 months.

How long after bankruptcy or foreclosure can you buy a house?

It depends on the event and the loan program. FHA and VA generally allow a purchase about 2 years after a Chapter 7 discharge, or after 12 months of on-time Chapter 13 plan payments with court approval. Fannie Mae generally requires 4 years after Chapter 7 and 7 after a foreclosure.

Each period is a minimum. Reaching it makes you eligible to apply, not approved, and lenders can add stricter rules, called overlays.

How long after Chapter 7 can you get a mortgage?

The Chapter 7 clock runs from the discharge date, not the filing date. FHA and VA generally look for about 2 years, USDA 3 and Fannie Mae 4. Documented extenuating circumstances can shorten them, to as little as 12 months for FHA and 2 years for Fannie Mae.

Each program counts to a different day: HUD to the FHA case-number date, USDA to the application and Fannie Mae to the day the new loan funds.

If the same mortgage was also foreclosed, Fannie Mae applies the longer wait unless the bankruptcy is shown to have discharged that debt. To refinance a home you own, see refinancing after bankruptcy.

Can you buy a house while in Chapter 13?

Yes, with FHA, VA or USDA. You need 12 months of on-time plan payments and written approval of the new mortgage from the bankruptcy court or trustee. Fannie Mae's conventional rules wait until 2 years after the discharge, or 4 years after a dismissal.

HUD asks for the bankruptcy court's written permission, while VA and USDA accept the trustee's or the judge's. Ask your bankruptcy attorney how your trustee handles the request.

For Fannie Mae, two or more filings in 7 years mean 5 years from the latest one, or 3 with extenuating circumstances.

How long after a foreclosure can you buy again?

Generally 3 years for FHA and USDA, about 2 for VA and 7 for a Fannie Mae conventional loan, counted from when the foreclosure was completed or title left your name. A deed in lieu or short sale carries a shorter conventional wait: 4 years, or 2 with extenuating circumstances.

After a foreclosure with extenuating circumstances, Fannie Mae allows only a principal residence purchase or a limited cash-out refinance until 7 years pass, at a lower maximum loan-to-value ratio.

When does the waiting period start after a Michigan foreclosure?

Michigan lenders can foreclose by advertisement, out of court, when the mortgage has a power of sale: a published sheriff's sale, then a redemption period, most often 6 months. Programs count from the completion date or the day title left your name, so get both dates from your county Register of Deeds.

Under MCL 600.3240, redemption lasts 6 months for a home of 1 to 4 units when over two-thirds of the original loan was owed at the foreclosure notice, generally 1 year otherwise, and as little as 30 days if abandoned. The Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA) foreclosure guide calls 6 months the most common.

If you do not redeem, the sheriff's deed takes effect when the period ends (MCL 600.3236), so the sale and title dates can be 6 months or more apart. Fannie Mae counts from the completion date on your credit report, which its automated underwriting assumes is the sale. HUD counts from the day ownership passed to the lender. Ask which date applies.

Does a Michigan property tax foreclosure count the same way?

It is a different process, run by the county treasurer. Michigan Treasury's timeline shows title passing to the foreclosing government on March 31, about 2 years after unpaid taxes turn delinquent. Fannie Mae's guide and HUD's handbook do not address it, so ask.

What counts as extenuating circumstances?

Fannie Mae treats extenuating circumstances as one-time events outside your control that caused a sudden, lasting drop in income or a severe jump in bills, such as a layoff or a serious illness. The lender must document the event and show that default was your only reasonable option.

Fannie Mae's Selling Guide B3-5.3-08 asks for papers that confirm the event, such as a divorce decree, medical bills or a layoff notice, papers showing why you could not resolve it, and your written explanation.

HUD allows an FHA exception for documented events beyond your control, such as a serious illness or a wage earner's death, once good credit is re-established. Divorce alone does not count for HUD, nor does a job transfer that left a home unsold.

What can you do during the waiting period to be ready?

Pay every bill on time. Keep or add a few accounts in good standing, keep card balances low, and save for the down payment and reserves. Keep records of what caused the event, and check your credit reports for errors well before you apply, so the file is ready on day one.

  1. Collect your dates: the discharge or dismissal order, the sheriff's deed, and any short sale or deed in lieu papers.
  2. Check your reports at AnnualCreditReport.com: confirm the dates and that discharged debts show a zero balance.
  3. Rebuild a record: USDA flags even one rent or mortgage payment 30 or more days late in the past 12 months. See credit repair steps before you apply.
  4. Know the score rules: see what credit score you need and buying a home with a low credit score.
  5. Protect the file once you apply: read what not to tell (or do) before closing.

Can you get a mortgage sooner with a non-QM loan?

Sometimes. Non-QM loans follow each lender's own rules, not Fannie Mae, FHA, VA or USDA guidelines. Some lenders accept a shorter time since a bankruptcy or foreclosure. The tradeoff is usually a larger down payment and higher costs, so compare it with waiting for an agency program.

Non-QM loans fall outside the federal qualified mortgage definition, but the lender must still decide in good faith that you can repay (Regulation Z, 12 CFR 1026.43). Waits vary by lender, and none is promised. See non-QM loans in Michigan.

Does a loan modification or forbearance affect buying a home?

Fannie Mae, FHA and USDA set no bankruptcy-style wait after a completed loan modification or forbearance. They look at your payment history instead. Fannie Mae turns away a file with a mortgage payment 60 or more days late in the past 12 months. FHA underwrites by hand if you have made fewer than 3 payments since forbearance ended.

To change the loan on a home you own, see refinancing after a loan modification and refinancing after forbearance.

Questions about buying after bankruptcy or foreclosure

Can I buy a house 2 years after Chapter 7?

Often, with FHA or VA, which generally look for about 2 years from discharge. A Fannie Mae conventional loan generally needs 4 years, or 2 with documented extenuating circumstances, and USDA 3. Reaching 2 years makes you eligible to apply, not approved.

What is the FHA waiting period after foreclosure?

Three years. HUD generally bars a new FHA-insured mortgage when a foreclosure or deed in lieu happened in the 3 years before the case number is assigned, counted from the date title left your name. Documented extenuating circumstances can allow an exception.

Can you buy a house while in Chapter 13?

Yes, with FHA, VA or USDA, after 12 months of on-time plan payments and written permission from the bankruptcy court or trustee. Fannie Mae's conventional rules wait until 2 years after discharge. Ask your bankruptcy attorney how your trustee handles the request.

Does a short sale have a waiting period?

Yes, for most programs. Fannie Mae sets 4 years, or 2 with documented extenuating circumstances. FHA sets 3, or none if every mortgage and installment payment was on time for the 12 months before the sale. Check VA and USDA rules with the lender.

Is the waiting period counted from filing or discharge?

Bankruptcy waits run from the discharge or dismissal date, not the filing date. Foreclosure waits run from completion: the date on your credit report for Fannie Mae, the date title left your name for FHA. In Michigan, that can be months after the sheriff's sale.

Can you get a mortgage after bankruptcy with a low credit score?

It can be possible, but a low score narrows the choices. Each program and lender sets its own credit rules, often with overlays, and Fannie Mae's Desktop Underwriter has no fixed 620 floor for case files created on or after November 16, 2025.

Can you refinance after a loan modification?

Usually, once the program's seasoning rules and your payment history line up. Refinancing has its own waiting rules after a modification, forbearance or bankruptcy, and Align's refinance guides cover them by program. Start there to replace the loan on a home you own.
