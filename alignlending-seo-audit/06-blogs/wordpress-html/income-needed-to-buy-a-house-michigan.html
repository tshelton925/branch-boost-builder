For Michigan homebuyers, there is no single salary you need. In 2025 the median buyer income on Michigan purchase loans was $91,000, and the median home value was $285,000 (Align's HMDA report). Lenders cap housing costs at a share of gross income. In Michigan, the new owner's property tax, not the seller's, takes a large part of that share.

Key takeaways

How much do Michigan homebuyers earn?

In 2025 the median buyer with a Michigan purchase loan reported $91,000 of income. The median home value on those loans was $285,000, about 3.1 times income. Across the 10 counties with the most purchase loans, median values ran from $215,000 in Ingham to $435,000 in Washtenaw. Source: Align Lending, Michigan Mortgage Market Report (2025 HMDA).

How do lenders decide how much income you need?

Lenders divide your monthly debts, including the new housing payment, by your gross monthly income. That is your debt-to-income ratio (DTI). The old 28/36 rule of thumb is a starting point, and automated underwriting often allows more. In 2025, 30% of Michigan purchase loans were made with a DTI above 43% (Align's HMDA report).

The housing ratio counts principal, interest, property tax, insurance, any mortgage insurance and dues. The total ratio adds car, student, card and other debts. As the CFPB's debt-to-income explainer notes, limits differ by loan and lender.

  • Fannie Mae. The Selling Guide, B3-6-02, caps total DTI at 36% on manually underwritten loans, or 45% with qualifying credit and reserves, and at 50% through Desktop Underwriter.
  • Freddie Mac. Guide section 5401.2 sets a 36% guideline and a 45% cap for manually underwritten loans, and Loan Product Advisor weighs the ratio file by file.
  • FHA. HUD Handbook 4000.1 sets manual-underwriting benchmarks of 31% housing and 43% total for credit scores of 580 or higher, with more room for compensating factors.

Lenders may add stricter overlays. In Align's report, 40.8% of denied purchase applications had a DTI of 50% or more, against 5.8% of loans made. See debt-to-income limits by loan type.

How much of your budget does Michigan property tax take?

The year after a home sells, its taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid. At Michigan's 2025 median homestead rate of 36.0 mills, that is about 1.8% of market value a year: about $5,400 on a $300,000 home, or $450 a month.

The rule is MCL 211.27a. In 2026 the reset raised a typical Michigan home's taxable value about 50.5% (Align Lending's 2026 uncapping study), so the listing's tax history understates the buyer's bill.

Hypothetical example, not an offer. Actual rates, costs and payments vary. At a 28% housing ratio, that $450 a month of tax alone uses about $19,300 of yearly gross income.

Millage depends on the city or township, school district and county, and the county medians above run from 25.7 to 53.9 mills. To estimate your Michigan property tax after you buy, use the address's millage. See also how Michigan taxable value resets after a sale.

The 36.0-mill figure assumes you file the Principal Residence Exemption. The seller's exemption does not carry over, so file by June 1 for the summer levy or November 1 for the winter levy.

Can I afford a $300,000 house on a $70,000 salary in Michigan?

Maybe. At a 28% housing ratio, a $70,000 income allows about $1,633 a month for all housing costs. A $300,000 Michigan home at the 36.0-mill median uses about $450 of that for property tax. Insurance, principal, interest and any mortgage insurance must fit in the rest, which depends on your rate, down payment and other debts.

Hypothetical example, not an offer. Actual rates, costs and payments vary.

  2. Subtract the address's property tax: about $450 on a $300,000 home at 36.0 mills, leaving about $1,183.
  3. Subtract a homeowners insurance quote for the address.

What salary do you need for a $200,000, $400,000 or $500,000 home in Michigan?

Work from the price down. Michigan property tax scales with price: at 36.0 mills, about $3,600 a year on a $200,000 home, $7,200 on $400,000 and $9,000 on $500,000. The loan part depends on your rate, down payment and debts, so only a calculator run at your own quote gives the full income answer.

Does a lower income mean you can't buy in Michigan?

No. A lower income means a smaller housing budget, not a closed door. FHA loans, USDA loans in eligible rural areas and the MI Home Loan from the Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA) help buyers who have less cash. A lower-millage address also changes the math. The answer depends on your debts and the address.

FHA loans, insured by HUD and made by FHA-approved wholesale lenders, allow more room on credit history in exchange for longer-lasting mortgage insurance. USDA loans cover eligible rural addresses, with household income limits; see USDA loan requirements.

MSHDA's MI Home Loan pairs with MI 10K down payment assistance, with income and price limits; see MSHDA MI Home Loan. Samantha Shelton, Align's broker owner, explains the MSHDA programs and, if one fits, refers you to a participating lender.

What is the difference between what you qualify for and what you can afford?

Qualifying is a ratio a lender applies to your gross income. Affordability is whether the payment still works after childcare, savings, repairs and everything else your budget really holds. The second number is often lower, and it is the one to shop with. A lender sees the ratio. Only you see the rest of the month.

Your budget runs on take-home pay and carries costs no ratio counts, such as utilities, upkeep and repairs. The guide to budgeting for your first home lists them.

Questions about income and home prices in Michigan

Hypothetical example, not an offer. Actual rates, costs and payments vary. Answers use ratio and tax arithmetic, not loan payments.

How much do you need to make to afford a $300,000 house in Michigan?

No single salary answers it, because the loan part depends on your rate, savings and debts. The property tax alone, about $5,400 a year at Michigan's 36.0-mill median, uses about $19,300 of yearly gross income at a 28% ratio. Add insurance and the loan at your quote.

Can I afford to buy a house if I make $40,000 a year?

Possibly. At a 28% housing ratio, $40,000 of gross income allows about $933 a month for all housing costs, or $1,200 for housing plus debts at 36%. FHA loans and MSHDA's MI Home Loan can help with cash to close. Start from the address's property tax.

What salary do you need for a $400,000 mortgage?

It depends on the rate, so a salary without one is a guess. The tax follows the price, not the loan: each $100,000 of price adds about $1,800 a year at 36.0 mills, using about $6,400 of yearly income at 28%. Then run the loan at your quoted rate.

How much house can I afford at $1,400 a month?

The $1,400 has to cover property tax, insurance and the loan together. Subtract the tax first: at 36.0 mills, a $250,000 home carries about $375 a month. Subtract an insurance quote, and the loan gets the rest. At a 28% ratio, $1,400 a month matches about $60,000 of gross income.

Do lenders use gross or take-home pay?

Gross pay. A debt-to-income ratio divides monthly debts by gross monthly income, before taxes and other deductions. Your own budget runs on take-home pay, which is one reason the amount you qualify for can feel larger than the amount you are comfortable paying.

Does the seller's property tax bill tell me what I will pay in Michigan?

No. The seller's bill reflects a capped taxable value. The year after a home sells, its taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid. In 2026 that reset raised a typical home's taxable value about 50.5%, per Align's uncapping study.

What is the median income of Michigan homebuyers?

$91,000 in 2025, for buyers with first-lien purchase loans on owner-occupied Michigan homes, per Align's Michigan Mortgage Market Report. The median home value on those loans was $285,000. Among the 10 counties with the most purchase loans, median buyer income ran from $80,000 in Genesee to $136,000 in Washtenaw.
