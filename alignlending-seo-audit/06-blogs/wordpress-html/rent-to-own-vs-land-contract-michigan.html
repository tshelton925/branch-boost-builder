Rent-to-own makes you a tenant with a contract to buy later. A Michigan land contract makes you the buyer, paying the seller instead of a bank. A mortgage makes you the owner, with a lender's lien. For a buyer weighing a seller's offer, three things differ most: who holds title, what a missed payment costs, and when property tax resets.

Key takeaways

  • Title: the owner keeps the deed under rent-to-own. A land contract buyer holds equitable title until payoff. A mortgage borrower gets the deed at closing.
  • Default: eviction can start 7 days after a written rent demand. A land contract buyer gets 90 days or 6 months after a forfeiture judgment, a mortgage borrower usually 6 months or 1 year after a foreclosure sale.
  • Property tax: taxable value uncaps the year after a land contract is signed or a home is sold. Under rent-to-own it usually waits for the deed.
  • Route to a mortgage: rent-to-own ends with a purchase loan. Fannie Mae treats a land contract payoff as a refinance once the contract is over 12 months old.

What is the difference between rent-to-own, a land contract and a mortgage?

With rent-to-own, the owner keeps title, you pay a landlord, and a missed payment can mean eviction and losing your option fee. With a land contract, the seller keeps legal title, you pay the seller, and default can end in forfeiture. With a mortgage, you hold title, pay a lender, and risk foreclosure.

Are you a tenant or a buyer under rent-to-own in Michigan?

You are a tenant. Rent-to-own is a lease with a right, or sometimes a duty, to buy at a set price by a set date. You usually pay an option fee, and part of the rent may be credited. Michigan Treasury treats a lease with an option to buy as a rental agreement until you exercise it.

Sellers use the labels loosely: a lease-option lets you walk away, usually losing the fee, while a lease-purchase can commit you to buy. The guide to lease to own vs. rent to own compares the two. Until you buy, Michigan's Truth in Renting Act bars your lease from waiving your remedies for an unfit home or your right to notice in court.

Who owns the home during a Michigan land contract?

You hold equitable title, the buyer's ownership interest, and the seller keeps legal title until the contract is paid off. A land contract is a sale the seller finances: you pay in installments, usually with a balloon payment due on a set date, move in, and usually pay the taxes and insurance.

Michigan tax law counts someone "purchasing property under a land contract" as an owner (MCL 211.7dd), so you can claim the Principal Residence Exemption under a written contract the seller signed. Treasury's PRE guidance recommends recording it and says an assessor can ask for the signed original. See how a Michigan land contract works and seller financing pros and cons.

What happens if you miss a payment on each one?

A rent-to-own tenant can be evicted and lose the option fee and credits. A land contract buyer faces forfeiture or foreclosure, with 90 days or 6 months to catch up after a forfeiture judgment. A mortgage borrower faces foreclosure, then usually 6 months or 1 year to redeem.

Rent-to-own. Eviction can start 7 days after a written demand for unpaid rent (MCL 600.5714). After a judgment, you have 10 days before an order to leave, and paying the judgment and costs stops it (MCL 600.5744).

Land contract. Forfeiture works only if the contract provides for it (MCL 600.5726). After a written notice, you have 15 days to pay or cure (MCL 600.5728). After a judgment, the order to leave waits 90 days if you have paid under half the price, or 6 months if half or more. Paying and curing in that window keeps the contract. Otherwise your payments are usually lost. The seller can instead foreclose in circuit court (MCL 600.3101).

Mortgage. A mortgage with a power of sale can be foreclosed by advertisement, outside court (MCL 600.3201). For a home of up to 4 units, redemption lasts 6 months if over two-thirds of the original loan was owed at the foreclosure notice, 1 year in most other cases, and as little as 30 days if the home is abandoned (MCL 600.3240).

How do property taxes work under each option in Michigan?

Under a land contract, the buyer usually pays the taxes, and taxable value uncaps the year after signing. Under rent-to-own, the owner pays until the deed transfers. With a mortgage, taxable value resets the year after the sale to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid.

A land contract is a transfer of ownership at signing, and taxable value does not reset again when the deed is recorded (MCL 211.27a). A lease counts too if it runs over 35 years with renewals or has a "bargain purchase option", a price of no more than 80% of projected true cash value at lease end, so have your attorney check it.

In 2026 the reset raised a typical Michigan home's taxable value about 50.5% (Align Lending's 2026 Michigan Property Tax Uncapping Study). File a Property Transfer Affidavit (Form 2766) within 45 days, then the exemption by June 1 or November 1. See how Michigan taxable value resets after a sale.

Do rent credits or land contract payments count toward a mortgage?

Rent credits count only under the lender's rules: Fannie Mae limits a credit to the difference between the rent you paid and the appraiser's market rent. Land contract payments build equity you can use when you refinance, and lenders ask for the contract and proof of every payment.

Fannie Mae's Selling Guide B3-4.3-12 also requires a written lease-option with an original term of at least 12 months stating the rent and monthly credit, proof of each payment, and an appraisal showing market rent. A land contract signed more than 12 months before the application is paid off as a limited cash-out refinance on a new appraisal, and cash-out is not allowed (B2-1.3-05). A seller's promise that your rent "goes toward the house" binds no lender.

How do you move from rent-to-own or a land contract to a mortgage?

Start a lender review well before the option deadline or balloon date. Lenders look at your credit, income, payment history and an appraisal. A land contract payoff may be financed as a purchase or a refinance, depending on the program and when the contract was signed.

  1. Find the deadline: the option or balloon date in the contract.
  2. Check your readiness early, so there is time to work on credit or savings. See credit repair steps before you apply.
  3. Keep the paper trail: the contract, proof of every payment, and the seller's written rent-credit or payoff statement. The guide on how to get preapproved for a mortgage lists the other documents lenders want.
  4. Apply with time left for the appraisal and title work.

What should you ask before you sign a rent-to-own or land contract deal?

Have a Michigan real estate attorney review the agreement first. Confirm the seller owns the home and whether a mortgage on it could be called due. Record the land contract or a memorandum of it. Get the price, credits, deadlines and default terms in writing.

  1. Does a title search show the seller owns the home free of other claims?
  2. Could the seller's lender call its mortgage due? Federal due-on-sale rules do not shield a land contract or a lease with an option to buy (12 CFR 191.5).
  3. Will the contract or a memorandum be recorded with the county Register of Deeds?
  4. Who pays for major repairs?
  5. Who insures the home, and is your interest named?
  6. When is the balloon or option deadline, and what if you cannot get a mortgage by then?
  7. Is the price fixed now or set later?
  8. How will option fees and rent credits be documented for a lender?

Questions about rent-to-own and land contracts in Michigan

Is a land contract better than rent-to-own?

It depends on what you need. A land contract makes you the buyer: you hold equitable title and build equity, but carry taxes, repairs and a balloon date. A lease-option lets you walk away, usually losing the option fee. Have an attorney compare the actual contracts.

Who pays property taxes on a land contract in Michigan?

Usually the buyer, because the contract says so. Taxable value uncaps for the year after the contract is signed, not when the deed is recorded at payoff. The buyer files a Property Transfer Affidavit within 45 days and can claim the Principal Residence Exemption.

Can you claim the homestead exemption on a land contract in Michigan?

Yes. Michigan tax law counts a person purchasing property under a land contract as an owner for the Principal Residence Exemption, often called the homestead exemption. Use a written contract signed by the seller, and file the affidavit by June 1 or November 1.

Do rent-to-own payments count toward a down payment?

Only part of them, and only under the lender's rules. Fannie Mae counts no more than the difference between the rent you paid and the appraiser's market rent, under a written lease-option with an original term of at least 12 months. Ask a lender to review it first.

What happens at the end of a land contract if you cannot get a mortgage?

The balloon payment is still due. You can ask the seller for a written extension, sell and pay off the contract, or keep working toward a loan. If you miss it, forfeiture gives you 15 days after the notice, then 90 days or 6 months after a judgment.

Is rent-to-own a good idea with a low credit score?

It can buy time, but it costs money if the plan fails: the option fee and rent credits can be lost if no mortgage comes through by the deadline. Before you sign, find out how far your credit really is from a mortgage.

Do you need a lawyer for a land contract in Michigan?

Yes, have one review it before you sign. A Michigan real estate attorney can check the title, the forfeiture and balloon terms and the seller's mortgage, and record the contract. Align is a mortgage broker and does not give legal advice.
