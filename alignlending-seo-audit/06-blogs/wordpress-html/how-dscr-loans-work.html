Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 17, 2026

For investors buying a one- to four-unit rental, a debt-service coverage ratio (DSCR) loan qualifies the property on its rent, not your income. The lender divides the rent by the full monthly housing cost: principal, interest, taxes, insurance and association dues. Many lenders look for 1.0 or more. In Michigan, a rental pays more property tax than an owner-occupied home.

Key takeaways

  • The formula: DSCR = monthly rent ÷ monthly PITIA; 1.0 means the rent exactly covers the cost.
  • What counts as rent: the signed lease, or the appraiser's market rent on a vacant unit, often whichever is lower.
  • Lenders set the minimum: DSCR loans follow each lender's guidelines, not Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac rules.
  • Michigan rentals pay non-homestead millage: up to 18 more school operating mills, on a taxable value that resets after you buy.

What is a DSCR loan?

A debt-service coverage ratio (DSCR) loan is a mortgage for investment property that qualifies on the property's rent compared with its housing cost, not on your tax returns or W-2s. Private lenders make DSCR loans under their own non-QM guidelines, not Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac rules. They are for rentals only, never a home you live in.

Non-QM means non-qualified mortgage, a loan written outside federal qualified-mortgage standards. Federal Truth in Lending rules treat credit for a rental its owner does not live in as business-purpose credit, outside Regulation Z (official interpretation of 12 CFR 1026.3(a), comment 3(a)-4). That is why a DSCR lender can look at the property instead of your paycheck.

For program specifics, see DSCR loan requirements in Michigan.

How is the DSCR ratio calculated?

To calculate DSCR, divide the property's gross monthly rent by its full monthly housing cost, called PITIA: principal, interest, taxes, insurance and association dues. A ratio of 1.0 means the rent exactly covers the cost. Above 1.0, the rent covers it with room to spare. Below 1.0, the rent falls short of the cost.

DSCR = gross monthly rent ÷ monthly PITIA

Some lenders instead divide annual net operating income by annual debt service, which subtracts operating costs first and shows a lower number. Ask which method a lender uses.

What DSCR do lenders require?

No regulation sets a minimum DSCR, so each lender sets its own. Many lenders look for a ratio of about 1.0 or higher, and some offer better terms at higher ratios. Some allow a ratio below 1.0 if you bring a larger down payment or more reserves.

A larger down payment raises the ratio. In Michigan, the tax line usually lowers it after you buy.

What counts as rent for a DSCR loan?

For a DSCR loan, lenders count the signed lease on a unit that is already rented, or the appraiser's market rent estimate on a vacant unit or a new purchase. Many lenders use the lower of the two when both exist. Some lenders accept short-term rental income, usually with a booking history or a market study.

The appraiser's estimate comes on Fannie Mae's Single-Family Comparable Rent Schedule (Form 1007) for one unit, or in the Form 1025 appraisal report for two to four units. Fannie Mae's rental income rules for the subject property rely on both, and DSCR lenders often use the same forms.

Nightly income also depends on whether the city or township lets you operate. Read about short-term rental regulations and investing in short-term rentals first.

How do Michigan property taxes affect a DSCR loan?

A Michigan rental gets no Principal Residence Exemption, so it pays up to 18 mills of school operating tax that an owner-occupied home does not. The year after a home sells, its taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid. Both raise the ratio's tax line.

Michigan Treasury's Principal Residence Exemption page says the exemption "exempts an owner’s principal residence from the local school operating millage, up to 18 mills." Treasury's 2025 millage rate list shows both rates; in most places the gap is the full 18 mills.

The reset adds to it. Align's 2026 Michigan Property Tax Uncapping Study puts the typical jump in taxable value the year after a sale at 50.5%, so the seller's tax bill tells you little about yours. Here is how Michigan taxable value resets after a sale.

What else do DSCR lenders check?

Besides the ratio, DSCR lenders usually check your credit, cash for the down payment and closing costs, and reserves of several months of housing costs. They review the appraisal and its rent schedule, and some ask about your experience as a landlord. They also ask how you will hold title: in your own name or in an LLC.

Each lender sets these items, and the DSCR loan page linked above lists the common current standards.

How does a DSCR loan compare with a conventional investment property loan?

A conventional investment property loan qualifies you on your personal income and tax returns under Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac rules, and usually costs less if you qualify. A DSCR loan skips personal income but usually costs more and may carry a prepayment penalty. A portfolio loan is kept by the lender, under its own rules.

What are the downsides of a DSCR loan?

DSCR loans usually cost more than conventional loans, many carry a prepayment penalty, and down payment and reserve requirements run larger. The biggest risk is a thin ratio: a vacancy, a rent cut or a tax reassessment can push it below 1.0. Run the numbers with conservative rent and the new owner's tax bill.

A prepayment penalty matters if you may sell or refinance within a few years. Read that clause in the note, and see how to refinance an investment property.

Do Michigan cities regulate rentals in ways that affect DSCR income?

Yes. Many Michigan cities require rental registration and periodic inspections before a unit can be rented, and some cities and townships limit short-term rentals through zoning or licensing. A unit that cannot legally be rented on your plan's terms cannot produce the income the ratio assumes, so check local rules before you count the rent.

Michigan's Housing Law lets local governments run rental inspection programs by ordinance (MCL 125.526). Detroit requires a registered rental to pass an inspection for a Certificate of Compliance from the city. Grand Rapids runs a rental certification program. Questions about how DSCR loans work

What is a good DSCR ratio?

It depends on the lender, but 1.0 is the usual floor, because at 1.0 the rent exactly covers the monthly housing cost. Many lenders offer better terms at higher ratios, and room above the minimum also cushions vacancy, repairs and a Michigan tax reset.

Can you get a DSCR loan with no rental history?

Often, yes. On a vacant unit or a new purchase, lenders usually use the appraiser's market rent estimate instead of a lease. History matters more for short-term rental income, where many lenders want a booking record or a market study before they count nightly rent.

Does a DSCR loan count toward your personal debt-to-income ratio?

It can. The DSCR loan is qualified on the property, but the mortgage still shows on your credit report. A later lender that uses your personal income decides how to count it. Under Fannie Mae's rules, for example, the rental's full payment is subtracted from its documented net rent, and any shortfall counts as debt.

Can you use a DSCR loan for a short-term rental?

Some lenders allow it, often with a higher minimum ratio and a booking history or market study. Local rules matter as much. Many Michigan cities and townships regulate short-term rentals through zoning, licensing or inspections, and a unit that cannot operate legally produces no qualifying income.

Can you use a DSCR loan for a home you will live in?

No. A DSCR loan is for investment property only, and lenders usually ask you to sign a statement that you will not live in it. If you plan to live in one unit of a two- to four-unit building, look at owner-occupied loans, which use your personal income.

Are DSCR loans only for experienced investors?

No, not always. Many lenders allow first-time investors, sometimes with a larger down payment, more reserves or a higher minimum ratio. Some limit first-time investors to certain property types or loan sizes. Ask about experience rules early, because they differ from lender to lender.
