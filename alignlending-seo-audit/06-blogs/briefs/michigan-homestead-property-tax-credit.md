## 5. Outline

**Above the fold (≤60 words), must say:** the homestead property tax credit is a Michigan income tax credit, claimed on Form MI-1040CR, that returns part of the property tax (or a share of the rent) on your Michigan home when it is high compared with your household income; eligibility depends on household resources and your home's taxable value, with special rules for seniors and people with disabilities.

**Key takeaways (4):** who can claim (owners and renters) · the two limits (household resources; taxable value) · filed with or without a state return · different from the PRE.

**Module CTA after the eligibility H2.**

### H2 What is the Michigan homestead property tax credit?
Answer (40–60 words) must say: a refundable credit on the Michigan income tax return, filed on Form MI-1040CR, for people whose property taxes, or the part of rent treated as property tax, are high relative to total household resources; you can claim it even if you do not owe income tax `[VERIFY: refundability and filing without a return]`.

### H2 Who qualifies for the homestead property tax credit?
Answer must say: Michigan residents who own or rent their Michigan homestead, with total household resources at or under the current limit and, for owners, a homestead taxable value at or under the current cap; full-year and part-year rules apply; seniors, people with disabilities and veterans have special calculations.
- **Table (dated, tax year 2026):** Rule · Current figure · Source. Rows: household resources limit, taxable value cap, renter share of rent treated as tax, maximum credit, phase-out start `[VERIFY all with Michigan Treasury MI-1040CR instructions]`.

### H2 How much is the Michigan homestead property tax credit?
Answer must say: the credit is a percentage of the property tax that exceeds a set share of your household resources, up to a maximum; seniors with low income can receive a larger share; the exact figures change each tax year, so use Treasury's current instructions or tax software.
- Hypothetical example (F12 label): household resources $40,000; property tax $3,600. Walk through Treasury's steps with the tax-year figures `[VERIFY formula percentages before writing the numbers]`. Tax figures only; no loan figures.

### H2 How is the homestead property tax credit different from the homestead exemption?
Answer must say: the Principal Residence Exemption lowers your tax bill by exempting the home from up to 18 mills of school operating tax and is filed with the assessor (Form 2368, June 1 / November 1, F5); the homestead property tax credit is claimed later on the state income tax return and returns part of the tax you paid.
- **Two-column table:** PRE vs. MI-1040CR (what it does, who files, where, when, income test, renters).
- Link "Michigan's Principal Residence Exemption" → `/learn/homestead-exemption-benefits-and-filing-guide/`.

### H2 How do you file the homestead property tax credit?
Answer must say: file Form MI-1040CR with your Michigan return or by itself, using your property tax bills (or rent paid and landlord information) and your household resource total; Treasury allows filing for prior years within a limited window `[VERIFY: 4-year rule]`.
- Ordered list (visible), 4 steps.

### H2 Can renters claim the Michigan property tax credit?
Answer must say: yes; renters treat a set share of the rent paid on their Michigan home as property tax and claim the credit on the same form, subject to the household resources limit `[VERIFY: share of rent]`.

### H2 Do seniors get a property tax break in Michigan?
Answer must say: there is no automatic reduction at 65; seniors may get a larger homestead property tax credit, can ask about a summer tax deferment, and low-income owners can apply to the local board of review for a poverty exemption `[VERIFY each]`.
- Link (new, link once live) "getting a mortgage at 60, 70 or 80" → `/learn/mortgage-for-seniors/`.

### H2 What happens to the credit in the year after you buy a Michigan home?
Answer must say: the year after a sale, taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid (F16), so your property tax usually rises; for an eligible household a higher tax can mean a larger credit, subject to the taxable value cap and the maximum.
- One sentence with Align data (50.5% typical jump in 2026). Note on prorations: use the taxes levied on your homestead for the year as Treasury defines them `[VERIFY]`.
