## 3. Search intent and the reader's problem

Informational with a real decision behind it. The reader is 55 to 80, retired or close to it, and wants to downsize, move closer to family, buy a condo or refinance. They have heard lenders won't give a 30-year loan at 75, or that Social Security "doesn't count". They want to know their rights, how retirement income and savings are counted, and whether to carry a mortgage into retirement at all. Many are in Michigan and moving between Michigan homes, which triggers the property tax reset.

## 4. The angle that beats current results

1. **The law first:** the Equal Credit Opportunity Act and Regulation B bar lenders from denying credit or setting terms because of age (with narrow exceptions) `[VERIFY: 12 CFR 1002.6(b)(2)]`. A 75-year-old can take a 30-year loan.
2. **How each retirement income counts**, in one table: Social Security retirement, SSDI (link the disability post), pensions, IRA/401(k) distributions (continuance rule), annuities, and asset-based qualifying (Fannie's "employment-related assets" and non-QM asset depletion) `[VERIFY: Fannie Mae B3-3.1-09 and related]`, plus grossing up non-taxable income `[VERIFY]`.
3. **The Michigan downsizing surprise:** selling a long-held home and buying another resets taxable value on the new one. Align's 2026 Uncapping Study puts the typical jump at about 50.5%, so a smaller home can carry a property tax bill close to or above the old one. A worked tax-only example makes this concrete.
4. **Michigan relief worth checking:** the homestead property tax credit has rules for seniors (link the new post), the senior summer tax deferment `[VERIFY: MCL 211.51 / Treasury]`, and the poverty exemption through the local board of review `[VERIFY]`.
5. **An honest "pay it off or keep it" section** framed as trade-offs, with the not-advice line, and reverse mortgages mentioned in one neutral sentence with a HUD link (D1).

## 5. Outline

**Above the fold (≤60 words), must say:** yes; federal law bars lenders from turning you down or shortening your term because of age, so you can get a 30-year mortgage at 60, 70 or 80 if your income and credit qualify; lenders count Social Security, pensions, retirement withdrawals and, in some programs, savings; Michigan movers should budget for the property tax reset.

**Key takeaways (4):** ECOA age rule · retirement income types that count · term length is your choice · Michigan's post-sale tax reset (about 50.5% typical in 2026).

**Module CTA after the income H2.**

### H2 Can a lender deny you a mortgage because of your age?
Answer (40–60 words) must say: no; the Equal Credit Opportunity Act and Regulation B prohibit age discrimination in credit, so a lender cannot deny you or limit the term because you are older; it can only consider age in narrow, permitted ways, and it still checks income, credit and the property.
- One sentence on what to do if you think it happened (CFPB complaint link).

### H2 Can you get a 30-year mortgage at 70 or 75?
Answer must say: yes; the term is your choice, not a function of age; some older borrowers prefer a shorter term to finish sooner, others prefer the lower required payment of a longer term and plan to pay extra or sell; both are allowed.
- No payment comparison figures (trigger terms). Link "15-year vs. 30-year mortgage" → `/learn/15-year-vs-30-year-mortgage-comparison/`.

### H2 How do lenders count Social Security and retirement income?
Answer must say: lenders count Social Security retirement, pensions and annuities with an award letter or statements; retirement account withdrawals count if they can continue for at least three years `[VERIFY]`; non-taxable income such as Social Security may be grossed up under program rules `[VERIFY]`.
- **Table:** Income type · What lenders usually ask for · Continuance or special rule. Rows: Social Security retirement, SSDI, pension, annuity, IRA/401(k) distributions, interest/dividends, rental income. `[VERIFY each against Fannie Mae B3-3.1-09 and HUD 4000.1]`
- Link (new, link once live) "buying a home on SSI or disability income" → `/learn/buying-a-home-on-ssi-or-disability-income/`.

### H2 Can you qualify for a mortgage using your savings instead of income?
Answer must say: sometimes; Fannie Mae lets lenders count income from certain retirement assets under set rules, and non-QM asset depletion loans divide eligible assets over a set period to create qualifying income; terms and costs differ from standard loans.
- Link "asset depletion loans in Michigan" → `/product-loans/asset-based-income-loans/` (money page owns the commercial term).

### H2 What are the mortgage options for people over 60?
Answer must say: the same options as anyone (conventional, FHA, VA for eligible veterans, USDA in eligible areas) plus a cash purchase, a bridge or buy-before-you-sell plan, and, as a separate product, a reverse mortgage for homeowners 62 and older `[VERIFY: HUD HECM age rule]`.
- One neutral sentence on reverse mortgages: "A reverse mortgage (HECM) is a different product with HUD-required counseling; see HUD's HECM page." `[DECISION D1 PENDING: link to /learn/reverse-mortgages-how-they-work/ only if Align confirms it brokers reverse mortgages]`
- Link "plan a buy-before-you-sell move" → `/purchase/buy-before-you-sell/`.

### H2 What happens to your property taxes when you downsize in Michigan?
Answer must say: buying a new Michigan home resets its taxable value the next year to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid (F16); because your old home's taxable value was capped for years, a smaller home can cost as much or more in property tax.
- Hypothetical example (F12 label): you sell a home whose taxable value had been capped at about $90,000 and buy a $260,000 condo. At the statewide median 36.0 homestead mills, the new home's tax after the reset is about $4,680 a year (taxable value about $130,000 × 36.0 ÷ 1,000), against about $3,240 on the old home at the same millage. Taxes only; no loan figures.
- Link "estimate your Michigan property tax after you buy" → `/michigan-property-tax-calculator/`; "how Michigan taxable value resets after a sale" → `/learn/michigan-taxable-value/`.

### H2 What Michigan property tax help is there for seniors?
Answer must say: there is no automatic senior property tax cut in Michigan; seniors may qualify for the homestead property tax credit on the state return, a summer tax deferment and, with low income, a poverty exemption from the local board of review `[VERIFY each]`.
- Links (new, link once live): "Michigan homestead property tax credit" → `/learn/michigan-homestead-property-tax-credit/`; "Michigan's Principal Residence Exemption" → `/learn/homestead-exemption-benefits-and-filing-guide/`.

### H2 Should you pay off your mortgage before you retire?
Answer must say: it depends on your income, savings, taxes and the rate on your loan; paying it off removes a payment, keeping it can keep savings available; there is no single right answer, so compare your own numbers and talk with a tax or financial professional.
