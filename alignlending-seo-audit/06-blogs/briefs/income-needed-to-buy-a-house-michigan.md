## 3. Search intent and the reader's problem

Informational, pre-shopping. The reader has a salary and a price in mind ("$70,000 and a $300,000 house") and wants a yes or no. National answers assume a rate and a national tax rate. Michigan readers need the tax piece right, because the new-owner tax can be far above the listing's tax history.

## 4. The angle that beats current results (and stays compliant)

Competitors answer with a payment built on an assumed interest rate, which Align never publishes (F13) and which ages in weeks. We answer in three parts that need no rate:
1. **What Michigan buyers actually earned** (HMDA 2025), by county: the value-to-income ratio was about 3.1 statewide, 2.6 to 3.6 across the counties shown.
2. **How lenders turn income into a ceiling:** debt-to-income limits (28/36 rule of thumb, program limits higher with automated approval; in 2025, 30% of Michigan purchase loans were approved above 43% DTI, per Align's report). At a 28% housing ratio, each $100 of monthly housing cost needs about $4,300 of yearly gross income.
3. **How much of that the Michigan property tax takes:** at the statewide median 36.0 mills, the new-owner tax on a $300,000 home is about $5,400 a year, about $450 a month, which by itself uses about $19,300 of yearly income at a 28% housing ratio. The rest of the budget (principal, interest, insurance, any PMI) comes from the affordability calculator at the reader's quoted rate.

Payment amounts and rates never appear; every figure is income, a ratio limit or a tax. Label any example (F12) and flag tables `[COMPLIANCE REVIEW]`.

### H2 How do lenders decide how much income you need?
Answer must say: lenders divide your monthly debts, including the new housing payment, by your gross monthly income; the old 28/36 rule of thumb is a starting point, and automated underwriting often allows more; in 2025, 30% of Michigan purchase loans were approved with a debt-to-income ratio above 43% (Align HMDA report).
- **Table: housing budget by income (ratio arithmetic, not a payment):** income $50,000 / $70,000 / $100,000 → 28% of gross monthly ≈ $1,167 / $1,633 / $2,333; 36% ≈ $1,500 / $2,100 / $3,000. Header: "Maximum monthly housing cost at each ratio, before other debts". `[COMPLIANCE REVIEW]`
- Link "debt-to-income limits by loan type" → `/learn/debt-to-income-ratio-dti/`.

### H2 How much of your budget does Michigan property tax take?
Answer must say: the year after you buy, taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid (F16); at the statewide median 36.0 mills that is about 1.8% of market value a year, so a $300,000 home carries about $5,400 a year, about $450 a month.
- Hypothetical example (F12 label): $450 a month of property tax alone uses about $19,300 of yearly gross income at a 28% housing ratio.
- One sentence: "In 2026 the reset raised a typical Michigan home's taxable value about 50.5% (Align Lending Uncapping Study), so the listing's tax history understates the buyer's bill."
- Link "estimate your Michigan property tax after you buy" → `/michigan-property-tax-calculator/`; "how Michigan taxable value resets after a sale" → `/learn/michigan-taxable-value/`.

### H2 Can I afford a $300,000 house on a $70,000 salary in Michigan?
Answer must say: maybe; at a 28% housing ratio a $70,000 income allows about $1,633 a month for all housing costs; a $300,000 Michigan home at the median 36.0 mills uses about $450 of that for property tax, and insurance, principal, interest and any mortgage insurance must fit in the rest, which depends on your rate, down payment and other debts.

### H2 What salary do you need for a $200,000, $400,000 or $500,000 home in Michigan?
Answer must say: use the same three steps; the property tax part scales with price (about $3,600, $7,200 and $9,000 a year at 36.0 mills), and the loan part depends on your rate and down payment, so the calculator gives the real answer for your quote.

### H2 Does a lower income mean you can't buy in Michigan?
Answer must say: no; programs such as FHA, USDA in eligible areas and MSHDA's MI Home Loan with MI 10K help buyers with less cash, and a lower-tax community changes the math; the answer depends on your debts and the address.

### H2 What is the difference between what you qualify for and what you can afford?
Answer must say: qualifying is a ratio a lender applies to your gross income; affordability is whether the payment still works after childcare, savings, repairs and the things your budget really holds; the second number is often lower, and it is the one to shop with.
