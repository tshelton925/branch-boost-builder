## 3. Search intent and the reader's problem

Informational qualification check. The reader has heard USDA loans need no down payment and is in or near an eligible area (much of outer Metro Detroit and small-town Michigan). They already know the address and income tests exist and now want the rest: what credit score, how much debt is too much, what the house must pass, who can live there and be on the loan, and whether they can have a second USDA loan.

## 4. The angle that beats current results

1. **All the borrower tests in one table** with the exact USDA source chapter for each (HB-1-3555), instead of a scattered list.
2. **Straight answers to the questions people actually type:** no USDA-set minimum credit score, but GUS automated approval usually needs 640 `[VERIFY: HB-1-3555 ch. 10]`; 29% housing and 41% total debt ratios, with GUS able to approve higher `[VERIFY: ch. 11]`; no maximum loan amount on Guaranteed loans, because income and DTI cap it `[VERIFY]`.
3. **The Michigan math:** USDA's 29% housing ratio includes property tax. Michigan resets taxable value the year after a sale (Align's 2026 Uncapping Study: about 50.5% typical jump; 36.0 mills statewide median, about 1.8% of market value a year after the reset), so a lender must use the new-owner tax. A tax-only example shows how much of the 29% the tax can take.
4. **Household rules that surprise people:** every adult in the household counts toward the income test even if not on the loan; non-occupant co-borrowers are not allowed `[VERIFY]`.
5. **Michigan property notes:** private wells and septic (link the existing well and septic guides), condo approval, acreage and outbuildings, manufactured homes `[VERIFY each]`.

### H2 What credit score do you need for a USDA loan?
Answer must say: USDA does not set a minimum score in its handbook, but its automated system (GUS) generally needs 640 or higher for an automated approval; below that, or with thin credit, lenders may manually underwrite with stricter documentation, and many lenders add their own minimum `[VERIFY]`.
- H3 Can I get a USDA loan with a 550 credit score? (PAA) Honest answer: very unlikely with most lenders; explain manual underwriting and rebuilding.
- Link "what credit score you need, by loan type" → `/learn/whats-good-credit-score-to-buy-house/`.

### H2 What is the maximum debt-to-income ratio for a USDA loan?
Answer must say: USDA's standard ratios are 29% of gross monthly income for the housing payment (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, the annual fee and any HOA dues) and 41% for all debts; GUS can accept higher ratios with strong compensating factors `[VERIFY: HB-1-3555 ch. 11]`.
- H3 How do Michigan property taxes affect your USDA ratio? Hypothetical example (F12 label): household income $78,000 → 29% housing limit about $1,885 a month. A $240,000 home at the statewide median 36.0 homestead mills carries about $4,320 a year in new-owner tax, about $360 a month, which uses about 19% of that housing limit before principal, interest, insurance and the annual fee. State the limit as arithmetic on income and the tax as tax; no loan payment, no rate. `[COMPLIANCE REVIEW]`
- Links: "how debt-to-income ratio is calculated" → `/learn/debt-to-income-ratio-dti/`; "estimate your Michigan property tax after you buy" → `/michigan-property-tax-calculator/`.

### H2 How is household income counted for a USDA loan?
Answer must say: USDA counts the income of every adult who will live in the home, not just the borrowers, then subtracts allowances for children, household members 62 or older, and some childcare and medical costs before comparing it with the county limit `[VERIFY: HB-1-3555 ch. 9]`.
- One sentence + link: "check your county's current income limit" → `/usda-eligibility-check/`. Do not reproduce the county table (the tool and product page own it).
- H3 Can my partner live with me if they are not on the loan? (PAA "Can my boyfriend live with me…") Yes, but their income counts toward the household limit.

### H2 Is there a maximum USDA loan amount?
Answer must say: Guaranteed loans have no set maximum; your income, debt ratios and the appraised value decide how much you can borrow; USDA's Direct loans (made by USDA Rural Development itself) do have area loan limits `[VERIFY]`.
- Link "USDA Direct vs. Guaranteed loans" → `/learn/usda-loan-basics/` (basics owns the comparison).

### H2 Who can be on a USDA loan, and can you use a co-signer?
Answer must say: borrowers must live in the home; non-occupant co-signers are not allowed on USDA Guaranteed loans `[VERIFY]`; a co-borrower who will live there can be added if they meet the credit and income rules.
- Link "co-signing a mortgage, risks and benefits" → `/learn/co-signing-mortgage-risks-and-benefits/`.

### H2 Can you have two USDA loans or use the program twice?
Answer must say: generally one at a time, because the home must be your primary residence and you cannot own another adequate home in the area; you can use USDA again after selling, and limited exceptions exist (for example a relocation beyond commuting distance) `[VERIFY]`.
- H3 Can I use a USDA loan for a rental property? (PAA) No: primary residence only.

### H2 What property and house rules does a USDA loan have?
Answer must say: the home must be modest for the area, safe and sanitary, with no income-producing use; condos must be in an approved project; land and outbuildings are allowed only as typical for the area; wells and septic must meet local health rules `[VERIFY: HB-1-3555 ch. 12]`.
- H3 What will fail a USDA inspection? (PAA) Explain that USDA uses the appraisal and local standards; common issues: water, septic, roof, health and safety. Link "buying a home with a septic system" → `/learn/buying-home-with-septic-system/` and "well water and home value" → `/learn/how-well-water-impacts-homes-value/`.
- H3 Can you buy land or a hobby farm with a USDA loan? `[VERIFY]` Link "buying a home in a rural area" → `/learn/tips-for-buying-home-in-rural-area/`.

### H2 What disqualifies you from a USDA loan?
Answer must say: most often the address or the household income; after that, credit below what the lender will accept, debt ratios that GUS will not approve, owning an adequate home nearby, or a property with an income-producing use or major repair needs.
