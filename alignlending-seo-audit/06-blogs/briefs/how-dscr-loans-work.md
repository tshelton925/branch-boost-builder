## 5. Outline

**Above the fold (≤60 words), must say:** a DSCR loan qualifies an investment property on its own rent instead of your personal income; the lender divides the property's monthly rent by its full monthly housing cost (principal, interest, taxes, insurance and any HOA dues); a result at or above the lender's minimum, often around 1.0, can qualify `[VERIFY]`; in Michigan, rental property taxes run higher than homestead taxes.

**Key takeaways (4):** the formula · what counts as rent · lenders set their own minimum ratio · Michigan rentals pay non-homestead millage, so taxes weigh more in the ratio.

**Module CTA after the worked example.**

### H2 What is a DSCR loan?
Answer (40–60 words) must say: a debt-service coverage ratio loan is a non-QM mortgage for investment property that qualifies on the property's rent compared with its payment, not on your tax returns or W-2s; it is made by private lenders under their own guidelines, not by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, and is for rentals only.

### H2 How is the DSCR ratio calculated?
Answer must say: divide the property's gross monthly rent by its full monthly housing cost, PITIA (principal, interest, taxes, insurance and association dues); a ratio of 1.0 means the rent exactly covers the cost; above 1.0 the rent covers it with room to spare; below 1.0 it falls short.
- Formula box.
- Hypothetical example (F12 label): monthly rent $2,000; monthly housing cost $1,700 (from your Loan Estimate, plus taxes, insurance and dues) → DSCR = 2,000 ÷ 1,700 = 1.18. State that the $1,700 is an assumed figure for illustration, not a payment Align offers. `[COMPLIANCE REVIEW]`
- Tool link: **Run the DSCR calculator** → `/product-loans/dscr-loans-michigan/#p-dscr` (the products-b team's tertiary link label).

### H2 What DSCR do lenders require?
Answer must say: each lender sets its own minimum, commonly near 1.0, with better terms often available at higher ratios and some lenders allowing below 1.0 with more cash down or reserves `[VERIFY: no regulatory source exists; describe as lender practice and avoid specific cutoffs unless sourced]`.

### H2 What counts as rent for a DSCR loan?
Answer must say: lenders use the lease on a rented property or the appraiser's market rent estimate on a vacant one, and many use the lower of the two; short-term rental income is accepted by some lenders with a track record or a market study `[VERIFY]`.
- Link "short-term rental regulations" → `/learn/navigating-short-term-rental-regulations/`; "investing in short-term rentals" → `/learn/investing-in-short-term-rentals/`.

### H2 How do Michigan property taxes affect a DSCR loan?
Answer must say: a Michigan rental does not get the Principal Residence Exemption, so it pays the non-homestead school operating millage, up to 18 more mills, and its taxable value resets the year after you buy to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value (F16); both raise the tax line in the ratio.
- Hypothetical example (F12 label): a $250,000 rental. At the statewide median 36.0 homestead mills, tax after the reset would be about $4,500 a year (taxable value about $125,000). Adding up to 18 non-homestead mills brings it to about $6,750 a year, about $190 a month more in the ratio's denominator. `[VERIFY: 18-mill figure and that the homestead median plus 18 is a fair illustration; local millage varies]`
- Link "estimate your Michigan property tax after you buy" → `/michigan-property-tax-calculator/`; "how Michigan taxable value resets after a sale" → `/learn/michigan-taxable-value/`.

### H2 What else do DSCR lenders check?
Answer must say: credit, cash for the down payment and closing, reserves of several months of payments, the appraisal and rent schedule, your experience as a landlord for some lenders, and how you will hold title (personally or in an LLC) `[VERIFY: typical items]`.
- One sentence + link to the product page for specific requirements (owner): "DSCR loan requirements in Michigan" → `/product-loans/dscr-loans-michigan/`.

### H2 How does a DSCR loan compare with a conventional investment property loan?
Answer must say: a conventional investment loan uses your personal income and tax returns under Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac rules and usually costs less if you qualify; a DSCR loan skips personal income but usually costs more and may carry a prepayment penalty; a portfolio loan is held by the lender with its own rules.
- **Table:** DSCR · Conventional investment · Portfolio. Rows: how you qualify, documents, typical down payment described as "larger than for a home you live in" (no percentages, §7.2), prepayment penalty, who it fits. No rates.
- Links: "what a portfolio loan is" → `/learn/what-is-portfolio-loan/`; "buying a home as an investment property" → `/learn/buying-home-as-investment-property/`.

### H2 What are the downsides of a DSCR loan?
Answer must say: higher cost than an agency loan, prepayment penalties on many loans, larger down payment and reserve requirements, and the risk that a vacancy or a tax reassessment pushes a thin ratio below 1.0; run the numbers with conservative rent and the new-owner tax.

### H2 Do Michigan cities regulate rentals in ways that affect DSCR income?
Answer must say: many Michigan cities require rental registration and periodic inspections before a unit can be rented, and some limit short-term rentals; a unit that cannot legally be rented on your plan's terms cannot produce the income the ratio assumes `[VERIFY: cite 1–2 city ordinance pages by name]`.
