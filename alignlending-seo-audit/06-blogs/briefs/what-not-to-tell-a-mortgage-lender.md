## 3. Search intent and the reader's problem

Informational, slightly anxious. The reader worries that something in their life (a side job, a past late payment, cash at home, a child support order, a plan to change jobs, a pregnancy) will sink the loan, and has seen online advice to keep quiet. They want to know what lenders legally ask and cannot ask, what will come out anyway, and what actions between application and closing cause trouble.

## 4. The angle that beats current results

1. **Lead with the law, not tricks:** knowingly false statements on a loan application are a federal crime `[VERIFY: 18 U.S.C. §1014]`, and underwriting verifies most of it anyway (credit report, tax transcripts, bank statements, employment verification).
2. **What lenders cannot consider** under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act: race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age (with limits), receipt of public assistance, or exercising consumer credit rights `[VERIFY: 12 CFR 1002.4, 1002.6]`; plans to have children cannot be asked or used `[VERIFY: 12 CFR 1002.5]`. This is the honest version of "what not to tell".
3. **What you must share, with Michigan examples** a national article misses: support payments set through the Friend of the Court, land contract payments you make, delinquent Michigan property taxes, a state tax lien from Michigan Treasury, ownership of another Michigan home with a Principal Residence Exemption `[VERIFY each as an underwriting item]`.
4. **A "before closing" don't-list** in one table, with why each matters and what to do instead.
5. **How to explain things:** the letter of explanation (link to the underwriting guide's sample).

## 5. Outline

**Above the fold (≤60 words), must say:** you should not hide or misstate anything material on a mortgage application, because lenders verify income, assets and debts and false statements can be a federal crime; what you do not need to discuss are things lenders are not allowed to consider, such as plans to have children; the bigger risk is what you do before closing.

**Key takeaways (4):** nothing material to hide · what ECOA bars lenders from considering · the 6 most common pre-closing mistakes · explain unusual items early with a letter of explanation.

**Module CTA after the first H2.**

### H2 Is there anything you should not tell a mortgage lender?
Answer (40–60 words) must say: nothing material should be hidden; the application asks about income, debts, assets, property and legal matters, and lenders check them; you are not required to discuss things the law bars lenders from considering, and you should not guess or volunteer opinions about your future that are not facts.

### H2 What can't a mortgage lender ask you or use against you?
Answer must say: under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, lenders cannot discriminate based on race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age (within limits), receipt of public assistance, or exercising consumer credit rights; they cannot ask about plans to have children; some demographic questions on the application are for government monitoring and are optional `[VERIFY: HMDA/URLA demographic section]`.
- Link "Equal Credit Opportunity Act" → CFPB.
- Fair lending line: "Align Lending is an Equal Housing Opportunity company."

### H2 What do you have to tell a mortgage lender?
Answer must say: every source of income you want counted, all debts and monthly obligations including support payments, assets and where your down payment comes from (including gifts), other real estate you own, and legal matters such as bankruptcies, foreclosures, judgments or lawsuits the application asks about.
- **Table: What to share · Why lenders ask · How it is verified.** Rows: income, debts, child support/alimony (Michigan Friend of the Court orders), gifts (gift letter), other homes, land contract payments, tax liens and delinquent property taxes, past bankruptcy/foreclosure, pending job change. `[VERIFY: Fannie Mae URLA / Form 1003 sections]`
- Link "earnest money deposits" → `/learn/earnest-money-deposits/` (source-of-funds context).

### H2 What happens if you leave something out?
Answer must say: most omissions surface in underwriting through the credit report, tax transcripts, bank statements or verification calls, which can delay or end the approval; a knowingly false statement on a loan application can be a federal crime; disclosing early gives your loan officer time to find the right program.
- `[NEEDS SAMANTHA QUOTE: why she wants to hear about the complicated part of a file on the first call]` (do not reuse the stock "Mine is complicated" line).

### H2 What should you not do after applying for a mortgage?
Answer must say: until closing, do not open new credit, finance a car or furniture, change jobs without telling your loan officer, move large sums without a paper trail, make cash deposits you cannot document, co-sign for someone, or miss any payment; each can change your approval.
- **Table: Don't · Why it matters · Do this instead.** 8 rows. No time promises (F10).
- Link "how to improve your debt-to-income ratio" → `/learn/how-to-improve-your-debt-to-income-ratio/`.

### H2 What should you do if something changes before closing?
Answer must say: tell your loan officer right away (a new job, a raise, a lost job, a new debt, a large deposit or a change in who will live in the home); many changes are workable if the lender learns early, and some need updated documents or a new disclosure.
- Link "home closing process" → `/learn/home-closing-process/`.

### H2 How do you explain a late payment, gap in work or large deposit?
Answer must say: underwriters ask for a short, factual letter of explanation with dates and documents; it should say what happened, why it will not continue, and what proof is attached.
- Two sentences + link "letter of explanation, with a sample" → `/learn/mortgage-underwriting/` (anchor targets the refreshed H2).

### H2 What should you tell a mortgage broker on the first call?
Answer must say: the goal (buy, refinance or use equity), your rough income and debts, anything unusual in your credit or work history, how much cash you have and where it is, and your timeline; a first conversation does not require a credit pull and lets the broker point you to programs that fit.

## 6. FAQ (visible = FAQPage, word for word)

1. **What should you not tell a mortgage lender?** (PAA)
2. **What not to say to a mortgage broker?** (PAA)
3. **Do you have to tell a lender you are pregnant or plan to have children?** No; ECOA bars it (answer carefully; mention that a planned leave that changes income may matter to the lender) `[VERIFY: 12 CFR 1002.5(d)(4); Fannie Mae temporary leave policy]`.
4. **Do lenders check bank statements for cash deposits?** Yes; undocumented large deposits need sourcing `[VERIFY: Fannie Mae B3-4.2-02]`.
5. **Can you change jobs while getting a mortgage?** Tell the loan officer first; same-line-of-work changes are often fine, others may need documentation.
6. **Is it illegal to lie on a mortgage application?** Yes; cite 18 U.S.C. §1014 `[VERIFY]`.
7. **Do I have to tell my lender about a side business?** Only if you want the income counted, but any related debts or tax liabilities must be disclosed `[VERIFY]`.
