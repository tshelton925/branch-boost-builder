### H2 How long after bankruptcy or foreclosure can you buy a house?
Answer (40–60 words) must say: it depends on the event and the program; FHA and VA generally allow a purchase about two years after a Chapter 7 discharge and during or after Chapter 13 with court approval; conventional loans generally require four years after Chapter 7 and seven after a foreclosure `[VERIFY all]`.

### H2 How long after Chapter 7 can you get a mortgage?
Answer must say: the period runs from the discharge date: about two years for FHA and VA, three for USDA and four for conventional loans, with shorter periods possible for documented extenuating circumstances `[VERIFY]`.

### H2 Can you buy a house while in Chapter 13?
Answer must say: FHA and VA can allow a purchase after 12 months of on-time plan payments with the bankruptcy court's or trustee's written permission; conventional loans generally wait two years after discharge or four after dismissal `[VERIFY]`.

### H2 How long after a foreclosure can you buy again?
Answer must say: generally three years for FHA, two for VA, three for USDA and seven for conventional loans, measured from the completion date; a deed in lieu or short sale usually carries a shorter conventional wait `[VERIFY]`.

### H2 When does the waiting period start after a Michigan foreclosure?
Answer must say: most Michigan mortgages are foreclosed by advertisement, followed by a sheriff's sale and a redemption period, usually six months for most homes; lenders use the completion date reported on your credit report or foreclosure documents, so get the sheriff's deed and the end of redemption dates from the county Register of Deeds `[VERIFY: agency definitions and Michigan practice]`.
- H3 Does a Michigan property tax foreclosure count the same way? `[VERIFY]`
- Link "Michigan Register of Deeds" (county) generic explanation; no portal links.

### H2 What counts as extenuating circumstances?
Answer must say: a one-time event outside your control, such as a serious illness, a death of a wage earner or a sudden loss of income, that caused the bankruptcy or foreclosure and has been resolved; lenders want documents showing the cause and that it is unlikely to recur `[VERIFY: Fannie Mae B3-5.3-08]`.

### H2 What can you do during the waiting period to be ready?
Answer must say: keep every payment on time, add or keep a few accounts in good standing, keep credit card balances low, save for the down payment and reserves, keep records of the cause, and check your credit reports for errors well before you apply.
- Links: "credit repair steps before you apply" → `/learn/credit-repair-strategies-for-home-buyers/`; "what credit score you need" → `/learn/whats-good-credit-score-to-buy-house/`.

### H2 Can you get a mortgage sooner with a non-QM loan?
Answer must say: some non-QM lenders lend sooner after a bankruptcy or foreclosure, sometimes within a year or two, in exchange for a larger down payment and a higher cost; compare it against waiting for an FHA, VA or conventional loan.
- Link "non-QM loans in Michigan" → `/product-loans/non-qm-loans/`.

### H2 Does a loan modification or forbearance affect buying a home?
Answer must say: a completed loan modification or forbearance usually does not carry a bankruptcy-style waiting period, but lenders review payment history after it, and some programs require a set number of on-time payments `[VERIFY]`.
- Links: "refinancing after a loan modification" → `/learn/refinancing-after-loan-modification/`; "refinancing after forbearance" → `/learn/how-to-refinance-mortgage-in-forbearance/`.
