## 3. Search intent and the reader's problem

Informational with an eligibility check. The reader is a Michigan first-time buyer (or a repeat buyer in a targeted area) with moderate income who has heard "MSHDA" from an agent, a friend or a city page. They want to know four things fast: do I qualify, what are the income and price limits for my county and household, is it a grant or a loan, and how do I actually apply. Most are also confused about whether MSHDA lends directly and whether any lender can do it.

## 4. The angle that beats current results

Current results are MSHDA's own pages (accurate but spread across several URLs), national roundups that copy outdated amounts ($7,500, $25,000) and lender pages that say "we offer MSHDA" without the rules. We win by being the one page that:
1. Puts every eligibility test in **one dated table**: first-time or targeted-area status, county income limit by household size, the 2026 sales price limit, credit minimum, education, occupancy.
2. Says plainly that **MSHDA loans go only through MSHDA participating lenders** (F2) and how to find one, with Align's role stated honestly.
3. Settles the confusing numbers: MI 10K is up to $10,000 as a **deferred loan, not a grant**; the $25,000 First-Generation program is **closed** (F1); the $7,500 figure is an older amount `[VERIFY]`.
4. Adds the **Michigan cost a program page never mentions**: the year after you buy, taxable value resets (Align's 2026 Uncapping Study: about 50.5% typical jump; statewide median homestead rate 36.0 mills, about 1.8% of market value a year after the reset). An MSHDA buyer's payment has to carry the new-owner tax, not the seller's.
5. Gives an honest "when MSHDA is not the best fit" section with a comparison table (no rates).
