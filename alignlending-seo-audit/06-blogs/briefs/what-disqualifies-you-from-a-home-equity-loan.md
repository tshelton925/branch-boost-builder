## 3. Search intent and the reader's problem

Informational, often after a denial or before applying. The reader owns a Michigan home, has equity on paper (often from a Zestimate-style number) and wants cash for a roof, tuition or debt. They either got turned down or worry they will. They want the list of deal-breakers, which ones they can fix, and how fast.

## 4. The angle that beats current results

National articles list generic factors. We add:
1. **Michigan's own denial numbers** from Align's 2025 HMDA report, quotable in one sentence: home equity applications were denied about twice as often as purchase loans.
2. **The math that decides most files: combined loan-to-value (CLTV).** Show the formula with a hypothetical example and explain that lenders cap CLTV (often somewhere between 80% and 90% `[VERIFY: typical program caps; cite a GSE or regulator source if available, otherwise say "many lenders" without a number]`), so "equity" on paper is not the amount you can borrow.
3. **Michigan-specific disqualifiers** a national article misses: delinquent property taxes heading toward county tax foreclosure, owning on a **land contract** (you hold equitable title, not the deed), an MSHDA MI 10K or city DPA lien that must be repaid or subordinated `[VERIFY]`, a home listed for sale, an estate or trust title.
4. **A fix-it order** for the top two reasons (DTI and credit), tied to Align's no-credit-pull conversation.
5. One line noting that Texas, where Align is also licensed, has its own home equity rules, so this guide covers Michigan.

## 5. Outline

**Above the fold (≤60 words), must say:** the most common reasons are too little equity once the lender applies its combined loan-to-value cap, a debt-to-income ratio that is too high, and credit history; in Michigan in 2025 lenders denied about 30% of HELOC applications against 15.5% for purchase loans (Align HMDA report); most reasons can be checked before you apply.

**Key takeaways (4):** the 30.1% / 27.3% / 15.5% denial comparison · CLTV, not "equity", sets the line size · DTI and credit history were the top reasons for Michigan home equity denials in 2025 · a land contract, unpaid property tax or a recent sale listing can stop a file regardless of credit.

### H2 How much equity do you need for a HELOC?
Answer must say: lenders limit the combined loan-to-value, all mortgages divided by the appraised value; the cap varies by lender and program, so the line you can get is the cap times the value minus what you owe, not your full equity.
- Formula box: CLTV = (first mortgage balance + new line) ÷ appraised value.
- Hypothetical example (F12 label): value $300,000, first mortgage $180,000, an 85% cap → $255,000 − $180,000 = up to $75,000 line before other tests. State it as arithmetic on the cap, not an offer. `[COMPLIANCE REVIEW]`

### H2 What debt-to-income ratio do you need for a home equity loan?
Answer must say: the new payment counts in your debt-to-income ratio along with the first mortgage, so DTI is the most common deal-breaker; limits vary by lender; in 2025, debt-to-income was a leading reason for Michigan home equity denials.
- Link "how debt-to-income ratio is calculated" → `/learn/debt-to-income-ratio-dti/`.

### H2 What credit score do you need for a HELOC or home equity loan?
Answer must say: there is no single federal minimum; each lender sets its own, and recent late mortgage payments, collections or a fresh bankruptcy weigh more than the score alone; credit history was the other leading reason for 2025 Michigan denials.
- H3 Can you get a home equity loan with a 520 credit score? (supporting kw) Honest answer: unlikely with most lenders; options are to wait and rebuild, or compare a cash-out refinance, which has its own credit rules. ### H2 Which properties and ownership situations disqualify a home equity loan?
Answer must say: lenders usually decline or restrict homes that are listed for sale, investment properties under some programs, certain manufactured homes, homes held in some trusts or an estate, and homes bought on a land contract, where you hold equitable title rather than the deed.
- Michigan bullets: land contract buyers (link "how a Michigan land contract works" → `/learn/michigan-land-contract/`) · MSHDA MI 10K or city DPA liens `[VERIFY: subordination policy]` · delinquent property taxes (Michigan county tax foreclosure process `[VERIFY: Michigan Treasury / General Property Tax Act timeline]`) · unpaid contractor liens.

### H2 Does a recent bankruptcy or foreclosure disqualify you?
Answer must say: usually for a period; lenders set waiting periods after a bankruptcy, foreclosure or short sale, and home equity lenders are often stricter than first-mortgage programs.

### H2 What can you fix before you apply, and how fast?
Answer must say: pay down revolving balances to lower DTI, correct credit report errors, document income, clear tax delinquencies and title issues, and get a realistic value before applying; most of this shows up in a first conversation, before any credit pull.
