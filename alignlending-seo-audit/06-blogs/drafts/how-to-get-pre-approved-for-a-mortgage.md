# How to get preapproved for a mortgage in Michigan: steps, documents and timeline

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 16, 2026

To get preapproved for a mortgage, you share your income, assets and debts with a lender, authorize a credit check and send documents the lender reviews before it issues a conditional letter. Michigan homebuyers should check one more thing: that the lender figured your payment with the new owner's property tax, which can run well above the seller's bill.

**Key takeaways**

- **6 steps:** check your credit and budget, gather documents, choose a lender or broker, apply and authorize the credit check, answer underwriting questions, then receive the letter.
- **Core documents:** for most W-2 employees, a pay stub dated within 30 days of the application, W-2s for the most recent 2 years, the 2 most recent monthly bank statements, photo ID and a list of debts, under Fannie Mae's Selling Guide.
- **The credit check:** a preapproval usually includes a hard inquiry, and the CFPB says multiple mortgage credit checks within a 45-day window count as one inquiry. [VERIFY: CFPB "What exactly happens when a mortgage lender checks my credit?"; some older FICO versions use 14 days]
- **Letters expire:** lenders set an expiration, often tied to how old your credit report and pay stubs are. Fannie Mae generally allows credit documents up to 4 months old on the note date. [VERIFY: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B1-1-03]

This guide covers how to get preapproved. If you are still deciding whether you need a letter at all, start with [why preapproval matters](/learn/importance-of-mortgage-preapproval/).

## How do you get preapproved for a mortgage, step by step?

You get preapproved in 6 steps: check your credit and budget, gather your documents, choose a lender or broker, apply and authorize the credit check, answer the underwriter's questions, and receive the letter. A real preapproval rests on documents someone has reviewed. A letter based only on what you typed into a form is a prequalification.

1. **Check your credit and set your budget.** Look at your credit reports for errors before a lender does; [the CFPB explains how to get free copies and dispute mistakes](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/credit-reports-and-scores/). Then decide what monthly payment you are comfortable with, property tax and insurance included, before anyone tells you the maximum you qualify for.
2. **Gather your documents.** Pull together pay stubs, W-2s or tax returns, bank statements, photo ID and a list of your debts. The table in the next section shows what changes for self-employed borrowers, retirement income and gift funds.
3. **Choose a lender or broker.** A bank or credit union offers its own loan programs. An independent mortgage broker compares several wholesale lenders on one application. Ask each one what its letter will verify.
4. **Apply and authorize the credit check.** The application covers your income, assets, debts and work history, and asks for permission to pull your credit. Once a lender has your name, income, Social Security number, a property address, an estimated value and the loan amount, federal rules treat that as an application, and the lender must send a [Loan Estimate within 3 business days](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/subpart-C/section-1026.19). [VERIFY: 12 CFR 1026.19(e); [CFPB: what information you must provide to receive a Loan Estimate](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-information-do-i-have-to-provide-a-lender-in-order-to-receive-a-loan-estimate-en-1987/)]
5. **Answer underwriting questions.** Expect requests to explain large deposits, gaps in employment or recent credit inquiries. Answer with documents where you can, because a written explanation usually needs a paper trail behind it.
6. **Receive the letter.** The letter names a loan amount and loan type and lists its conditions, such as an appraisal and a final review of your file. It is conditional, not a commitment. Loan approval is subject to underwriting, credit, income, asset, appraisal and property eligibility review.

This guide covers the preapproval step in depth. For the full sequence from application to closing day, see [our mortgage process, step by step](/mortgage-process/).

## What documents do you need to get preapproved?

Most buyers need recent pay stubs, W-2s for the last 2 years, the 2 most recent monthly bank statements, photo ID and details on their debts. Self-employed borrowers usually add 2 years of tax returns, or 12 to 24 months of bank statements for a bank statement loan. Gift funds need a signed gift letter.

Fannie Mae's Selling Guide sets the baseline most conventional lenders work from. A pay stub must be dated no earlier than 30 days before the application and show year-to-date earnings, and W-2s must cover the most recent 1 or 2 years, depending on the type of income ([Fannie Mae: standards for employment documentation](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.1-02/standards-employment-documentation)). Bank and investment accounts are verified with the 2 most recent monthly statements, or the most recent quarterly statement ([Fannie Mae: verification of deposits and assets](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-4.2-01/verification-deposits-and-assets)). FHA, VA and USDA loans follow their own agency rules, and any lender can ask for more.

| Document | W-2 employee | Self-employed | Retirement income | Gift funds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Photo ID | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes, for the borrower |
| Income | Pay stub dated within 30 days of the application; W-2s for the most recent 1 or 2 years | Personal federal tax returns, usually 2 years, plus business returns if you own 25% or more of the business; bank statement loans use 12 to 24 months of statements instead | Social Security or pension award letter, or statements showing regular retirement account distributions | Not income; a gift is counted as an asset |
| Assets | 2 most recent monthly statements for each account you will use | Same, plus business account statements if business funds go toward closing | Same, including retirement account statements | Signed gift letter from the donor stating no repayment is expected, plus proof the money moved to you |
| Debts and other items | Debts not on your credit report, such as child support; a divorce decree if it sets payments | Same | Same | Same |

Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide, [chapter B3-3 (income)](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3/income-assessment) and [B3-4.3-04 (personal gifts)](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-4.3-04/personal-gifts), for conventional loans. Bank statement loans are non-QM loans set by each lender's guidelines. [VERIFY each row against the current Fannie Mae Selling Guide document requirements; B3-3.1-02 may now be numbered B3-3.2-01]

## Does getting preapproved hurt your credit?

Usually only a little. A preapproval normally includes a hard credit inquiry, which can lower a score slightly. The CFPB says multiple mortgage credit checks within a 45-day window count as one inquiry, so comparing lenders in that window costs about the same as applying once. Talking with a lender or broker first needs no credit pull.

The shopping window is a feature of credit-scoring models, not a federal law, and its length depends on the scoring version a lender uses ([CFPB: what happens when a mortgage lender checks my credit](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-exactly-happens-when-a-mortgage-lender-checks-my-credit-en-2005/)). [VERIFY: CFPB 45-day window; some older FICO versions used in mortgage lending apply 14 days] If you want room for both, keep your applications within about 2 weeks. Each inquiry still appears on your report; the scoring model groups mortgage inquiries in the window and counts them once.

What matters more than the inquiry is the score and history the lender sees. For the numbers each program looks at, see [what credit score you need](/learn/whats-good-credit-score-to-buy-house/).

## What credit score and income do you need to get preapproved?

There is no single number. FHA, VA, USDA and conventional programs each set credit rules or leave minimums to lenders. For conventional loans, Fannie Mae's Desktop Underwriter (DU) evaluates credit without a fixed 620 floor for case files created on or after November 16, 2025, though lenders may add overlays. Income counts through your debt-to-income ratio (DTI).

An overlay is a lender's own rule on top of the agency's, so two lenders can answer the same file differently ([Fannie Mae: Selling Guide announcement SEL-2025-09](https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/news-events/announcement-sel-2025-09-selling-guide-updates)). On the government side, [HUD's Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1](https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/OCHCO/documents/4000.1hsgh.pdf) sets FHA's minimum credit score at 500 and asks for 580 or higher for maximum financing. [VERIFY: HUD 4000.1, minimum decision credit score] VA and USDA loans add their own agency underwriting rules.

Your DTI is your total monthly debt payments divided by your gross monthly income. The housing payment in that math includes property tax, which is why the Michigan tax figure later in this guide matters: a payment built on the seller's capped tax bill understates your DTI. Limits differ by program; see [debt-to-income limits by loan type](/learn/debt-to-income-ratio-dti/). For how income turns into a price range here, see [income needed to buy a house in Michigan](/learn/income-needed-to-buy-a-house-michigan/) <!-- new, link once live -->.

## How long does preapproval take, and how long does a letter last?

Once your documents are in, preapproval can take anywhere from the same day to a few business days, depending on how complete the file is and how many questions come up. The lender sets the letter's expiration date. Your pay stubs, bank statements and credit report must still be current when you close, so expect to send updates.

For conventional loans, Fannie Mae's rule is that credit documents, including the credit report, pay stubs and bank statements, can be no more than 4 months old on the note date, the day you sign at closing ([Fannie Mae: allowable age of credit documents](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b1-1-03/allowable-age-credit-documents-and-federal-income-tax-returns)). [VERIFY: Fannie Mae B1-1-03, which documents the 4-month limit covers] Many lenders write their letters to expire on a similar schedule. A long home search usually means a refreshed letter, new pay stubs and statements, and sometimes a new credit report.

No one can promise a timeline before seeing the file. What helps is on your side: a complete set of documents the first time, and quick answers to follow-up requests.

## Is there such a thing as instant mortgage preapproval?

Not in the way that matters to a seller. An instant online result is a prequalification based on what you typed, not a review of your documents. The CFPB notes that lenders use these terms differently, so ask what each letter verified. Use online tools to estimate, then get a document-backed preapproval before you make an offer.

Quick online tools have a place. A calculator or a fast prequalification shows a price range before you share documents. The [CFPB says neither kind of letter is a guaranteed loan offer](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/whats-the-difference-between-a-prequalification-letter-and-a-preapproval-letter-en-127/). [VERIFY: CFPB en-127 URL and wording] Michigan sellers and their agents generally expect a preapproval backed by reviewed documents when an offer comes in. For a side-by-side of the two, see [prequalified vs. preapproved](/learn/prequalified-vs-preapproved-whats-difference/).

## Where should you get preapproved: a bank, a credit union or a broker?

No single place fits everyone. Compare a bank, a credit union and an independent mortgage broker on the loan programs each can offer, the Loan Estimate each gives you and how clearly each explains your options. A bank or credit union offers its own programs. A broker compares several wholesale lenders on one application.

Whoever you choose, the comparison that counts is on paper. A lender that receives a full application must send a [Loan Estimate](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/loan-estimate/), the standard federal form, so you can line up loan costs, lender credits and cash to close side by side. [Check my Loan Estimate](/compare-loan-estimates/) reads a Loan Estimate PDF on your device and compares two offers box by box. For how the three kinds of lender differ, see [mortgage broker vs. bank vs. credit union in Michigan](/learn/benefits-of-working-with-mortgage-broker/).

A preapproval from one lender does not lock you in. You can switch before closing, though changing lenders late can crowd the financing deadline in your purchase agreement.

## What should Michigan buyers know about preapproval?

Ask the lender to budget with the new owner's property tax, because the year after a home sells, its taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid. Your purchase agreement sets a financing deadline, and Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA) loans require a participating lender.

Align Lending's [2026 Michigan Property Tax Uncapping Study](/research/michigan-property-tax-uncapping-study/) puts the typical jump in a home's taxable value the year after it sells at about 50.5% statewide, and the statewide median homestead rate at 36.0 mills (2025 rates), about 1.8% of market value a year after the reset. Lenders qualify you on the full payment, taxes included. A payment built on the seller's capped bill makes both your budget and your DTI look better than they will be. Before you ask for a letter, [estimate your Michigan property tax after you buy](/michigan-property-tax-calculator/).

| Michigan item | What it means for your preapproval | What to ask the lender |
|---|---|---|
| Property tax after the sale | The year after a sale, taxable value resets to the state equalized value ([MCL 211.27a](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-27a)), so the seller's bill is not your bill | "Which tax figure is in my payment?" |
| Financing contingency date | Most Michigan purchase agreements set a date by which your loan must be approved [VERIFY: standard Michigan purchase agreement form] | "Can my file be approved by this date?" |
| MSHDA programs | [MSHDA's MI Home Loan](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/mi-home-loan) and its other loans are made only through participating lenders | "Are you a participating lender, or can you refer me to one?" |
| Letter amount | The letter can show the price you plan to offer rather than your maximum, so the seller does not see your ceiling | "Can you write the letter for my offer price?" |

## What can void a preapproval after you have the letter?

A preapproval can change or be canceled if your file changes after the letter: new debt, a job change, large deposits you cannot document, missed payments, or a change in the price or loan type. Until closing, tell your loan officer before you make any financial change, including opening a card or moving money between accounts.

- **New debt.** A car loan, a new credit card or a financed furniture purchase adds a monthly payment and can push your DTI past the program's limit.
- **A job change.** A new employer, a move to commission or self-employment, or a gap in work means income has to be verified again. Lenders typically confirm employment again shortly before closing. [VERIFY: Fannie Mae B3-3.1-07, verbal verification of employment within 10 business days of the note date]
- **Large deposits you cannot document.** Lenders have to source the money you use to close, and cash without a paper trail may not count. [VERIFY: Fannie Mae B3-4.2-02]
- **Missed payments.** A late payment reported after your preapproval can lower your score and change the result.
- **A different home or loan.** A higher price, a different property type or a switch to another loan program can mean a new review.

## Questions about mortgage preapproval

### How long does it take to get preapproved for a mortgage?

It depends on your file more than the calendar. With complete documents, a preapproval can come back the same day or within a few business days. Missing pages, self-employment income, gift funds or credit questions add time, because each needs a document and a review. No one can promise a timeline before seeing your file, so send everything at once and answer follow-up requests quickly.

### Does preapproval hurt your credit score?

Slightly. The hard inquiry in a preapproval can lower a score a little. The CFPB says multiple mortgage credit checks within a 45-day window count as one inquiry, so you can compare lenders without the effect stacking up. A conversation with a lender or broker before you apply needs no credit pull at all.

### What disqualifies you from getting preapproved?

Credit history and debt are the reasons lenders give most often. On denied Michigan purchase applications in 2025, lenders cited credit history and debt-to-income ratio each 42% of the time, and not enough cash to close 22%, according to Align Lending's Michigan Mortgage Market Report (2025 HMDA data). Income you cannot document, recent late payments and cash you cannot source can also stop a preapproval.

### Can you get preapproved with more than one lender?

Yes. You can apply with more than one lender and compare the results, and mortgage credit checks inside a short shopping window count as one inquiry for scoring. Once a lender has a full application, including a property address, it must send a Loan Estimate within 3 business days, so you can compare costs on the same form. A broker can compare several wholesale lenders on one application.

### How much house can I get preapproved for?

A lender preapproves you up to a maximum based on your income, debts, credit and cash, with property tax and insurance in the payment. That maximum is not a spending target. Decide the monthly payment you are comfortable with first. Align's budget review and the home affordability calculator help you set that number before you ask for a letter.

### Do I need a preapproval to make an offer in Michigan?

Not by law, but most sellers and listing agents expect one. A preapproval backed by reviewed documents shows the seller that a lender has already looked at your income, assets and credit. That matters in Michigan because the purchase agreement usually sets a date by which your financing must be approved.

### What is the difference between prequalification and preapproval?

A prequalification is an estimate based on what you tell a lender, usually without documents or a full review. A preapproval rests on documents the lender has reviewed and a credit check, and because lenders do not all use the terms the same way, ask what each letter verified. Align's prequalified vs. preapproved guide covers the rest.
