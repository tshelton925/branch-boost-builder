# Michigan homestead property tax credit: who qualifies, how much and how to file

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 16, 2026

<!-- .al-answer -->
For Michigan homeowners and renters, the homestead property tax credit is a state income tax credit, claimed on Form MI-1040CR, that returns part of the property tax on your home, or a share of your rent, when it is high compared with your household income. It depends on household resources and, for owners, taxable value, with special rules for seniors and people with disabilities.

*The figures in this guide are Michigan Treasury's limits for tax year 2025 (claims filed in 2026), the latest year Treasury had posted when this guide was updated. Treasury adjusts them each year. This is general education, not tax advice. Confirm figures with Michigan Treasury's current MI-1040CR instructions or a tax professional.* [VERIFY: replace every tax year 2025 figure with Treasury's tax year 2026 figures before publishing; Treasury's tax year 2026 credit page returned "not found" when checked on November 16, 2026.]

<!-- .al-takeaways -->
**Key takeaways**

- **Owners and renters can claim it.** In most cases, renters count 23% of the rent they paid as property tax (Treasury, tax year 2025).
- **Two limits decide who qualifies.** For tax year 2025, total household resources must be $71,500 or less, and an owner's taxable value $165,400 or less. The maximum credit is $1,900.
- **You can file it with or without a state return.** It goes with your MI-1040 or by itself, and Treasury accepts claims up to four years after the original due date.
- **It is not the homestead exemption.** The Principal Residence Exemption (PRE) lowers your bill through the assessor. The credit refunds part of the tax through Treasury.

## What is the Michigan homestead property tax credit?

<!-- .al-answer -->
The homestead property tax credit is a refundable Michigan income tax credit, filed on Form MI-1040CR, for residents whose property taxes, or the part of rent treated as property tax, are high compared with their total household resources. Because it is refundable, you can receive it even when you owe no Michigan income tax.

[Michigan Treasury's credit page](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/credits-exemptions/homestead-property-tax-credit) describes it as a credit that helps qualified homeowners and renters pay some of the property taxes they were billed. The credit is paid back to you through your state return. It does not change the tax bill itself. Most people file MI-1040CR. Some veterans and blind homeowners file MI-1040CR-2, covered below.

## Who qualifies for the homestead property tax credit?

<!-- .al-answer -->
You can claim the credit if your homestead is in Michigan, you lived in Michigan for at least six months of the year, and your total household resources are at or under Treasury's limit. Owners also need a taxable value at or under Treasury's cap. Seniors, people with disabilities and some veterans have special calculations.

Treasury adds a few conditions. You must be a person, not a trust or a company. You must own and live in the home, or rent it under a lease, and you can have only one homestead at a time. A vacation home or rental property does not count.

**Homestead property tax credit limits, tax year 2025 (claims filed in 2026)**

| Rule | Tax year 2025 figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Total household resources limit | $71,500 | Treasury, 2025 credit information |
| Phase-out starts (credit cut 10% per $1,000 above it) | $62,500 | Treasury, 2025 credit information |
| Taxable value cap (owners only) | $165,400 | Treasury, 2025 credit information |
| Maximum credit | $1,900 | Treasury, 2025 credit information |
| Share of rent treated as property tax (most renters) | 23% | 2025 MI-1040CR instructions |
| Share of household resources not refunded (most claimants) | 3.2% | 2025 MI-1040CR instructions |
| Michigan residency | At least 6 months of the year | Treasury credit page |

[VERIFY: tax year 2026 figures.]

**Total household resources** are the taxable and nontaxable income of you and your spouse, or of a single person keeping a household. That means adjusted gross income plus amounts such as Social Security, with some losses left out. Treasury's [total household resources page](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/total-household-resources) lists what counts.

**Taxable value** is printed on your summer and winter tax statements. Treasury's [homeowner guidance](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/credits-exemptions/hptc/homeowners) warns not to use the state equalized value (SEV), a different number on the same bill. If you are buying, [estimate your Michigan property tax after you buy](/michigan-property-tax-calculator/), because your taxable value will not be the seller's.

Three groups get special rules, per the 2025 instructions:

- **Age 65 or older**, or the unremarried surviving spouse of someone who was: a larger share of the tax, from 60% up to 100% depending on income.
- **People who are deaf or blind, have hemiplegia, paraplegia or quadriplegia, or are totally and permanently disabled:** 100% of the tax above the threshold, up to the maximum. Someone who was 66 by February 28, 2025 cannot claim as totally and permanently disabled. Related: buying a home on SSI or disability income [LINK ONCE LIVE: /learn/buying-a-home-on-ssi-or-disability-income/].
- **Veterans and blind owners:** [Treasury's forms page](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/credits-exemptions/hptc/forms) points blind homeowners, veterans with a service-connected disability and surviving spouses of veterans who died in service to MI-1040CR-2. Complete both forms and file the one that gives the larger credit. Michigan also has a separate [Disabled Veterans Exemption](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/exemptions/homeowner/disabled-veteran-exemption) from property tax. See Michigan benefits for disabled veterans [LINK ONCE LIVE: /learn/va-benefits-disabled-veterans-michigan/].

## How much is the Michigan homestead property tax credit?

<!-- .al-answer -->
The credit is a share of the property tax that exceeds 3.2% of your total household resources, up to $1,900 for tax year 2025. Most claimants get 60% of that excess. Claimants age 65 or older get 60% to 100% depending on income, and people with disabilities get 100%. Treasury updates the figures each tax year.

Treasury's method on the 2025 MI-1040CR runs in five steps:

1. Start with the property taxes levied on your homestead for the year, plus 23% of any rent you paid.
2. Subtract 3.2% of your total household resources. Claimants age 65 or older and people with disabilities whose resources are $6,000 or less subtract a smaller share, from 0% to 3%.
3. Multiply what is left by 60% for most claimants, by Treasury's Table A percentage for claimants age 65 or older, or by 100% for people with disabilities.
4. Cap the result at $1,900.
5. If total household resources are over $62,500, cut the credit by 10% for every $1,000, or part of $1,000, above that. Above $71,500, no credit is allowed.

**Hypothetical example, not an offer. Actual rates, costs and payments vary.** This is tax and credit arithmetic only, with Treasury's tax year 2025 figures. "Property taxes levied" means the total taxes billed on the homestead for the year. A household has $40,000 in total household resources and $3,600 in property taxes levied on a home with a taxable value under $165,400.

| Step | Most claimants | Claimant with a disability |
|---|---|---|
| Property taxes levied | $3,600 | $3,600 |
| Less 3.2% of $40,000 | −$1,280 | −$1,280 |
| Tax above the threshold | $2,320 | $2,320 |
| Share allowed | 60% = $1,392 | 100% = $2,320 |
| After the $1,900 cap | $1,392 | $1,900 |
| Phase-out (resources under $62,500) | None | None |
| Credit | $1,392 | $1,900 |

A claimant age 65 or older with the same $40,000 falls in Table A's 60% band, so the credit is also $1,392. Treasury's form and tax software do this arithmetic for you.

## How is the homestead property tax credit different from the homestead exemption?

<!-- .al-answer -->
The Principal Residence Exemption lowers your tax bill by exempting the home you own and live in from up to 18 mills of school operating tax. You file Form 2368 with your assessor by June 1 or November 1. The homestead property tax credit is claimed later, on your state income tax return, and refunds part of the tax.

| | Principal Residence Exemption (PRE) | Homestead property tax credit (MI-1040CR) |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Exempts your home from school operating millage, up to 18 mills, so the bill is lower | Refunds part of the property tax billed, or of rent, when it is high for your income |
| Who can get it | Owners who live in the home as their principal residence | Owners and renters who meet the residency, income and taxable value limits |
| Where you file | Form 2368, with your city or township assessor | Form MI-1040CR, with Michigan Treasury |
| When | By June 1 for the summer levy, or by November 1 for the winter levy | With your MI-1040 (due April 15, 2026 for tax year 2025), or alone |
| How often | Once; it stays on while you qualify | Every year |
| Income test | None | Total household resources of $71,500 or less (tax year 2025) |
| Renters | Not eligible | Eligible, 23% of rent in most cases |
| If you missed it | The assessor can grant it for the current year and the three before | File up to four years after the original due date |

Treasury's [Principal Residence Exemption page](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/principal) says the exemption "is distinct from the Michigan Homestead Property Tax Credit, which applies to income tax returns." Its [PRE claim rules](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/principal/principal-residence-exemption-pre-guidelines-and-faqs/claim-requirement) set the June 1 and November 1 deadlines. An owner can have both. The credit instructions do not count school operating tax levied on any part of a home that is not your homestead, and they point owners billed at the higher non-homestead level to Form 2368. For PRE filing steps and denials, see [Michigan's Principal Residence Exemption](/learn/homestead-exemption-benefits-and-filing-guide/).

Because the credit comes back through Treasury, it does not change what your [mortgage escrow accounts](/learn/mortgage-escrow-accounts/) pay toward the bill. A PRE lowers the bill itself.

## How do you file the homestead property tax credit?

<!-- .al-answer -->
File Form MI-1040CR with your Michigan MI-1040 return, or by itself if you are not required to file a return. You need your property tax statements, or rent paid and your landlord's details, plus your total household resources. Treasury accepts claims for up to four years after the original due date. For tax year 2025, that is April 15, 2030.

1. **Gather your records.** Owners need the summer and winter tax statements, which show taxable value and taxes levied. Renters need the rent paid and the landlord's name and address. Your city, township or village assessor can supply a missing statement.
2. **Add up total household resources** for you and your spouse, including nontaxable income such as Social Security.
3. **Choose the form.** Use MI-1040CR. If you qualify for MI-1040CR-2, complete both and file the one that gives the larger credit.
4. **File it.** E-file, or mail it to the Michigan Department of Treasury, Lansing, MI 48956, with your MI-1040 or on its own. Treasury's [how to file page](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/credits-exemptions/hptc/how-to-file) lists both options. [VERIFY: tax year 2026 claim due date in Treasury's 2026 instructions.]

One federal point: Treasury's [2025 MI-1040 instruction book](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/-/media/Project/Websites/taxes/Forms/IIT/TY2025/MI-1040-Book.pdf) repeats an IRS reminder that a credit received may be taxable on your federal return if you itemized property taxes the year before. A tax professional can say whether that applies. Federal rules are covered in [new tax laws and homeownership](/learn/impact-of-new-tax-laws-on-homeownership/).

## Can renters claim the Michigan property tax credit?

<!-- .al-answer -->
Yes. Renters with a lease or rental agreement treat 23% of the rent paid on their Michigan home as property tax in most cases, and claim the credit on the same MI-1040CR. The tax year 2025 household resources limit of $71,500 still applies, but the taxable value cap does not apply to renters.

Treasury's [renter guidance](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/credits-exemptions/hptc/renters) and the 2025 instructions list the exceptions:

- **Service-fee housing** (payment in lieu of taxes): 10% of rent, not 23%.
- **Subsidized housing:** count only the rent you pay, not the government's share.
- **Mobile home parks:** subtract the $3 a month specific tax from rent and claim it as property tax.
- **Tax-exempt housing with no service fee**, including university or college housing, does not qualify.
- **Land contract buyers** are owners, not renters, so they claim the property taxes levied.
- **Renters age 65 or older** may get Treasury's alternate credit: rent paid minus 40% of total household resources, if that is larger than the regular credit, up to $1,900.

## Do seniors get a property tax break in Michigan?

<!-- .al-answer -->
There is no automatic reduction at 65. Claimants age 65 or older may get a larger homestead property tax credit, may be able to delay paying property taxes through the local or county treasurer, and lower-income owners can apply each year to the local assessor or board of review for a poverty exemption.

Treasury's list of [homeowner exemption programs](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/exemptions/homeowner) has no exemption based on age. Here is what does exist:

- **A larger credit.** For tax year 2025, Treasury's Table A allows 100% of the tax above the threshold when total household resources are $21,000 or less. The share steps down 4 points per $1,000, to 60% above $30,000. Treasury counts you as 65 on the day before your 65th birthday.
- **Delayed payment.** The 2025 credit instructions say seniors, people with disabilities, veterans, surviving spouses of veterans and farmers may be able to delay paying property taxes, and send you to your local or county treasurer. [VERIFY: summer tax deferment income limit and filing deadline under MCL 211.51; legislature.mi.gov was not reachable on November 16, 2026.]
- **Poverty exemption.** Treasury's [poverty exemption page](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/exemptions/homeowner/poverty-exemption) explains that under MCL 211.7u an owner who cannot pay because of poverty can apply each year for a full or partial exemption. Income guidelines come from your local assessor.

If you are planning a purchase or refinance later in life, see getting a mortgage at 60, 70 or 80 [LINK ONCE LIVE: /learn/mortgage-for-seniors/].

## What happens to the credit in the year after you buy a Michigan home?

<!-- .al-answer -->
The year after a home sells, its taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid. Your property tax usually rises. For an eligible household, a higher tax can mean a larger credit, up to the maximum, unless the new taxable value passes Treasury's cap.

[Align Lending's 2026 uncapping study](/research/michigan-property-tax-uncapping-study/) found that in 2026 the typical Michigan home's taxable value jumped 50.5% the year after it sold. For the mechanics, see [how Michigan taxable value resets after a sale](/learn/michigan-taxable-value/).

**The cap matters more after a purchase.** The reset brings taxable value up to about half of the assessor's market value. So a home the assessor values at more than about $330,800 would be over the tax year 2025 cap of $165,400 once it resets, and would not qualify.

**In the year you buy, prorate.** Treasury uses the taxes levied on each Michigan homestead for the year, times the share of the year you lived there, "no matter when you pay them." Use the tax statements, not the proration lines on your closing statement. If you rented earlier in the year, add 23% of that rent.

**File your own PRE.** The seller's exemption does not carry over. If you think the assessor's market value is too high, see [how property tax assessments work and how to appeal](/learn/property-tax-assessments-how-they-work/).

## Questions about the Michigan homestead property tax credit

### Who qualifies for the homestead property tax credit in Michigan?

Michigan residents of at least six months who own or rent their Michigan homestead can qualify if total household resources are within Treasury's limit, $71,500 for tax year 2025. Owners also need a taxable value of $165,400 or less. A trust or company cannot claim it, and a vacation home or rental property does not count.

### What is the income limit for the Michigan property tax credit?

For tax year 2025, total household resources must be $71,500 or less. The credit starts shrinking above $62,500, by 10% for each $1,000 or part of $1,000, and reaches zero above $71,500. Total household resources include taxable and nontaxable income, such as Social Security. Treasury adjusts the limits each year.

### How is the Michigan homestead property tax credit calculated?

Treasury subtracts 3.2% of your total household resources from your property tax, or from 23% of rent, then allows 60% of the rest for most claimants, up to 100% for lower-income claimants age 65 or older, and 100% for people with disabilities. For tax year 2025 the credit cannot exceed $1,900, and the phase-out applies above $62,500.

### Do property taxes go down when you turn 65 in Michigan?

No. Turning 65 does not by itself lower a Michigan property tax bill. What changes is the help you can claim: a larger homestead property tax credit for lower-income claimants, a possible delay in paying taxes through your local or county treasurer, and, for owners who cannot pay because of poverty, a yearly poverty exemption application.

### Can I claim the credit if I do not file a Michigan income tax return?

Yes. Treasury's instructions say that if you are not required to file an MI-1040, you can file the MI-1040CR by itself once you know your total household resources and the property taxes levied for the year. The credit is refundable, so it is paid to you even if you owe no Michigan income tax.

### Can I claim the homestead property tax credit for past years?

Yes, within limits. Treasury allows a claim up to four years from the date the original return was due. For tax year 2025, the last day to claim the credit is April 15, 2030. Use the MI-1040CR and the limits for the year you are claiming, not the current year's.

### Is the homestead property tax credit the same as the homestead exemption?

No. The homestead exemption, officially the Principal Residence Exemption, lowers your bill by exempting your home from up to 18 mills of school operating tax, and you file Form 2368 with your assessor. The credit is claimed on your state income tax return and refunds part of the tax. Owners can have both.
