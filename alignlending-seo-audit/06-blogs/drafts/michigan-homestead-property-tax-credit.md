# Michigan homestead property tax credit: who qualifies, how much and how to file

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 17, 2026

<div class="al-answer">

Michigan homeowners and renters can get part of their property tax back through the homestead property tax credit, a state income tax credit claimed on Form MI-1040CR. It applies when the tax, or the part of rent treated as tax, is high for your household income. Taxable value limits apply to owners. Seniors and people with disabilities get special rules.

</div>

*Figures below are Treasury's tax year 2025 limits, the latest published when this guide was updated. Treasury adjusts them each year. This is not tax advice: confirm figures with Michigan Treasury's current MI-1040CR instructions or a tax professional.* [VERIFY: Treasury had not posted tax year 2026 figures on November 17, 2026. Before publishing, swap in the tax year 2026 figures if posted; see the front matter for every place they appear.]

<div class="al-takeaways">

**Key takeaways**

- **Owners and renters can claim it.** Most renters count 23% of rent as property tax.
- **Tax year 2025 limits:** $71,500 in household resources, $165,400 in taxable value for owners, and a $1,900 maximum.
- **File with or without a state return,** up to four years after the due date.
- **It is not the homestead exemption.** The PRE lowers the bill; the credit refunds part of it.

</div>

## What is the Michigan homestead property tax credit?

The homestead property tax credit is a refundable [Michigan income tax credit](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/credits-exemptions/homestead-property-tax-credit), filed on Form MI-1040CR, for residents whose property tax, or rent treated as property tax, is high compared with their total household resources. Because it is refundable, you can get it even if you owe no Michigan income tax.

## Who qualifies for the homestead property tax credit?

You qualify if your homestead is in Michigan, you were a Michigan resident at least six months of the year, and your total household resources are within Treasury's limit. Owners also need a taxable value within Treasury's cap. Seniors, people with disabilities and some veterans have special calculations.

**Tax year 2025 limits,** from Treasury's [2025 credit information](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/credits-exemptions/hptc/tax-year-credit-information/2025) and [2025 MI-1040CR instructions](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/-/media/Project/Websites/taxes/Forms/IIT/TY2025/MI-1040CR-Instr.pdf).

| Rule | Tax year 2025 |
|---|---|
| Total household resources limit | $71,500 |
| Phase-out starts | $62,500 |
| Taxable value cap (owners only) | $165,400 |
| Maximum credit | $1,900 |
| Rent treated as property tax (most renters) | 23% |

**Total household resources** are all taxable and nontaxable income of you and your spouse, including Social Security. Use your **taxable value**, not the state equalized value (SEV), per Treasury's [homeowner guidance](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/credits-exemptions/hptc/homeowners). If you are buying, [estimate your Michigan property tax after you buy](/michigan-property-tax-calculator/); your taxable value will not be the seller's.

- **Age 65 or older** (or the unremarried surviving spouse of someone who was): 60% to 100% of the excess tax, by income.
- **People who are deaf, blind, hemiplegic, paraplegic, quadriplegic or totally and permanently disabled:** 100%.
- **Blind owners, active military, eligible veterans and veterans' surviving spouses** can also file [MI-1040CR-2](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/credits-exemptions/hptc/forms) and use whichever form gives the larger credit. See [property tax benefits for disabled veterans in Michigan](/learn/va-benefits-disabled-veterans-michigan/).

## How much is the Michigan homestead property tax credit?

The credit is a share of the property tax above 3.2% of your total household resources, up to $1,900 for tax year 2025. Most claimants get 60% of that excess, claimants 65 or older get 60% to 100% by income, and people with disabilities get 100%.

1. Add the year's property taxes levied on your homestead, or 23% of rent paid.
2. Subtract 3.2% of total household resources (less for some claimants with $6,000 or under).
3. Multiply by 60%, by Table A's 60% to 100% if 65 or older, or by 100% with a disability.
4. Cap it at $1,900.
5. Above $62,500, cut it 10% per $1,000 or part; above $71,500, it is zero.

**Hypothetical example, not an offer. Actual rates, costs and payments vary.** Tax arithmetic, tax year 2025: $40,000 in household resources, $3,600 in taxes levied.

| Step | Most claimants | Claimant with a disability |
|---|---|---|
| Taxes levied | $3,600 | $3,600 |
| Less 3.2% of $40,000 | −$1,280 | −$1,280 |
| Share allowed | 60% of $2,320 = $1,392 | 100% of $2,320 = $2,320 |
| Credit ($1,900 cap) | $1,392 | $1,900 |

## How is the homestead property tax credit different from the homestead exemption?

The Principal Residence Exemption lowers your bill by exempting the home you own and live in from up to 18 mills of school operating tax. You file Form 2368 with your assessor by June 1 or November 1. The credit is claimed later, on your state income tax return, and refunds part of the tax.

| | Principal Residence Exemption (PRE) | Homestead property tax credit |
|---|---|---|
| Effect | Lowers the bill | Refunds part of the tax or rent |
| Who | Owners in their principal residence | Owners and renters within the limits |
| File with | Your assessor, Form 2368 | Michigan Treasury, Form MI-1040CR |
| When | June 1 or November 1; stays on while you qualify | Every year, with your MI-1040 or alone |
| Income test | None | $71,500 limit (tax year 2025) |
| If you missed it | Assessor can grant this year and three prior | Up to four years after the due date |

Treasury's [PRE page](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/principal) calls the exemption "distinct from" the credit, and its [claim rules](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/principal/principal-residence-exemption-pre-guidelines-and-faqs/claim-requirement) set both deadlines. Owners can have both; see [Michigan's Principal Residence Exemption](/learn/homestead-exemption-benefits-and-filing-guide/). Because Treasury pays the credit to you, it does not change what [mortgage escrow accounts](/learn/mortgage-escrow-accounts/) pay toward the bill.

## How do you file the homestead property tax credit?

File Form MI-1040CR with your MI-1040 return, or by itself if you are not required to file one. You need your tax statements, or rent paid and landlord details, plus your total household resources. Treasury accepts claims up to four years after the original due date: April 15, 2030, for tax year 2025.

1. **Gather** summer and winter tax statements, or rent paid and landlord details.
2. **Total** household resources, including Social Security.
3. **Pick the form:** MI-1040CR, plus MI-1040CR-2 if you qualify.
4. **File** online or by mail, with your MI-1040 (due April 15) or alone.

Treasury's [2025 MI-1040 book](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/-/media/Project/Websites/taxes/Forms/IIT/TY2025/MI-1040-Book.pdf) lists the credit as refundable and notes it may be federally taxable if you itemized property taxes; see [new tax laws and homeownership](/learn/impact-of-new-tax-laws-on-homeownership/).

## Can renters claim the Michigan property tax credit?

Yes. Renters with a lease or rental agreement treat 23% of the rent paid on their Michigan home as property tax in most cases and claim on the same MI-1040CR. The tax year 2025 household resources limit of $71,500 applies, but the taxable value cap does not.

Treasury's [renter guidance](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/credits-exemptions/hptc/renters) lists exceptions, such as 10% of rent in service-fee housing, only the rent you pay in subsidized housing, and an alternate credit for renters 65 or older.

## Do seniors get a property tax break in Michigan?

There is no automatic reduction at 65. Claimants 65 or older may get a larger homestead property tax credit, homeowners 62 or older with household income of $40,000 or less can delay the summer tax, and lower-income owners of any age can apply each year for a poverty exemption.

Treasury's [homeowner exemption programs](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/exemptions/homeowner) include none based on age.

- **A larger credit:** 100% of the excess tax at $21,000 or less in resources, down to 60% above $30,000 (tax year 2025).
- **Summer tax deferment:** homeowners 62 or older, or with certain disabilities or veteran status, with prior-year household income of $40,000 or less can ask the local treasurer, generally by September 15, to defer the summer tax to the next February 15 without penalty or interest ([MCL 211.51](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-51)).
- **Poverty exemption:** under MCL 211.7u, owners [apply each year](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/exemptions/homeowner/poverty-exemption) under local income guidelines.

Buying or borrowing later in life? See [getting a mortgage at 60, 70 or 80](/learn/mortgage-for-seniors/).

## What happens to the credit in the year after you buy a Michigan home?

The year after a home sells, its taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid. Your property tax usually rises. For an eligible household, a higher tax can mean a larger credit, up to the maximum, unless the new taxable value passes Treasury's cap.

[Align Lending's 2026 uncapping study](/research/michigan-property-tax-uncapping-study/) found a typical 50.5% jump in taxable value the year after a sale in 2026. See [how Michigan taxable value resets after a sale](/learn/michigan-taxable-value/).

**Watch the cap.** After the reset, a home the assessor values above about $330,800 passes the tax year 2025 cap of $165,400.

**Prorate the purchase year.** Treasury uses the taxes levied, "no matter when you pay them," times the share of the year you lived there, not your closing-statement prorations.

**File your own PRE** (Form 2368) by June 1 or November 1. To challenge the value, see [how property tax assessments work and how to appeal](/learn/property-tax-assessments-how-they-work/).

## Questions about the Michigan homestead property tax credit

### Who qualifies for the homestead property tax credit in Michigan?

Michigan residents of at least six months who own or rent their homestead qualify if total household resources are $71,500 or less for tax year 2025. Owners also need a taxable value of $165,400 or less. Vacation homes and rentals do not count.

### What is the income limit for the Michigan property tax credit?

For tax year 2025, total household resources must be $71,500 or less, and the credit shrinks 10% for each $1,000, or part of $1,000, above $62,500. Resources include taxable and nontaxable income, such as Social Security. Treasury adjusts the limits each year.

### How is the Michigan homestead property tax credit calculated?

Treasury subtracts 3.2% of total household resources from your property tax, or 23% of rent, then allows 60% of the rest for most claimants, up to 100% for lower-income claimants 65 or older, and 100% with a disability, capped at $1,900 for tax year 2025.

### Do property taxes go down when you turn 65 in Michigan?

No. Turning 65 does not by itself lower a Michigan property tax bill. It changes the help you can claim: a larger credit for lower-income claimants, a summer tax deferment available from 62 with household income of $40,000 or less and, at any age, a yearly poverty exemption application.

### Can I claim the credit if I do not file a Michigan income tax return?

Yes. Treasury's instructions say that if you are not required to file an MI-1040, you can file the MI-1040CR by itself once you know your household resources and taxes levied. Because the credit is refundable, it is paid even if you owe no tax.

### Can I claim the homestead property tax credit for past years?

Yes, within limits. Treasury allows a claim up to four years from the date the original return was due. For tax year 2025, the last day is April 15, 2030. Use the form and limits for the year you claim.

### Is the homestead property tax credit the same as the homestead exemption?

No. The homestead exemption, officially the Principal Residence Exemption, exempts your home from up to 18 mills of school operating tax through your assessor, on Form 2368. The credit is claimed on your state income tax return and refunds part of the tax.
