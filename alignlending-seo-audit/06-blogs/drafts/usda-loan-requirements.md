# USDA loan requirements: credit, debt-to-income, property and occupancy rules

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 16, 2026

<div class="al-answer">

For Michigan buyers using a USDA Guaranteed loan, the address and household income are only the first two tests. The lender also checks credit (GUS usually wants 640 for an automated approval), debt ratios of 29% for housing and 41% in total, that you will live in the home, and that the house meets USDA's property standards.

</div>

**Key takeaways**

<div class="al-takeaways">

- **Address and income come first.** The home must sit in a USDA-eligible area, and the income of every adult who will live there counts toward the county limit.
- **Credit:** USDA sets no minimum score in its handbook, but its automated system generally needs 640 or higher for an automated approval.
- **Debt:** the standard ratios are 29% of gross monthly income for housing and 41% for all debts. In Michigan, the tax inside that 29% is the new owner's tax, after taxable value resets.
- **Occupancy:** the home must be your primary residence, everyone on the loan must live there, and non-occupant co-signers are not allowed.
- **Property and amount:** the house must be mainly residential, sound and sanitary, and Guaranteed loans have no set maximum loan amount; income, debt ratios and the appraisal cap it.

</div>

This guide covers the rules a lender checks after the address. For the program itself, start with [how USDA loans work](/learn/usda-loan-basics/).

## What are the requirements for a USDA loan?

<p class="al-answer">A USDA Guaranteed loan has six tests: an eligible address, household income at or under the county limit, acceptable credit, debt ratios of 29% and 41%, the home as your primary residence with no other adequate home nearby, and a property that meets USDA's standards. You can check the address and income yourself before you talk to a lender.</p>

Each rule is set in a chapter of [HB-1-3555](https://www.rd.usda.gov/resources/directives/handbooks), the technical handbook that lenders follow for USDA's Section 502 Guaranteed loan. The table puts them side by side. For how the program fits a purchase here, see [USDA Rural Development loans in Michigan](/product-loans/usda-loans/).

| Requirement | USDA rule | Where it is set | How to check |
|---|---|---|---|

| Household income | Adjusted income of everyone in the household at or under the county's moderate-income limit | HB-1-3555 [Chapter 9](https://www.rd.usda.gov/files/3555-1chapter09.pdf) and [Appendix 5](https://www.rd.usda.gov/sites/default/files/rd-grhlimitmap.pdf) | The income step of Align's eligibility check |

## What credit score do you need for a USDA loan?

<p class="al-answer">USDA does not set a minimum credit score in its handbook. Its Guaranteed Underwriting System (GUS) generally needs a score of 640 or higher for an automated approval. Below that, or with thin credit, a lender can underwrite the file manually with stricter documentation, and many lenders add their own minimum score.</p> [VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Ch. 10, sections 10.3 to 10.5 and Attachment 10-A, for the 640 standard and the GUS Refer below it]

The regulation's standard is an acceptable credit history, one that shows a reasonable ability and willingness to repay debts ([7 CFR 3555.151](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/section-3555.151)). The score is how GUS sorts files quickly. A score of 640 or higher generally meets the standard when nothing else on the report raises a concern, but the score alone does not prove acceptable credit.

Two cases surprise people. A file with no score at all can still qualify on a nontraditional credit history, such as a record of rent and utility payments [VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Ch. 10, nontraditional credit]. And a strong score does not cancel items that make credit unacceptable, such as a delinquent federal debt [VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Ch. 10 list of unacceptable credit indicators]. Lenders also add their own minimums, called overlays, so two lenders can answer the same file differently. Compare the programs in [what credit score you need, by loan type](/learn/whats-good-credit-score-to-buy-house/).

### Can I get a USDA loan with a 550 credit score?

It is very unlikely with most lenders. Nothing in the handbook rules out a 550 score by name, but a score that low sends the file to manual underwriting, where the lender has to document why the credit is acceptable, and lenders often set their own floor well above 550. The practical step is to find out what is holding the score down and work on those items before you apply.

## What is the maximum debt-to-income ratio for a USDA loan?

<p class="al-answer">USDA's standard ratios are 29% of gross monthly income for the housing payment, which counts principal, interest, property taxes, insurance, the USDA annual fee and any HOA dues, and 41% for that payment plus all other monthly debts. GUS can accept higher ratios when strong compensating factors support the rest of the file.</p> [VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Ch. 11, section 11.2 and the housing expense list]

The ratios use repayment income, the income of the people on the loan, not the whole household's. A debt-to-income ratio (DTI) is your monthly debt payments divided by your gross monthly income; see [how debt-to-income ratio is calculated](/learn/debt-to-income-ratio-dti/) for what counts as a debt.

On a manually underwritten file, a lender can ask USDA to accept ratios above 29% and 41% when documented compensating factors support it. The regulation lists a credit score of 680 or higher first among them ([7 CFR 3555.151](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/section-3555.151)). USDA's revision aid for Chapter 11 caps those manual waivers at 32% and 44% [VERIFY: current HB-1-3555 Ch. 11 waiver maximums]. With a GUS Accept, the system's approval covers ratios above the standard without a separate waiver [VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Ch. 11]. USDA republishes its upfront and annual fee figures each fiscal year; [USDA's current fees](/learn/recent-changes-to-usda-loan-programs/) tracks them.

### How do Michigan property taxes affect your USDA ratio?

In Michigan, the tax inside that 29% should be the new owner's tax. The year after a home sells, its taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid ([MCL 211.27a](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-27a)). [Align's 2026 Uncapping Study](/research/michigan-property-tax-uncapping-study/) puts the typical jump in a Michigan home's taxable value the year after it sells at about 50.5%.

A household's borrowers earn $78,000 a year, or $6,500 a month. At 29%, the most the full housing payment can be under the standard ratio is about $1,885 a month. Take a $240,000 home where the assessor's market value is close to the price, so taxable value resets to about $120,000. At 36.0 mills, the statewide median homestead rate (2025 rates, per Align's study), the tax comes to about $4,320 a year, or about $360 a month. That uses about 19% of the $1,885 before principal, interest, insurance or the annual fee. These figures are arithmetic on income and tax only, not a loan payment.

The homestead rate assumes you file the Principal Residence Exemption, by June 1 for the summer bill or November 1 for the winter bill. Enter the address to [estimate your Michigan property tax after you buy](/michigan-property-tax-calculator/).

## How is household income counted for a USDA loan?

<p class="al-answer">USDA counts the income of every adult who will live in the home, not just the borrowers. It then subtracts allowances for each child under 18, for a household member 62 or older, and for some childcare and medical costs, and compares the result, called adjusted income, with the county limit.</p> [VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Ch. 9, annual and adjusted income, and any exclusions for adult full-time students]

This is an eligibility test, separate from the debt ratios. A working adult in the home can push the household over the limit without adding a dollar of repayment income. USDA updates the limits each fiscal year, so [check your county's current income limit](/usda-eligibility-check/) rather than relying on a figure from last year.

### Can my partner live with me if they are not on the loan?

Yes. A partner, adult child or roommate can live in the home without being on the loan. Their income still counts toward the household limit, but it cannot help you qualify for the payment, because the debt ratios use only the borrowers' income.

## Is there a maximum USDA loan amount?

<p class="al-answer">USDA Guaranteed loans have no set maximum loan amount. Your repayment income, the 29% and 41% ratios and the appraised value decide how much you can borrow, and the loan generally cannot exceed the appraised value plus the financed guarantee fee. USDA's Direct loans, made by Rural Development itself, do have area loan limits.</p> [VERIFY: 7 CFR 3555.103 maximum loan amount; Direct area loan limits on the USDA Direct program page]

Most buyers who search for a USDA loan limit run into the household income limit first, not a cap on the loan. Direct loans have their own income, property and loan-amount rules, and [USDA's Direct program page](https://www.rd.usda.gov/programs-services/single-family-housing-programs/single-family-housing-direct-home-loans) explains them. For the comparison, see [USDA Direct vs. Guaranteed loans](/learn/usda-loan-basics/).

## Who can be on a USDA loan, and can you use a co-signer?

<p class="al-answer">Everyone on a USDA Guaranteed loan must live in the home. Non-occupant co-signers and co-borrowers, such as a parent who keeps their own house, are not allowed. A co-borrower who will live there can be added if they meet the credit, income and citizenship rules, and their income and debts then count in the ratios.</p> [VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Ch. 8 and Ch. 9 on non-occupant co-borrowers; lender overlays cite section 9.8]

Adding an occupying co-borrower can help the ratios, but their monthly debts come with them. For what signing for someone else's loan means, see [co-signing a mortgage, risks and benefits](/learn/co-signing-mortgage-risks-and-benefits/).

## Can you have two USDA loans or use the program twice?

<p class="al-answer">Generally one at a time. The home must be your primary residence, and when the loan closes you cannot own another home in the area that is adequate for your household. You can use USDA again after you sell, and limited exceptions exist, such as moving beyond a reasonable commute for a new job.</p> [VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Ch. 8, section 8.2, on current homeowners and the exceptions]

The other exceptions in Chapter 8 cover a current home that is not structurally sound or no longer fits the household [VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Ch. 8]. Replacing the loan on the home you already have is a different path; see [refinancing a USDA loan](/learn/what-is-usda-streamline-refinance/).

### Can I use a USDA loan for a rental property?

No. USDA Guaranteed loans finance a primary residence only, so a rental, a second home or an investment property does not qualify. You must agree to live in the home yourself.

## What property and house rules does a USDA loan have?

<p class="al-answer">The home must be mainly residential, structurally sound, sanitary and free of health and safety hazards, on a site typical in size for the area, with no income-producing use. Condos must be in a project approved or accepted by HUD/FHA, VA, Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, and wells and septic systems must meet state and local health rules.</p> [VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Ch. 12, including 12.11(A) on condominiums]

Outbuildings such as storage sheds and non-commercial workshops are fine. Barns, silos, commercial greenhouses or livestock buildings used mainly for farming or a business make a property ineligible. New manufactured homes can qualify under their own rules, and existing ones generally cannot [VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Ch. 12, manufactured housing].

### What will fail a USDA inspection?

USDA does not send its own inspector on a Guaranteed loan for an existing home. The appraiser checks the house against the property standards, and the lender requires repairs before closing when something fails [VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Ch. 12 and the HUD minimum property standards it applies]. Common problems in rural Michigan homes: water that fails a test or a well that does not produce enough, a failing septic system, a roof near the end of its life, no working heat, peeling paint in a home built before 1978, and structural damage. Your county or district health department sets the well and septic rules for the parcel. See [buying a home with a septic system](/learn/buying-home-with-septic-system/) and [well water and home value](/learn/how-well-water-impacts-homes-value/).

### Can you buy land or a hobby farm with a USDA loan?

Not land by itself. A USDA Guaranteed loan finances a home and the lot it sits on, and the site must be typical in size for the area. A house on acreage can qualify, but vacant land and property used mainly for farming or a business cannot. A garden that brings in a little income does not disqualify a home [VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Ch. 12, site and income-producing rules]. For the rest of the rural checklist, see [buying a home in a rural area](/learn/tips-for-buying-home-in-rural-area/).

## What disqualifies you from a USDA loan?

<p class="al-answer">Most often, the address or the household income. After those, the common reasons are credit the lender will not accept, debt ratios that GUS will not approve and no waiver supports, already owning an adequate home nearby, a co-signer who will not live there, or a property with an income-producing use or major repairs.</p>

An unresolved delinquent federal debt can also stop a file until it is cleared [VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Ch. 8 and Ch. 10 on delinquent federal debt and the CAIVRS check]. Most of these can be checked before you make an offer, which is the point of running the address, the income and the tax line first.

## Questions about USDA loan requirements

### What credit score do I need for a USDA home loan?

USDA itself sets no minimum score. Its Guaranteed Underwriting System generally needs 640 or higher for an automated approval, and files below that are usually underwritten manually with more documentation. Many lenders add their own minimum, so the score a lender will accept can be higher than USDA's standard.

### What is the maximum DTI for a USDA loan?

The standard limits are 29% of gross monthly income for the housing payment and 41% for all monthly debts. GUS can approve higher ratios on a strong file, and a manually underwritten file can go higher only with documented compensating factors, such as a credit score of 680 or higher.

### Can you have a co-signer on a USDA loan?

Not one who lives elsewhere. USDA Guaranteed loans do not allow non-occupant co-signers or co-borrowers. A co-borrower who will live in the home can be added if they meet the credit and income rules, and their income and debts then count in the ratios.

### Can you have two USDA loans?

Usually not at the same time. A USDA loan must be for your primary residence, and you generally cannot own another adequate home in the area when the new loan closes. Limited exceptions exist, such as relocating beyond a reasonable commute, and you can use the program again after selling.

### Is there a limit on how much you can borrow with a USDA loan?

Not a set dollar limit on Guaranteed loans. How much you can borrow depends on your repayment income, the 29% and 41% debt ratios and the appraised value. USDA's Direct loans, which Rural Development makes itself, do have area loan limits.

### Why would a USDA loan get denied?

The most common reasons are an ineligible address or household income over the county limit. After those come credit the lender will not accept, debt ratios too high to approve, owning another adequate home nearby, a co-signer who will not live in the home, or a property that fails USDA's standards.

### Can I put a down payment on a USDA loan?

Yes. [USDA's Guaranteed program](https://www.rd.usda.gov/programs-services/single-family-housing-programs/single-family-housing-guaranteed-loan-program) does not require a down payment, but it allows one. A down payment lowers the amount you borrow, and you still pay closing costs and prepaid items whether or not you put money down.
