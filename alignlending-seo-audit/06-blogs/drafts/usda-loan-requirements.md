# USDA credit score and DTI rules: what lenders check in Michigan

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 17, 2026

<div class="al-answer">

For Michigan buyers using a USDA Guaranteed loan, the address and household income are only the first two tests. The lender also checks credit (USDA's automated system, GUS, generally looks for 640), debt ratios of 29% for housing and 41% in total, that you will live in the home, and that the house meets USDA's property standards.

</div>

<div class="al-takeaways">

**Key takeaways**

- **Address and income first:** a USDA-designated rural area, and the whole household's income under the county limit.
- **Credit:** USDA's regulation sets no minimum score, but GUS generally looks for 640 for an automated approval.
- **Debt:** 29% of gross monthly income for housing and 41% for all debts; in Michigan, housing includes the new owner's property tax.
- **Occupancy and property:** a primary residence everyone on the loan lives in, meeting USDA's property standards.
- **Loan size:** no dollar maximum on Guaranteed loans; the appraisal, your income and the ratios set it.

</div>

New to USDA loans? Start with [how USDA loans work](/learn/usda-loan-basics/).

## What does a USDA lender check before approving the loan?

A USDA Guaranteed loan has six tests: an eligible address, household income at or under the county limit, acceptable credit, debt ratios of 29% and 41%, the home as your primary residence, and a property that meets USDA's standards. You can check the first two yourself; the lender checks the rest.

The rules sit in 7 CFR part 3555 and in [HB-1-3555](https://www.rd.usda.gov/resources/directives/handbooks), the handbook lenders follow. See also [USDA Rural Development loans in Michigan](/product-loans/usda-loans/).

| Household income | Adjusted household income at or under the county's moderate income limit | [7 CFR 3555.152](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/section-3555.152); ch. 9 | The same page |

## What credit score do you need for a USDA loan?

USDA's regulation sets no minimum credit score; it asks for a credit history that shows you can and will repay. Its Guaranteed Underwriting System (GUS) generally looks for 640 or higher before it can return an automated approval. Below that, or with thin credit, the lender underwrites the file manually with more documentation.

[VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Chapter 10 (rd.usda.gov returned 403). Confirm that 640 is the GUS Accept threshold, and whether the current chapter says manually underwritten loans at 580 or below should not be approved. If it does, add that rule here, in the 550 answer and in FAQ 1.]

If you have no credit score, the regulation allows nontraditional credit, such as rent and utility records. Lenders also add their own minimums, called overlays. Compare programs in [what credit score you need, by loan type](/learn/whats-good-credit-score-to-buy-house/).

### Can I get a USDA loan with a 550 credit score?

It is very unlikely. A 550 score sits far below the 640 GUS looks for, so the file would need manual underwriting with documented reasons for the low score, and most lenders' minimums sit well above it. Fix what is holding the score down first.

## What is the maximum debt-to-income ratio for a USDA loan?

USDA's standard ratios are 29% of gross monthly income for the housing payment, which counts principal, interest, property taxes, insurance, the USDA annual fee and any HOA dues, and 41% for that payment plus all other monthly debts. GUS can accept higher ratios on a file with strong compensating factors.

Both ratios use repayment income, the income of the people on the loan. Your debt-to-income ratio (DTI) is monthly debts divided by gross monthly income; see [how debt-to-income ratio is calculated](/learn/debt-to-income-ratio-dti/). On a manual file, ratios above 29% and 41% need a waiver backed by compensating factors, led in the regulation by a credit score of 680 or higher, and USDA's handbook caps those waivers at 32% and 44%. A GUS Accept needs no separate waiver. For the fee amounts, see [how USDA's upfront and annual fees work](/product-loans/usda-loans/).

[VERIFY: HB-1-3555 Chapter 11, rev. 11-25-25 (rd.usda.gov returned 403). Confirm the manual-waiver caps of 32% and 44% with a 680 score; search results quoting the chapter show 32/44, but a November 2024 USDA policy deck mentions 34%.]

### How do Michigan property taxes affect your USDA ratio?

In Michigan, the tax inside that 29% should be the new owner's. The year after a home sells, its taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid ([MCL 211.27a](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-27a)). [Align's 2026 Uncapping Study](/research/michigan-property-tax-uncapping-study/) puts the typical jump in taxable value the year after a sale at about 50.5%.

## How is household income counted for a USDA loan?

USDA counts the income of everyone in the household, not just the borrowers, though children's earnings are left out. It then subtracts allowances for dependents, for a household whose head or spouse is 62 or older, and for some childcare, disability and medical costs. The result, adjusted income, is compared with the county limit.

A working adult in the home can push the household over the limit, while most earnings of a full-time student 18 or older, other than the head of household or spouse, are left out. Limits change each fiscal year, so [check your county's current income limit](/usda-eligibility-check/).

### Can my partner live with me if they are not on the loan?

Yes. A partner, adult child or roommate can live there without being on the loan. Their income counts toward the household limit but not toward the debt ratios, which use only the borrowers' income. If the ratios fall short, an eligible household member can join the application.

## Is there a maximum USDA loan amount?

USDA Guaranteed loans have no set maximum loan amount. Your repayment income, the 29% and 41% ratios and the appraised value decide how much you can borrow, and the loan generally cannot exceed the appraised value plus the financed guarantee fee. Direct loans, made by USDA itself, have area loan limits.

Direct-loan area limits are in [7 CFR 3550.63](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/section-3550.63); for the comparison, see [USDA Direct vs. Guaranteed loans](/learn/usda-loan-basics/).

## Who can be on a USDA loan, and can you use a co-signer?

Everyone on a USDA Guaranteed loan must live in the home, because USDA requires each applicant to occupy it as a principal residence. Non-occupant co-signers and co-borrowers, such as a parent who keeps their own house, are not allowed. A co-borrower who will live there can be added if they meet the credit, income and citizenship rules.

An occupying co-borrower can help the ratios, but their debts come too. See [co-signing a mortgage, risks and benefits](/learn/co-signing-mortgage-risks-and-benefits/).

## Can you have two USDA loans or use the program twice?

Generally one at a time. You cannot still be responsible for another USDA loan when the new one closes, and the new home must be your primary residence. You can keep a home you already own only if it no longer meets your household's needs and you qualify to carry both. After you sell, you can use the program again.

The regulation's full list also caps you at one other home. Replacing the loan on your current home is a different path: [refinancing a USDA loan](/learn/what-is-usda-streamline-refinance/).

### Can I use a USDA loan for a rental property?

No. USDA will not guarantee loans for investment properties or for temporary, short-term housing, so a rental or second home does not qualify.

## What property and house rules does a USDA loan have?

USDA's rule is that the home be "modest, decent, safe, and sanitary," on a site typical in size for the area, with no land or buildings used mainly to produce income. Condos must meet HUD, VA, Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac condo standards, and private wells and septic systems must be adequate, safe and up to code.

Manufactured homes on a permanent foundation can qualify, new or existing; an existing unit must meet HUD's code, never have been moved from another site and be built after a date USDA sets ([7 CFR 3555.208](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/section-3555.208)).

### What will fail a USDA inspection?

Anything that keeps an existing home from meeting HUD's standards for one- to four-unit homes: it must be structurally sound, in good repair or repaired with loan funds, and have safe electrical, heating, plumbing, water and wastewater systems. Common problems in rural Michigan homes: a well that fails a water test, a failing septic system, a worn-out roof, no working heat, peeling paint in a pre-1978 home and structural damage. See [buying a home with a septic system](/learn/buying-home-with-septic-system/) and [well water and home value](/learn/how-well-water-impacts-homes-value/).

### Can you buy land or a hobby farm with a USDA loan?

Not land by itself. Vacant land without a home, and property used mainly for farming or a business, are not eligible. A house on acreage can qualify if the site is typical in size for the area. See [buying a home in a rural area](/learn/tips-for-buying-home-in-rural-area/).

## What disqualifies you from a USDA loan?

Most often, the address or the household income. After those come credit the lender will not accept, debt ratios that GUS will not approve and no waiver supports, already owning a home that still meets your needs, a co-signer who will not live there, or a property with an income-producing use or major repairs.

An outstanding federal judgment or delinquent federal debt also stops a file until it is resolved, and so does child support delinquent enough for federal collection.

## Questions about USDA credit and debt rules

### What credit score do I need for a USDA home loan?

USDA's regulation sets no minimum score. Its Guaranteed Underwriting System generally looks for 640 or higher before it returns an automated approval, and files below that are usually underwritten manually with more documentation. Many lenders also add a higher minimum score of their own.

### What is the maximum DTI for a USDA loan?

The standard limits are 29% of gross monthly income for the housing payment and 41% for all monthly debts. GUS can approve higher ratios on a strong file, and a manual file can go higher only with documented compensating factors.

### Can you have a co-signer on a USDA loan?

Not one who lives elsewhere. USDA Guaranteed loans do not allow non-occupant co-signers or co-borrowers. A co-borrower who will live in the home can be added if they meet the credit and income rules; their income and debts then count.

### Can you have two USDA loans?

Usually not at the same time. You cannot be responsible for another USDA loan when the new one closes. You can keep a current home only in limited cases, such as when it no longer meets your household's needs, and you can use the program again after selling.

### Is there a limit on how much you can borrow with a USDA loan?

Not a set dollar limit on Guaranteed loans. How much you can borrow depends on your repayment income, the 29% and 41% debt ratios and the appraised value. USDA's Direct loans, which Rural Development makes itself, do have area loan limits.

<!-- [COMPLIANCE REVIEW]: FAQ below states USDA's down payment rule as the agency's rule, with the regulation linked; no CTA in this block. -->

### Can I put a down payment on a USDA loan?

Yes. USDA does not require a down payment: its rule lets the loan cover the full price, up to the appraised value plus the upfront guarantee fee ([7 CFR 3555.103](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/section-3555.103)). Money you put down lowers the amount you borrow. Closing costs still have to be covered in cash, through credits or in the loan when the appraisal leaves room.
