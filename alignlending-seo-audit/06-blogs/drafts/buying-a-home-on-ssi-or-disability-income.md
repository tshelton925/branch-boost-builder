# Buying a home on SSI or disability income in Michigan

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 17, 2026

<div class="al-answer">

Yes. If you receive Social Security disability benefits, Supplemental Security Income (SSI), VA disability or long-term disability payments, you can buy a home in Michigan. Lenders count those benefits when they are documented and expected to continue, and federal law bars discrimination because income comes from public assistance. On SSI, how you save matters, and Michigan's MiABLE account can help.

</div>

<div class="al-takeaways">

**Key takeaways**

- **Disability income counts.** Fannie Mae, FHA, VA and USDA rules accept documented disability benefits as income.
- **Lenders can't discriminate.** The Equal Credit Opportunity Act and Regulation B make public assistance income, including Social Security and SSI, a prohibited basis.
- **SSI's resource limit is $2,000 for one person and $3,000 for a couple.** The home you live in does not count, and neither does the first $100,000 in an ABLE account such as MiABLE.
- **Michigan help has rules.** Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA) programs work through participating lenders, and its First-Generation assistance is closed. See [Michigan down payment assistance options](/purchase/down-payment-assistance/).

</div>

## Can you get a mortgage on SSI or SSDI?

Yes. Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI), SSI and other disability benefits can qualify as income for conventional, FHA, VA and USDA loans when you document them with an award letter and they are expected to continue. The loan still has to fit the same debt-to-income and credit rules as any other loan.

SSDI is paid on your work record. SSI is needs-based, with a resource limit, so how you save matters.

## How do lenders count disability income?

Lenders use the monthly benefit on your award letter or benefit statement. Program rules let them gross up non-taxable benefits by a set percentage, which raises the income used to qualify. For Social Security disability on your own record, Fannie Mae does not require proof of continuance unless there is reason to believe it will end.

| Income type | Lenders usually ask for | Continuance | Gross-up? |
|---|---|---|---|
| SSDI, own work record | SSA award letter, SSA-1099, tax return or proof of current receipt | Not verified unless there is reason to doubt it | The non-taxable part |
| SSI | SSA award letter and proof of current receipt | Expected to last 3 years; no end date needed | Yes, once documented as non-taxable |
| Social Security from another person's record, or paid for a dependent | SSA award letter and proof of current receipt | At least 3 years from the application | If not taxed |
| VA disability compensation | VA award letter or benefit statement | Not required | Yes, not taxed |
| Private long-term disability | Policy or benefit statement from the payer | Not verified unless there is reason to doubt it; a scheduled review is not a reason | If verified as not taxed |
| Workers' compensation | Payer's benefit statement | Lender judgment (general rule) | If verified as not taxed |

Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide [B3-3.4-15](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.4-15/social-security-income) (10/07/2026), [B3-3.4-09](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.4-09/long-term-disability-income) (03/04/2026), [B3-3.4-18](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.4-18/va-benefits-income) (10/07/2026) and [B3-3.1-01](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.1-01/general-income-information) (10/07/2026). FHA, VA and USDA rules can differ.

Fannie Mae's general income rule lets the lender add 25% to verified non-taxable income. For Social Security, 15% of the benefit can count as non-taxable without extra documents; more needs proof. FHA allows the greater of 15% or the borrower's tax rate from the prior year ([HUD Handbook 4000.1](https://www.hud.gov/hud-partners/single-family-handbook-4000-1)). For retirement income, see [getting a mortgage at 60, 70 or 80](/learn/mortgage-for-seniors/).

Actual rates, costs and payments vary.** A $1,000 monthly SSI benefit, documented as non-taxable and grossed up 25% under Fannie Mae's rule, counts as $1,250 of income when the lender checks your [debt-to-income limits by loan type](/learn/debt-to-income-ratio-dti/).

## Will saving for a down payment affect your SSI?

It can. SSI has a resource limit of $2,000 for one person and $3,000 for a couple, so cash savings above it can affect eligibility. The home you live in does not count. An ABLE account, such as Michigan's MiABLE, lets eligible people save, and the first $100,000 does not count. Check with the Social Security Administration before moving money.

Those limits have not changed since 1989 ([20 CFR 416.1205](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-416.1205)), and SSA counts resources as of the first moment of each month ([20 CFR 416.1207](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-416.1207)). SSA excludes the home you own and live in, whatever its value ([20 CFR 416.1212](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-416.1212)). The savings for it are not excluded, which is where an ABLE account helps.

Since January 1, 2026, you can open one if your disability began before age 46. [MiABLE](https://www.michigan.gov/miable/frequently-asked-questions/for-account-holders-and-caregivers/general), run by the Michigan Department of Treasury, lists housing as a qualified disability expense, sets the 2026 contribution limit at $20,000 and does not count the first $100,000 toward SSI's limit. SSA counts a housing withdrawal as a resource if you keep it into the next month, so time it for the month you close ([SSA's ABLE spotlight](https://www.ssa.gov/ssi/spotlights/spot-able.html)). SSDI has no resource limit, but if you receive both SSDI and SSI, the SSI limit applies.

## What loan programs work for buyers with disability income?

Conventional, FHA, VA (for eligible veterans) and USDA (in eligible areas) loans all accept documented disability income. The right one depends on your credit, the cash you have for closing and the address. MSHDA's MI Home Loan can pair with some of them for first-time buyers, through participating lenders.

MSHDA makes its [MI Home Loan](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/mi-home-loan) through participating lenders, for first-time buyers statewide and repeat buyers in targeted areas. MSHDA lists household income limits, a 640 minimum credit score and a $566,355 sales price limit (after June 1, 2026). Its [MI 10K DPA loan](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/mi-10k-dpa-loan), up to $10,000, must be paired with an MI Home Loan. The [First-Generation down payment assistance](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/firstgendpa) program is closed: its funds are exhausted and there is no waitlist. County limits are in the [MSHDA MI Home Loan](/learn/mshda-mi-home-loan/) guide.

## Are there housing grants for people with disabilities in Michigan?

There is no single federal grant to buy a home. Help comes as down payment assistance from MSHDA and some cities, Housing Choice Voucher homeownership programs where offered, adapted-housing grants for eligible veterans, and nonprofit programs. Each has its own income and eligibility rules, and many are loans rather than grants.

- **Key to Own.** MSHDA's program lets current MSHDA Housing Choice Voucher holders convert a rental voucher into a homeownership voucher, with exceptions to its work-income rule for people with disabilities ([MSHDA](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/rental/housing-choice-voucher/key-to-own-homeownership-program)).
- **VA adapted-housing grants** help eligible veterans and service members with certain service-connected disabilities buy, build or adapt a home ([VA](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/disability-housing-grants/)).
- **Habitat for Humanity affiliates** in Michigan run their own homeownership programs.

Align's guide to [Michigan first-time homebuyer programs and grants](/learn/first-time-home-buyer-grants-and-programs/) lists city and county programs.

## Can a lender turn you down because your income comes from disability benefits?

No. The Equal Credit Opportunity Act and Regulation B bar discrimination because any part of your income comes from a public assistance program, and the Fair Housing Act prohibits disability discrimination in housing and mortgage lending. A lender may still verify the amount and that it will continue, as it would for any income.

Under [Regulation B](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/6/), a lender may not discount income because of a prohibited basis, though it may weigh the amount and probable continuance of any income. [HUD](https://www.hud.gov/stat/fheo/rights-obligations) lists refusing to consider a mortgage applicant's disability-related income, such as SSI or SSDI, as an example of discrimination under the Fair Housing Act. If you think a lender treated you unfairly, you can [submit a complaint to the CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/) or [report it to HUD](https://www.hud.gov/reporthousingdiscrimination). ## What Michigan property tax help is available to homeowners with disabilities?

Michigan's homestead property tax credit has a category for homeowners who are totally and permanently disabled, and low-income owners can ask the local board of review for a poverty exemption. Veterans with qualifying VA ratings may owe no property tax on their home under MCL 211.7b. Each has its own eligibility test.

- **Homestead property tax credit.** Treasury uses Social Security's definition of totally and permanently disabled ([2025 MI-1040CR instructions](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/-/media/Project/Websites/taxes/Forms/IIT/TY2025/MI-1040CR-Instr.pdf)). Tax year 2025 limits include a $165,400 taxable value and $71,500 in household resources, and the category cannot be claimed by anyone who was 66 by February 28, 2025 ([Treasury](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/credits-exemptions/hptc/tax-year-credit-information/2025)). See the [Michigan homestead property tax credit](/learn/michigan-homestead-property-tax-credit/) guide.
- **Poverty exemption.** Under MCL 211.7u, you apply to your city or township on Form 5737, the Application and Affirmation (Form 4988 if you are not required to file income tax returns), and the local unit sets the income and asset guidelines ([State Tax Commission](https://www.michigan.gov/treasury/-/media/Project/Websites/treasury/STC/Bulletins/2025/Bulletin-17-of-2025---Poverty-Exemption.pdf)).
- **Disabled veterans exemption.** [MCL 211.7b](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-7b) exempts the homestead of a veteran the VA rates 100% permanently and totally disabled or individually unemployable, or who received a VA adapted-housing grant.

## What should you look for in the home itself?

Think about accessibility needs now and later: entry steps, door widths, a main-floor bedroom and bath, and room to turn in the kitchen and bathroom. Price the modifications before you make an offer, and ask whether renovation financing can include them in the loan.

The guide to [buying a home for aging in place](/learn/buying-home-for-aging-in-place/) lists features to check on a showing, and [renovation financing](/purchase/renovation-financing/) explains how some loans fund improvements at closing.

## Questions about buying a home on disability income

### Can you buy a house on SSI disability?

Yes. SSI counts as income for conventional, FHA, VA and USDA loans when an SSA letter documents it. What limits you is the loan's debt-to-income and credit rules and the cash needed for closing, so plan how you will hold savings under SSI's resource limit.

### Do lenders count SSDI as income?

Yes. Lenders count SSDI from your SSA award or benefit letter. For SSDI on your own work record, Fannie Mae does not require the lender to prove it will continue unless there is reason to believe it will stop, and the non-taxable part can be grossed up.

### Is there a down payment grant for people with disabilities?

There is no single federal down payment grant for people with disabilities. In Michigan, help comes from MSHDA's MI 10K DPA, which is a loan, some city and county programs, MSHDA's Key to Own voucher program, VA adapted-housing grants and Habitat for Humanity affiliates.

### Will buying a house affect my SSI?

Owning the home you live in does not count against SSI's resource limit. Cash can: money above the limit at the start of any month counts, and if someone else pays your housing costs, SSA may count that as income and reduce your SSI. Report the purchase to SSA and ask how it will be treated.

### Can disability income be grossed up for a mortgage?

Yes, when it is not taxed. Fannie Mae lets lenders add 25% to verified non-taxable income and asks for extra documentation to gross up more than 15% of Social Security income. FHA allows the greater of 15% or your prior-year tax rate. Your lender applies the rule for your loan type.

### Can I use a Housing Choice Voucher to buy a home in Michigan?

Possibly, through MSHDA's Key to Own program. You need an active MSHDA voucher in good standing and no home owned in the last 3 years. MSHDA also lists homebuyer classes, being debt-free, credit scores over 640 and $1,500 saved for 2 months, with an exception to its earned-income rule for people with disabilities.

### What credit score do I need with disability income?

The same score as any other borrower, because disability income does not change a program's credit rules. MSHDA's MI Home Loan lists a 640 minimum, and conventional, FHA, VA and USDA loans each set their own standards. See [what credit score you need](/learn/whats-good-credit-score-to-buy-house/).
