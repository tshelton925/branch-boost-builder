# Buying a home on SSI or disability income in Michigan

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 16, 2026

Yes. If you receive Social Security disability benefits, Supplemental Security Income (SSI), VA disability or long-term disability payments, you can buy a home in Michigan. Lenders count those benefits when they are documented and expected to continue, and federal law bars discrimination because income comes from public assistance. On SSI, how you save matters, and Michigan's MiABLE account can help.

**Key takeaways**

- **Disability income counts.** Fannie Mae, FHA, VA and USDA rules accept documented disability benefits as income.
- **Lenders can't discriminate.** The Equal Credit Opportunity Act and Regulation B make public assistance income, including Social Security and SSI, a prohibited basis.
- **SSI's resource limit is $2,000 for one person and $3,000 for a couple.** The home you live in does not count, and neither does the first $100,000 in an ABLE account such as MiABLE.
- **Michigan help has rules.** MSHDA programs work through participating lenders, and its First-Generation assistance is closed. See [Michigan down payment assistance options](/purchase/down-payment-assistance/).

## Can you get a mortgage on SSI or SSDI?

Yes. Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI), SSI and other disability benefits can qualify as income for conventional, FHA, VA and USDA loans when you document them with an award letter and they are expected to continue. The loan still has to fit the same debt-to-income and credit rules as any other loan.

SSDI is paid on your own work record. SSI is a needs-based payment with a resource limit ([Social Security Administration](https://www.ssa.gov/ssi/text-resources-ussi.htm)). Underwriters treat both as steady monthly income. The difference shows up in how you save for the purchase.

## How do lenders count disability income?

Lenders use the monthly benefit on your award letter or benefit statement. Program rules let them gross up non-taxable benefits by a set percentage, which raises the income used to qualify. For Social Security disability on your own record, Fannie Mae does not require proof of continuance unless there is reason to believe it will end.

[VERIFY: Fannie Mae B3-3.4-15 and B3-3.4-09 continuance wording; HUD 4000.1 Social Security and disability income sections; VA Lenders Handbook M26-7 chapter 4; USDA HB-1-3555 chapter 9]

| Income type | Lenders usually ask for | Continuance | Gross-up? |
|---|---|---|---|
| SSDI, own work record | SSA award letter, proof of receipt | Not verified unless there is reason to doubt it | Non-taxable part |
| SSI | SSA award letter, proof of receipt | [VERIFY: Fannie Mae rule for SSI] | Yes, not taxed |
| Social Security paid for a dependent | SSA letter, proof of receipt | At least 3 years [VERIFY] | If not taxed |
| VA disability compensation | VA benefit letter | Not required | Yes, not taxed |
| Private long-term disability | Policy or benefit statement | Not verified unless there is reason to doubt it | Non-taxable part [VERIFY] |
| Workers' compensation | Payer's benefit statement | Lender judgment [VERIFY] | If not taxed [VERIFY] |

Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide sections [B3-3.4-15](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.4-15/social-security-income), [B3-3.4-09](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.4-09/long-term-disability-income) and [B3-3.4-18](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.4-18/va-benefits-income), March 2026 versions. FHA, VA and USDA rules can differ.

Fannie Mae's [general income rules](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.1-01/general-income-information) let the lender add 25% to verified non-taxable income, and grossing up more than 15% of Social Security income needs extra documentation because part of it can be taxed. FHA allows 15%, or more when tax returns support it ([HUD Handbook 4000.1](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/administration/hudclips/handbooks/hsgh/4000.1)). [VERIFY: HUD 4000.1 gross-up text and current URL]

A [2014 CFPB bulletin](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/201411_cfpb_bulletin_disability-income.pdf) warns that asking for more proof than agency rules require, such as a doctor's statement, can raise fair lending concerns. [VERIFY: CFPB Bulletin 2014-03 wording]

Hypothetical example, not an offer. Actual rates, costs and payments vary. A $1,000 monthly SSI benefit, grossed up 25% under Fannie Mae's rule, counts as $1,250 of income when the lender checks your [debt-to-income limits by loan type](/learn/debt-to-income-ratio-dti/).

## Will saving for a down payment affect your SSI?

It can. SSI has a resource limit of $2,000 for one person and $3,000 for a couple, so cash savings above it can affect eligibility. The home you live in does not count. An ABLE account, such as Michigan's MiABLE, lets eligible people save, and the first $100,000 does not count. Check with the Social Security Administration before moving money.

[VERIFY: SSA 2026 resource limits; resources are counted as of the first moment of each month]

SSA excludes the home you own and live in, whatever its value ([20 CFR 416.1212](https://www.ssa.gov/OP_Home/cfr20/416/416-1212.htm)). The savings for it are not excluded, which is where an ABLE account helps.

Since January 1, 2026, you can open one if your disability began before age 46 ([SSA](https://www.ssa.gov/ssi/spotlights/spot-able.html)). [MiABLE](https://www.michigan.gov/miable/frequently-asked-questions/for-account-holders-and-caregivers/general), run by the Michigan Department of Treasury, lists housing as a qualified disability expense and sets the 2026 contribution limit at $20,000. SSA counts a housing withdrawal as a resource if you keep it into the next month, so time the withdrawal for the month you close. [VERIFY: SSA ABLE spotlight and POMS SI 01130.740 on housing distributions used for a purchase]

SSDI has no resource limit, but if you receive both SSDI and SSI, the SSI limit applies. [VERIFY: SSA]

## What loan programs work for buyers with disability income?

Conventional, FHA, VA (for eligible veterans) and USDA (in eligible areas) loans all accept documented disability income. The right one depends on your credit, the cash you have for closing and the address. MSHDA's MI Home Loan can pair with some of them for first-time buyers, through participating lenders.

The Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA) makes its [MI Home Loan](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/homeownership/mi-home-loan) through participating lenders, for first-time buyers statewide and repeat buyers in targeted areas. MSHDA lists household income limits, a 640 minimum credit score and a 2026 sales price limit of $566,355. Its [MI 10K DPA loan](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/mi-10k-dpa-loan), up to $10,000, must be paired with an MI Home Loan. The [First-Generation down payment assistance](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/firstgendpa) program is closed: its funds are exhausted and there is no waitlist. ## Are there housing grants for people with disabilities in Michigan?

There is no single federal grant to buy a home. Help comes as down payment assistance from MSHDA and some cities, Housing Choice Voucher homeownership programs where offered, adapted-housing grants for eligible veterans, and nonprofit programs. Each has its own income and eligibility rules, and many are loans rather than grants.

- **Key to Own.** MSHDA's program lets current MSHDA Housing Choice Voucher holders convert a rental voucher into a homeownership voucher ([MSHDA](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/rental/housing-choice-voucher/key-to-own-homeownership-program)).
- **VA adapted-housing grants** help eligible veterans and service members with certain service-connected disabilities buy, build or adapt a home ([VA](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/disability-housing-grants/)). [VERIFY: VA eligibility wording]
- **Habitat for Humanity affiliates** in Michigan run their own homeownership programs.

Align's guide to [Michigan first-time homebuyer programs and grants](/learn/first-time-home-buyer-grants-and-programs/) lists city and county programs.

## Can a lender turn you down because your income comes from disability benefits?

No. The Equal Credit Opportunity Act and Regulation B bar discrimination because any part of your income comes from a public assistance program, and the Fair Housing Act prohibits disability discrimination in housing and mortgage lending. A lender may still verify the amount and that it will continue, as it would for any income.

[VERIFY: 12 CFR 1002.2(z) and comment 2(z)-3 (Social Security and SSI as public assistance); 1002.6(b)(5)]

Under [Regulation B](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/6/), a lender may not discount or exclude income because of a prohibited basis, but it may consider the amount and probable continuance of any income. HUD's [fair lending page](https://www.hud.gov/topics/fair_lending) covers the Fair Housing Act side. If you think a lender treated you unfairly, you can [submit a complaint to the CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/) or to HUD. Align Lending is an Equal Housing Opportunity company.

## What Michigan property tax help is available to homeowners with disabilities?

Michigan's homestead property tax credit has a category for homeowners who are totally and permanently disabled, and low-income owners can ask the local board of review for a poverty exemption. Veterans with qualifying VA ratings may owe no property tax on their home under MCL 211.7b. Each has its own eligibility test.

[VERIFY: MI-1040CR instructions on how the disability category changes the credit; MCL 211.7u; MCL 211.7b]

- **Homestead property tax credit.** The disability category uses Social Security's definition. Treasury's tax year 2025 limits include a $165,400 taxable value and $71,500 in household resources, and the category cannot be claimed by anyone who was 66 by February 28, 2025 ([Michigan Department of Treasury](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/credits-exemptions/hptc/tax-year-credit-information/2025)). <!-- LINK WHEN LIVE: "Michigan homestead property tax credit" → /learn/michigan-homestead-property-tax-credit/ -->
- **Poverty exemption.** Under MCL 211.7u, you apply to your city or township on Form 5737 (Form 4988 if you do not file income tax returns), and the local unit sets the income and asset guidelines ([State Tax Commission](https://www.michigan.gov/treasury/-/media/Project/Websites/treasury/STC/Bulletins/2025/Bulletin-17-of-2025---Poverty-Exemption.pdf)).
- **Disabled veterans exemption.** [MCL 211.7b](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/mcl-211-7b.pdf) exempts the homestead of qualifying disabled veterans.

## What should you look for in the home itself?

Think about accessibility needs now and later: entry steps, door widths, a main-floor bedroom and bath, and room to turn in the kitchen and bathroom. Price the modifications before you make an offer, and ask whether renovation financing can include them in the loan.

A step-free entry or a first-floor bathroom can cost less than adding a ramp and a new bath. The guide to [buying a home for aging in place](/learn/buying-home-for-aging-in-place/) lists features to check on a showing, and [renovation financing](/purchase/renovation-financing/) explains how some loans fund improvements at closing.

## Questions about buying a home on disability income

### Can you buy a house on SSI disability?

Yes. SSI counts as income for conventional, FHA, VA and USDA loans when an SSA letter documents it. What limits you is the loan's debt-to-income and credit rules and the cash needed for closing, so plan how you will hold savings under SSI's resource limit.

### Do lenders count SSDI as income?

Yes. Lenders count SSDI from your SSA award or benefit letter. For SSDI on your own work record, Fannie Mae does not require the lender to prove it will continue unless there is reason to believe it will stop, and the non-taxable part can be grossed up.

### Is there a down payment grant for people with disabilities?

There is no single federal down payment grant for people with disabilities. In Michigan, help comes from MSHDA's MI 10K DPA, which is a loan, some city and county programs, MSHDA's Key to Own voucher program, VA adapted-housing grants and Habitat for Humanity affiliates.

### Will buying a house affect my SSI?

Owning the home you live in does not count against SSI's resource limit. Cash can: money above the limit at the start of any month counts, and if someone else pays your housing costs, SSA may reduce your SSI. Report the purchase to SSA and ask how it will be treated.

<!-- [VERIFY: SSA home exclusion; in-kind support and maintenance when someone else pays housing costs; treatment of savings spent at closing] -->

### Can disability income be grossed up for a mortgage?

Yes, when it is not taxed. Fannie Mae lets lenders add 25% to verified non-taxable income and asks for extra documentation to gross up more than 15% of Social Security income. FHA allows 15%, or more when tax returns support it. Your lender applies the rule for your loan type.

<!-- [VERIFY: Fannie Mae B3-3.1-01, B3-3.4-15; HUD 4000.1 gross-up] -->

### Can I use a Housing Choice Voucher to buy a home in Michigan?

Possibly, through MSHDA's Key to Own program. You need an active MSHDA voucher in good standing and no home owned in the last 3 years. MSHDA also lists homebuyer classes, being debt-free, credit scores over 640 and $1,500 saved for 2 months, with an exception to its earned-income rule for people with disabilities.

<!-- [VERIFY at publish: MSHDA Key to Own page, program status and contact] -->

### What credit score do I need with disability income?

The same score as any other borrower, because disability income does not change a program's credit rules. MSHDA's MI Home Loan lists a 640 minimum, and conventional, FHA, VA and USDA loans each set their own standards. See [what credit score you need](/learn/whats-good-credit-score-to-buy-house/).
