# Buying a home on SSI or disability income in Michigan

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 16, 2026

Yes. If you receive Social Security disability benefits, Supplemental Security Income, VA disability or long-term disability payments, you can buy a home in Michigan. Lenders count those benefits as income when they are documented and expected to continue, and federal law bars discrimination because income comes from public assistance. If you receive SSI, how you save matters, and Michigan's MiABLE account can help.

**Key takeaways**

- **Disability income counts.** Fannie Mae, FHA, VA and USDA rules all accept documented disability benefits. For Social Security disability on your own work record, Fannie Mae does not ask the lender to prove it will continue unless there is reason to doubt it.
- **Lenders can't discriminate.** The Equal Credit Opportunity Act and Regulation B treat income from public assistance, including Social Security and SSI, as a prohibited basis.
- **SSI has a $2,000 resource limit for one person ($3,000 for a couple).** The home you live in does not count, and up to $100,000 in an ABLE account such as MiABLE does not count.
- **Michigan help has rules.** MSHDA's MI Home Loan and MI 10K DPA work through participating lenders, and the First-Generation program is closed. See [Michigan down payment assistance options](/purchase/down-payment-assistance/).

## Can you get a mortgage on SSI or SSDI?

Yes. Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI), Supplemental Security Income (SSI) and other disability benefits can qualify as income for conventional, FHA, VA and USDA loans. You document them with an award letter, and they must be expected to continue. The loan still has to fit the same debt-to-income and credit rules as any other.

The two Social Security programs work differently. SSDI is paid on your own work record. SSI is a needs-based payment for people with limited income and resources who have a disability, are blind or are 65 or older, and it has a resource limit ([Social Security Administration](https://www.ssa.gov/ssi/text-resources-ussi.htm)). Both are steady monthly income in an underwriter's eyes. What changes is how you save for the purchase, covered below.

## How do lenders count disability income?

Lenders use the monthly benefit on your award letter or benefit statement. Program rules let them gross up non-taxable benefits by a set percentage, which raises the income used to qualify. For Social Security disability on your own record, Fannie Mae generally does not require proof of continuance unless there is reason to believe the benefit will end.

[VERIFY: Fannie Mae B3-3.4-15 and B3-3.4-09 continuance wording; HUD 4000.1 Social Security and disability income sections; VA Lenders Handbook M26-7 chapter 4]

The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's 2014 bulletin](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/201411_cfpb_bulletin_disability-income.pdf) warns lenders that asking for more proof than agency rules require, such as a doctor's statement, can raise fair lending concerns. A scheduled review date is not, on its own, an end date. [VERIFY: CFPB Bulletin 2014-03 text on review dates]

| Income type | What lenders usually ask for | Must it last 3 more years? | Gross-up allowed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| SSDI (your own work record) | SSA award or benefit letter, proof of receipt | Fannie Mae: not verified unless there is reason to doubt it | Yes, for the non-taxable part |
| SSI | SSA award or benefit letter, proof of receipt | [VERIFY: Fannie Mae section and continuance rule for SSI] | Yes, SSI is not taxed |
| Social Security paid for a dependent, such as a child with a disability | SSA letter, proof the benefit is received | Yes, documented for at least 3 years [VERIFY] | Yes, if not taxed |
| VA disability compensation | VA benefit letter | Fannie Mae: not required for VA long-term disability | Yes, it is not taxed |
| Private long-term disability insurance | Policy or benefit statement from the payer | Fannie Mae: not verified unless there is reason to doubt it | Only the non-taxable part [VERIFY] |
| Workers' compensation | Benefit statement from the payer | Lender judgment [VERIFY] | Yes, if not taxed [VERIFY] |

Sources: Fannie Mae Selling Guide [B3-3.4-15 Social Security income](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.4-15/social-security-income), [B3-3.4-09 long-term disability income](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.4-09/long-term-disability-income) and [B3-3.4-18 VA benefits income](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.4-18/va-benefits-income), March 2026 versions. FHA, VA and USDA rules can differ.

Fannie Mae's [general income rules (B3-3.1-01)](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.1-01/general-income-information) let the lender add 25% to verified non-taxable income. Because part of SSDI can be taxable, grossing up more than 15% of Social Security income needs extra documentation. FHA allows 15%, or more when the borrower's tax returns support it, under [HUD Handbook 4000.1](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/administration/hudclips/handbooks/hsgh/4000.1). [VERIFY: HUD 4000.1 gross-up text and URL]

Hypothetical example, not an offer. Actual rates, costs and payments vary. A $1,000 monthly SSI benefit, grossed up by 25% under Fannie Mae's rule, counts as $1,250 of monthly income when the lender works out your [debt-to-income limits by loan type](/learn/debt-to-income-ratio-dti/).

## Will saving for a down payment affect your SSI?

It can. SSI has a resource limit of $2,000 for one person and $3,000 for a couple, so cash savings above it can affect eligibility. The home you live in does not count. An ABLE account, such as Michigan's MiABLE, lets eligible people save, and the first $100,000 does not count. Check with SSA before moving money.

[VERIFY: SSA 2026 resource limits; SSA counts resources as of the first moment of each month]

SSA's rule on the home is broad: the home you own and live in is excluded from resources regardless of its value ([20 CFR 416.1212](https://www.ssa.gov/OP_Home/cfr20/416/416-1212.htm)). The money you are saving for it is not. That is where an ABLE account helps.

Since January 1, 2026, you can open an ABLE account if your disability began before age 46 ([SSA ABLE spotlight](https://www.ssa.gov/ssi/spotlights/spot-able.html)). The [MiABLE program](https://www.michigan.gov/miable/frequently-asked-questions/for-account-holders-and-caregivers/general), run by the Michigan Department of Treasury, lists housing as a qualified disability expense and sets the 2026 contribution limit at $20,000. One timing rule matters: SSA counts a housing withdrawal as a resource if you keep it into the next month, so plan the withdrawal for the month you close. [VERIFY: SSA ABLE spotlight and POMS SI 01130.740 on housing distributions used for a purchase]

SSDI has no resource limit, so savings do not affect SSDI itself. If you receive both SSDI and SSI, the SSI limit still applies. [VERIFY: SSA]

Benefits rules are SSA's to apply, not a lender's. Check with the Social Security Administration before moving money, and report the purchase when you buy.

## What loan programs work for buyers with disability income?

Conventional, FHA, VA (for eligible veterans) and USDA (in eligible areas) loans all accept documented disability income. The right one depends on your credit, the cash you have for closing and the address. MSHDA's MI Home Loan can pair with some of them for first-time buyers, through participating lenders.

The Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA) makes its [MI Home Loan](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/homeownership/mi-home-loan) only through participating lenders. MSHDA lists it for first-time buyers statewide (no home owned in the previous three years) and repeat buyers in targeted areas, with household income limits, a 640 minimum credit score and a 2026 sales price limit of $566,355 after June 1, 2026. Its [MI 10K DPA loan](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/mi-10k-dpa-loan) adds up to $10,000 toward down payment, closing costs and prepaid expenses, repaid when you sell, refinance or pay off the first mortgage. MSHDA's [First-Generation down payment assistance](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/firstgendpa) is closed: its funds are exhausted and there is no waitlist. ## Are there housing grants for people with disabilities in Michigan?

There is no single federal grant to buy a home. Help comes as down payment assistance from MSHDA and some cities, Housing Choice Voucher homeownership programs where offered, adapted-housing grants for eligible veterans, and nonprofit programs. Each has its own income and eligibility rules, and many are loans rather than grants.

[VERIFY: local Michigan city and county DPA programs named in the refreshed first-time buyer guide]

- **MSHDA's Key to Own program** lets current MSHDA Housing Choice Voucher holders turn a rental voucher into a homeownership voucher ([MSHDA](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/rental/housing-choice-voucher/key-to-own-homeownership-program)).
- **VA adapted-housing grants** help eligible veterans and service members with certain service-connected disabilities build or adapt a home ([VA](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/disability-housing-grants/)). [VERIFY: VA eligibility wording]
- **Habitat for Humanity affiliates** in Michigan run their own homeownership programs. Ask the local affiliate about eligibility.

Align's guide to [Michigan first-time homebuyer programs and grants](/learn/first-time-home-buyer-grants-and-programs/) lists city and county programs.

## Can a lender turn you down because your income comes from disability benefits?

No. The Equal Credit Opportunity Act and Regulation B bar discrimination because any part of your income comes from a public assistance program, and the Fair Housing Act prohibits disability discrimination in housing and mortgage lending. A lender may still verify the amount and that it will continue, as it would for any income.

[VERIFY: 12 CFR 1002.2(z) and comment 2(z)-3 (Social Security and SSI as public assistance); 1002.6(b)(5)]

Under [Regulation B, section 1002.6](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/6/), a lender may not discount or exclude income because of a prohibited basis, but it may consider the amount and probable continuance of any income. HUD's [fair lending page](https://www.hud.gov/topics/fair_lending) explains the Fair Housing Act side. HUD has settled cases with lenders that asked applicants with disabilities for extra proof, or about the nature of a disability. [VERIFY: HUD settlement releases]

If you think a lender treated you unfairly, you can [submit a complaint to the CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/) or contact HUD. Align Lending is an Equal Housing Opportunity company.

## What Michigan property tax help is available to homeowners with disabilities?

Michigan's homestead property tax credit has a category for homeowners who are totally and permanently disabled, and low-income owners can ask the local board of review for a poverty exemption. Veterans with qualifying VA ratings may owe no property tax on their home under MCL 211.7b. Each has its own income or eligibility test.

[VERIFY: MI-1040CR instructions for how the disabled category changes the credit; MCL 211.7u; MCL 211.7b]

- **Homestead property tax credit.** Michigan uses Social Security's definition of disability for this category. For tax year 2025, Treasury lists a $165,400 taxable value limit, a $71,500 household resources limit and a $1,900 maximum credit, and the disabled category cannot be claimed by anyone who was 66 by February 28, 2025 ([Michigan Department of Treasury](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/credits-exemptions/hptc/tax-year-credit-information/2025)). <!-- LINK WHEN LIVE: "Michigan homestead property tax credit" → /learn/michigan-homestead-property-tax-credit/ -->
- **Poverty exemption.** MCL 211.7u allows a full or partial exemption for the principal residence of owners who cannot pay because of poverty. You file Form 5737 with your city or township, which sets its own income and asset guidelines; Form 4988 is for applicants who do not file income tax returns ([State Tax Commission](https://www.michigan.gov/treasury/-/media/Project/Websites/treasury/STC/Bulletins/2025/Bulletin-17-of-2025---Poverty-Exemption.pdf)).
- **Disabled veterans exemption.** [MCL 211.7b](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/mcl-211-7b.pdf) exempts the homestead of qualifying disabled veterans.

## What should you look for in the home itself?

Think about accessibility needs now and later: entry steps, door widths, a main-floor bedroom and bath, and room to turn in the kitchen and bathroom. Price the modifications before you make an offer, and ask whether renovation financing can include them in the loan.

A home with a step-free entry or a first-floor bathroom can cost less to adapt than one that needs a ramp and a new bath. Align's guide to [buying a home for aging in place](/learn/buying-home-for-aging-in-place/) lists features to check on a showing, and [renovation financing](/purchase/renovation-financing/) explains how some loans fund improvements at closing.

## Questions about buying a home on disability income

### Can you buy a house on SSI disability?

Yes. SSI counts as income for conventional, FHA, VA and USDA loans when an SSA letter documents it. The limits are the loan's debt-to-income and credit rules, and the cash you need for closing. Because SSI has a resource limit, plan how you will hold the money for closing before you start saving.

### Do lenders count SSDI as income?

Yes. Lenders count SSDI from your SSA award or benefit letter. Fannie Mae does not require a lender to prove that SSDI on your own work record will continue unless there is reason to believe it will stop. The non-taxable part can be grossed up under program rules.

### Is there a down payment grant for people with disabilities?

There is no single federal down payment grant for people with disabilities. Help in Michigan comes from MSHDA's MI 10K DPA, which is a loan, some city and county programs, MSHDA's Key to Own voucher program, VA adapted-housing grants for eligible veterans and local Habitat for Humanity affiliates.

### Will buying a house affect my SSI?

Owning the home you live in does not count against SSI's resource limit. Cash can: money above the limit at the start of a month counts, and if someone else pays your housing costs, SSA may reduce your payment. Report the purchase to SSA and ask how your situation will be treated.

<!-- [VERIFY: SSA home exclusion; in-kind support and maintenance rules when someone else pays housing costs; treatment of savings spent at closing] -->

### Can disability income be grossed up for a mortgage?

Yes, when it is not taxed. Fannie Mae lets lenders add 25% to verified non-taxable income, and asks for extra documentation to gross up more than 15% of Social Security income. FHA allows 15%, or more when tax returns support it. Your lender applies the rule for your loan type.

<!-- [VERIFY: Fannie Mae B3-3.1-01, B3-3.4-15; HUD 4000.1 gross-up] -->

### Can I use a Housing Choice Voucher to buy a home in Michigan?

Possibly, through MSHDA's Key to Own program. You need an active MSHDA voucher in good standing and no home owned in the last 3 years. MSHDA lists homebuyer classes, being debt-free, credit scores over 640 and $1,500 saved for 2 months, with an exception to its earned-income rule for people with disabilities.

<!-- [VERIFY at publish: MSHDA Key to Own page, program status and contact] -->

### What credit score do I need with disability income?

The same score as any other borrower. Disability income does not change a program's credit rules. MSHDA's MI Home Loan lists a 640 minimum, and conventional, FHA, VA and USDA loans each have their own standards. See [what credit score you need](/learn/whats-good-credit-score-to-buy-house/) for each loan type.
