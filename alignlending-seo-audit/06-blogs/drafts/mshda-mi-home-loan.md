# MSHDA MI Home Loan: who qualifies, the limits and how to apply in Michigan

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 16, 2026

**The short answer**

The MSHDA MI Home Loan is a fixed-rate first mortgage from the Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA), a state agency, for first-time buyers anywhere in Michigan and repeat buyers in targeted areas. Three limits decide it: the 2026 sales price limit of $566,355, a county income limit and a 640 minimum credit score. You get it through an MSHDA participating lender.

**Key takeaways**

- MSHDA's statewide sales price limit for the MI Home Loan is $566,355 for loans after June 1, 2026. Older figures that still circulate online, such as $544,233 and $224,500, are out of date.
- Income limits depend on the county, the household size (1 to 2 persons, or 3 or more) and whether the home is in a targeted area. In Oakland County outside its targeted cities, the June 1, 2026 chart allows $104,800 and $120,520.
- MI 10K adds up to $10,000 toward the down payment and closing costs. It is a deferred loan, not a grant, and it works only with an MI Home Loan.
- MSHDA's $25,000 First-Generation down payment assistance is closed: its funds are exhausted and there is no waitlist (status as of November 2026).

## What is the MSHDA MI Home Loan?

The MI Home Loan is a fixed-rate first mortgage run by the Michigan State Housing Development Authority, a state agency. It is a loan you repay, not a grant. MSHDA participating lenders originate it, and each one is underwritten as an FHA, VA, USDA Rural Development or conventional loan, so that loan type's rules apply too.

MSHDA sets the buyer, income, price and credit rules on its [MI Home Loan page](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/mi-home-loan). The loan type underneath sets the rest: mortgage insurance, the appraisal and the property standards. That is why two MI Home Loans can look different on a Loan Estimate. [VERIFY: the four loan types on MSHDA's MI Home Loan page; michigan.gov blocked automated access from this session]

MSHDA's help with the down payment, MI 10K, is a separate second loan that rides with the MI Home Loan. Align's page on [Michigan down payment assistance options](/purchase/down-payment-assistance/) sets it beside city, county and nonprofit help. MSHDA also runs a [Mortgage Credit Certificate](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/mortgage-credit-certificate-program), a federal tax credit of 20% of the mortgage interest you pay each year, claimed on [IRS Form 8396](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8396). [VERIFY: MCC availability in 2026 and any annual dollar cap, MSHDA MCC page]

### Is MSHDA a lender?

Not one you apply to. MSHDA funds the program and sets its rules. A participating lender takes your application, underwrites it to MSHDA's rules and the loan type's rules, and closes it. A lender that is not on MSHDA's list cannot originate an MI Home Loan.

## Who qualifies for a MSHDA loan?

First-time buyers qualify anywhere in Michigan, and repeat buyers qualify only in MSHDA's targeted areas. Household income must be at or under the county limit, the price at or under $566,355 (2026), and the credit score at least 640. You must live in the home as your principal residence and complete a homebuyer education class.

The 640 minimum is MSHDA's floor for the standard MI Home Loan. Lender guides report a 660 minimum for multi-section manufactured homes and for MSHDA's MI Home Loan Flex option, which looks only at the borrowers rather than every adult in the household. [VERIFY: 660 minimums and the Flex household rule, MSHDA MI Home Loan product guide]

### What counts as a first-time buyer for MSHDA?

MSHDA counts you as a first-time buyer if you have not owned a home in the previous three years. That matches the definition most programs use: no ownership interest in a principal residence during the past three years. A buyer who sold a home five years ago can count as first-time again. [VERIFY: how MSHDA treats a co-borrower's or spouse's past ownership]

### What is a targeted area?

A targeted area is a city, township or whole county where MSHDA accepts repeat buyers and sets higher income limits. MSHDA lists them county by county in its income and sales price limits chart. On the June 1, 2026 chart, all of Washtenaw County is targeted, and in Oakland County the City of Pontiac, Royal Oak Township and the City of Southfield are.

## What are the MSHDA income limits in 2026?

MSHDA sets MI Home Loan income limits by county and household size, in two columns: 1 to 2 persons, and 3 or more. Targeted areas get higher limits. The current chart is dated June 1, 2026, and MSHDA replaces it when new figures come out, so check the chart before relying on any number, including those below.

The figures below come from MSHDA's [Income and Sales Price Limits chart, June 1, 2026 (PDF)](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/-/media/Project/Websites/mshda/homeownership/Homebuyers/Income-and-Sales-Price-6126_accessible.pdf), which covers all 83 counties and also applies to the Mortgage Credit Certificate. Lender guides describe the standard MI Home Loan as counting the income of every adult who will live in the home, not only the borrowers. [VERIFY: MSHDA's household income definition]

### How much is considered low income in Michigan?

There is no single statewide number. HUD publishes [income limits for every county](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/datasets/il.html) each year, and its "low income" line is 80% of area median income. Fannie Mae's [HomeReady eligibility rule](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b5-6-01/homeready-mortgage-loan-and-borrower-eligibility) also uses 80% of area median income. MSHDA's chart for the MI Home Loan sits higher than both, so a household above HUD's "low income" line can still qualify.

## What is the MI Home Loan sales price limit?

MSHDA's sales price limit for the MI Home Loan is $566,355 statewide for loans after June 1, 2026. It caps the purchase price, not the loan amount, and it is the same in every county. Older limits still circulate online, including $544,233, the limit from May 1, 2025, and $224,500, the cap before that.

The jump came after a 2024 state law. MSHDA's [release on House Bill 5032](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/about/press-releases/2024/12/20/passage-of-house-bill-5032-paving-the-way-for-expanded-homeownership-opportunities-in-michigan) describes the change that let it raise the cap and tie it to federal price limits rather than a fixed dollar figure. [VERIFY: the indexing method, reported as 90% of the IRS maximum sales price, and the effective dates of the $544,233 and $224,500 limits, MSHDA lender bulletins]

For context, [Align Lending's Michigan Mortgage Market Report](/research/michigan-mortgage-market-report/) puts the median home value on 2025 purchase loans at $285,000 statewide, $385,000 in Oakland County and $435,000 in Washtenaw County (FFIEC HMDA data). The limit sits above all three, so it mainly rules out higher-priced homes, and it binds more often in Washtenaw and Oakland than in Genesee or Wayne. The loan type underneath also caps the loan amount; see [2026 loan limits by county](/loan-limits/).

## How does MI 10K down payment assistance work with the MI Home Loan?

MI 10K is MSHDA's down payment assistance: up to $10,000 toward the down payment and closing costs. It is a deferred second loan with no monthly payment, repaid when you sell, refinance, pay off the first mortgage or stop living in the home. It must be paired with an MI Home Loan, and homebuyer education is required.

Those terms are on MSHDA's [MI 10K DPA Loan page](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/mi-10k-dpa-loan). [COMPLIANCE REVIEW: MSHDA's page states MI 10K's interest terms; this draft leaves them out under F13] MI 10K has been open statewide since May 8, 2023. Before then it was limited to 236 ZIP codes, and MSHDA's statewide option was a $7,500 loan that it phased out at the same time, according to MSHDA's [May 12, 2023 release](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/about/press-releases/2023/05/12/mshda-expands-access-to-ten-thousand-dollar-down-payment-assistance-program). MSHDA publishes a [participating lender list](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/lender-list-for-mi-10k-loan) for the program. [VERIFY: whether MSHDA's MI Home Loan lender locator is a separate list]

### Is the $25,000 First-Generation down payment assistance still available?

No. MSHDA's [First-Generation Down Payment Assistance](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/firstgendpa) pilot is closed: its funds are exhausted and there is no waitlist. It launched in February 2025 with $8 million in state funding, according to MSHDA's [February 18, 2025 release](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/about/press-releases/2025/02/18/new-mshda-program-offers-$25000-in-down-payment-assistance-to-aspiring-first-generation-homebuyers), and offered a $25,000 deferred loan. It could not be combined with MI 10K. [VERIFY: re-check MSHDA's First-Generation DPA page on the publish date; if it has reopened, say so with the date]

### Can MI 10K be combined with other help?

Sometimes. Some city, county and nonprofit programs allow MSHDA help in the same purchase and set which lien comes first; others do not allow it at all. [VERIFY: MSHDA stacking rules with city programs, e.g. Detroit DPA lien order per the detroitmi.gov bulletin of 2026-08-12] Gift funds from a relative can also count toward cash to close when the loan type allows them. Align's guide to [Michigan first-time home buyer grants and programs](/learn/first-time-home-buyer-grants-and-programs/) lists the local programs.

## How do you apply for an MSHDA loan?

You apply through an MSHDA participating lender, not through MSHDA. The usual order is to check the income and price limits, take a homebuyer education class, get preapproved with a participating lender, shop at or under the price limit, and then let the lender reserve the loan and submit it to MSHDA.

1. **Check the limits.** Find your county and household size on MSHDA's chart, and confirm that the homes you want to see are priced at or under $566,355.
2. **Take the homebuyer education class.** MSHDA lists classes in its Housing Education Locator. Keep the certificate for the lender.
3. **Get preapproved with a participating lender.** Use MSHDA's lender list. The lender checks credit, income and assets against MSHDA's rules and the loan type's rules.
4. **Shop and write an offer under the limits.** Ask the lender how its MSHDA steps fit the dates in your purchase agreement.
5. **The lender reserves and submits the loan.** The lender reserves MSHDA funds, underwrites the file and submits it for MSHDA's review before closing. [VERIFY: MSHDA's reservation and review steps, MSHDA lender requirements page]

## When does an MSHDA loan not fit?

An MSHDA loan helps most when cash for the down payment is the barrier. For a buyer who already has the cash, it can cost more over time than an open-market loan. MI 10K comes due on a refinance or sale, and the income and price limits rule some buyers out. Compare Loan Estimates side by side.

## What Michigan costs should you plan for with an MSHDA loan?

Plan for the property tax reset. The year after a home sells, its taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid. In 2026 that reset raised a typical Michigan home's taxable value about 50.5%. File your Principal Residence Exemption (Form 2368) by June 1 or November 1.

The 50.5% figure is from [Align Lending's 2026 uncapping study](/research/michigan-property-tax-uncapping-study/), which measured all 83 counties from Michigan Treasury data. The reset itself is set by [MCL 211.27a](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-27a). Lenders qualify you on the full payment, taxes included, so an MSHDA buyer's budget has to carry the new owner's tax, not the seller's capped bill. Align's guide explains [how Michigan taxable value resets after a sale](/learn/michigan-taxable-value/).

> **Hypothetical example, not an offer. Actual rates, costs and payments vary.**
> A home bought for $250,000, with an assessor's market value near the price, has a state equalized value and a new taxable value of about $125,000 the year after the sale. At the 2025 statewide median homestead rate of 36.0 mills, the new owner's property tax is about $125,000 × 36.0 ÷ 1,000 = $4,500 a year, about 1.8% of the price. That is property tax only, not a loan payment, and your millage depends on the address.

The homestead rate assumes you have filed [Michigan's Principal Residence Exemption](/learn/homestead-exemption-benefits-and-filing-guide/). The seller's exemption does not carry over; file your own with the [Michigan Department of Treasury](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/principal-residence-exemption). To see your own number, [estimate your Michigan property tax after you buy](/michigan-property-tax-calculator/).

## Questions about MSHDA loans

### Who qualifies for a MSHDA loan?

First-time buyers anywhere in Michigan, and repeat buyers in MSHDA's targeted areas. For the MI Home Loan, household income must be at or under the county limit, the price at or under $566,355 (2026), and the credit score at least 640. You must live in the home, take a homebuyer education class and apply through a participating lender.

### Is MSHDA only for first-time home buyers?

No. First-time buyers, meaning anyone who has not owned a home in the previous three years, qualify statewide. Repeat buyers qualify too, but only for a home in one of MSHDA's targeted areas, which the income and sales price limits chart lists by county. All of Washtenaw County is targeted on the June 1, 2026 chart.

### How much is considered low income in Michigan?

It depends on the program and the county. HUD publishes income limits for every Michigan county each year, and its "low income" line is 80% of area median income. MSHDA uses its own, higher chart for the MI Home Loan: $104,800 for one or two persons in Oakland County outside its targeted cities (June 1, 2026).

### What is the $25,000 down payment assistance program in Michigan?

It was MSHDA's First-Generation Down Payment Assistance, a $25,000 deferred loan for first-generation buyers that launched in February 2025. It is closed: its funds are exhausted and there is no waitlist. MSHDA's statewide help today is MI 10K, up to $10,000 as a deferred loan paired with an MI Home Loan.

### What is the $7,500 first-time home buyer grant in Michigan?

It is usually an older MSHDA amount repeated online. MSHDA's statewide down payment loan was $7,500 until May 2023, when MSHDA phased it out and opened MI 10K statewide. Neither was a grant. Today's MI 10K is up to $10,000 and is a deferred loan you repay when you sell, refinance, pay off the first mortgage or move out.

### What is the difference between a grant and down payment assistance?

A grant is money you do not repay if you meet its conditions. Down payment assistance is the wider term: it can be a grant, a forgivable loan, a deferred loan or a repayable second loan. MSHDA's statewide help, MI 10K, is a deferred loan. Some city, county and nonprofit programs in Michigan offer grants or forgivable loans instead.

### Can you use an MSHDA loan with FHA, VA or USDA?

Yes. The MI Home Loan is underwritten as an FHA, VA, USDA Rural Development or conventional loan, so you meet that loan type's rules as well as MSHDA's. A USDA version, for example, still needs an eligible address and a household under USDA's income limit, in addition to MSHDA's own income and price limits.

### Does the state of Michigan offer help with mortgage payments?

Not through a statewide fund right now. The [Michigan Homeowner Assistance Fund](https://www.michigan.gov/mihaf), MSHDA's pandemic-era program, is closed because its funds are exhausted. If you are behind on payments or expect to be, call your servicer first, then contact a [HUD-approved housing counseling agency](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/find-a-housing-counselor/), which can review your options and local programs with you.
