# MSHDA MI Home Loan: who qualifies, the limits and how to apply in Michigan

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 16, 2026

**The short answer**

The MSHDA MI Home Loan is a fixed-rate first mortgage from the Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA) for first-time buyers anywhere in Michigan and repeat buyers in targeted areas. Three limits decide it: the 2026 sales price limit of $566,355, a county income limit and a 640 minimum credit score. You get it through an MSHDA participating lender.

**Key takeaways**

- MSHDA's sales price limit is $566,355 statewide for loans after June 1, 2026. Older figures online, such as $544,233, are out of date.
- Income limits vary by county, household size and targeted-area status: $104,800 for 1 to 2 persons in most of Oakland County (June 1, 2026 chart).
- MI 10K adds up to $10,000 for the down payment and closing costs as a deferred loan, not a grant, and only with an MI Home Loan.
- MSHDA's $25,000 First-Generation down payment assistance is closed: funds exhausted, no waitlist (November 2026).

## What is the MSHDA MI Home Loan?

The MI Home Loan is a fixed-rate first mortgage run by the Michigan State Housing Development Authority, a state agency. It is a loan you repay, not a grant. MSHDA participating lenders originate it, and each one is underwritten as an FHA, VA, USDA Rural Development or conventional loan, so that loan type's rules apply too.

MSHDA sets the buyer, income, price and credit rules on its [MI Home Loan page](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/mi-home-loan); the loan type sets mortgage insurance and property standards. [VERIFY: the four loan types on MSHDA's MI Home Loan page]

MI 10K, MSHDA's down payment help, is a separate second loan; Align's page on [Michigan down payment assistance options](/purchase/down-payment-assistance/) covers it with city, county and nonprofit help. MSHDA also offers a [Mortgage Credit Certificate](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/mortgage-credit-certificate-program), a federal tax credit of 20% of yearly mortgage interest, claimed on [IRS Form 8396](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8396). [VERIFY: MCC availability in 2026 and any annual cap]

### Is MSHDA a lender?

Not one you apply to. MSHDA funds the program and sets its rules; a participating lender takes the application, underwrites it and closes the loan. Lenders not on MSHDA's list cannot originate one.

## Who qualifies for a MSHDA loan?

First-time buyers qualify anywhere in Michigan, and repeat buyers qualify only in MSHDA's targeted areas. Household income must be at or under the county limit, the price at or under $566,355 (2026), and the credit score at least 640. You must live in the home as your principal residence and complete a homebuyer education class.

### What counts as a first-time buyer for MSHDA?

MSHDA counts you as a first-time buyer if you have not owned a home in the previous three years, so someone who sold a home five years ago can count again. [VERIFY: how MSHDA treats a co-borrower's past ownership]

### What is a targeted area?

A targeted area is a city, township or county where MSHDA accepts repeat buyers and sets higher income limits. On the June 1, 2026 chart, all of Washtenaw County is targeted, as are Pontiac, Royal Oak Township and Southfield in Oakland County.

## What is the MI Home Loan sales price limit?

MSHDA's sales price limit for the MI Home Loan is $566,355 statewide for loans after June 1, 2026. It caps the purchase price, not the loan amount, and it is the same in every county. Older limits still circulate online, including $544,233, the limit from May 1, 2025, and $224,500, the cap before that.

MSHDA raised the cap after a 2024 state law ([House Bill 5032 release](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/about/press-releases/2024/12/20/passage-of-house-bill-5032-paving-the-way-for-expanded-homeownership-opportunities-in-michigan)). [VERIFY: the indexing method, reported as 90% of the IRS maximum sales price, and the dates of the $544,233 and $224,500 limits]

[Align Lending's Michigan Mortgage Market Report](/research/michigan-mortgage-market-report/) puts the median home value on 2025 purchase loans at $285,000 statewide, $385,000 in Oakland County and $435,000 in Washtenaw County. The limit sits above all three, so it mainly rules out higher-priced homes, more often in Washtenaw and Oakland.

## How does MI 10K down payment assistance work with the MI Home Loan?

MI 10K is MSHDA's down payment assistance: up to $10,000 toward the down payment and closing costs. It is a deferred second loan with no monthly payment, repaid when you sell, refinance, pay off the first mortgage or stop living in the home. It must be paired with an MI Home Loan, and homebuyer education is required.

Those terms are on MSHDA's [MI 10K DPA Loan page](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/mi-10k-dpa-loan). [COMPLIANCE REVIEW: MSHDA's page states MI 10K's interest terms; this draft leaves them out under F13] MI 10K has been statewide since May 8, 2023, when MSHDA retired its $7,500 statewide loan ([May 12, 2023 release](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/about/press-releases/2023/05/12/mshda-expands-access-to-ten-thousand-dollar-down-payment-assistance-program)). MSHDA publishes a [participating lender list](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/lender-list-for-mi-10k-loan) for it. [VERIFY: whether MSHDA's MI Home Loan lender locator is a separate list]

### Is the $25,000 First-Generation down payment assistance still available?

No. MSHDA's [First-Generation Down Payment Assistance](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/firstgendpa) pilot is closed: its funds are exhausted and there is no waitlist. The $25,000 deferred loan launched in February 2025 with $8 million in state funding ([MSHDA release](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/about/press-releases/2025/02/18/new-mshda-program-offers-$25000-in-down-payment-assistance-to-aspiring-first-generation-homebuyers)). [VERIFY: re-check MSHDA's First-Generation DPA page on the publish date; if it has reopened, say so with the date]

### Can MI 10K be combined with other help?

Sometimes. Some city, county and nonprofit programs allow MSHDA help in the same purchase and set the lien order; others do not. ## How do you apply for an MSHDA loan?

You apply through an MSHDA participating lender, not through MSHDA. The usual order: check the income and price limits, take a homebuyer education class, get preapproved with a participating lender, shop at or under the price limit, and then the lender reserves the loan and submits it to MSHDA.

1. **Check the limits** for your county, household size and price range.
2. **Take the homebuyer education class.** MSHDA lists classes in its Housing Education Locator.
3. **Get preapproved with a participating lender** from MSHDA's lender list.
4. **Shop and write an offer under the limits.** Ask the lender how its MSHDA steps fit the dates in your purchase agreement.
5. **The lender reserves and submits the loan** to MSHDA for review before closing. [VERIFY: MSHDA's reservation and review steps]

## When does an MSHDA loan not fit?

An MSHDA loan helps most when cash for the down payment is the barrier. For a buyer who already has the cash, it can cost more over time than an open-market loan. MI 10K comes due on a refinance or sale, and the income and price limits rule some buyers out. Compare Loan Estimates side by side.

## What Michigan costs should you plan for with an MSHDA loan?

Plan for the property tax reset. The year after a home sells, its taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid. In 2026 that reset raised a typical Michigan home's taxable value about 50.5%. File your Principal Residence Exemption (Form 2368) by June 1 or November 1.

The 50.5% is from [Align Lending's 2026 uncapping study](/research/michigan-property-tax-uncapping-study/) (Michigan Treasury data, all 83 counties); [MCL 211.27a](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-27a) sets the reset. Lenders qualify you on the full payment, so budget for the new owner's tax, not the seller's. Here is [how Michigan taxable value resets after a sale](/learn/michigan-taxable-value/).

> **Hypothetical example, not an offer. Actual rates, costs and payments vary.**
> A home bought for $250,000, with an assessor's market value near the price, has a new taxable value of about $125,000 the year after the sale. At the 2025 statewide median homestead millage, 36.0 mills, the new owner's tax is about $125,000 × 36.0 ÷ 1,000 = $4,500 a year, about 1.8% of the price. That is tax only, not a loan payment; millage depends on the address.

The homestead millage assumes you file [Michigan's Principal Residence Exemption](/learn/homestead-exemption-benefits-and-filing-guide/); the seller's exemption does not carry over. File with the [Michigan Department of Treasury](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/principal-residence-exemption), then [estimate your Michigan property tax after you buy](/michigan-property-tax-calculator/).

## Questions about MSHDA loans

### Who qualifies for a MSHDA loan?

First-time buyers anywhere in Michigan and repeat buyers in targeted areas. Household income must be at or under the county limit, the price at or under $566,355 (2026) and the credit score at least 640. You also need homebuyer education and a participating lender.

### Is MSHDA only for first-time home buyers?

No. First-time buyers, meaning anyone who has not owned a home in the previous three years, qualify statewide. Repeat buyers qualify only in targeted areas, which MSHDA's limits chart lists by county; all of Washtenaw County is targeted (June 1, 2026).

### How much is considered low income in Michigan?

It depends on the program and county. HUD's [income limits](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/datasets/il.html) set "low income" at 80% of area median income, and Fannie Mae's [HomeReady rule](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b5-6-01/homeready-mortgage-loan-and-borrower-eligibility) uses the same line. MSHDA's MI Home Loan chart is higher: $104,800 for one or two persons in most of Oakland County (June 1, 2026).

### What is the $25,000 down payment assistance program in Michigan?

It was MSHDA's First-Generation Down Payment Assistance, a $25,000 deferred loan for first-generation buyers that launched in February 2025. It is closed: its funds are exhausted and there is no waitlist. MSHDA's statewide help today is MI 10K, up to $10,000, paired with an MI Home Loan.

### What is the $7,500 first-time home buyer grant in Michigan?

Usually an older MSHDA amount. MSHDA's statewide down payment loan was $7,500 until May 2023, when it was retired and MI 10K opened statewide. Neither was a grant. MI 10K is up to $10,000, repaid when you sell, refinance, pay off the first mortgage or move out.

### What is the difference between a grant and down payment assistance?

A grant is not repaid if you meet its conditions. Down payment assistance is the wider term: a grant, a forgivable loan, a deferred loan or a repayable second loan. MSHDA's MI 10K is a deferred loan; some Michigan city and nonprofit programs offer grants.

### Can you use an MSHDA loan with FHA, VA or USDA?

Yes. The MI Home Loan is underwritten as an FHA, VA, USDA Rural Development or conventional loan, so you meet that loan type's rules as well as MSHDA's. A USDA version still needs an eligible address and a household under USDA's income limit.

### Does the state of Michigan offer help with mortgage payments?

Not through a statewide fund right now. The [Michigan Homeowner Assistance Fund](https://www.michigan.gov/mihaf), MSHDA's pandemic-era program, is closed because its funds are exhausted. If you are behind on payments, call your servicer first, then a [HUD-approved housing counseling agency](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/find-a-housing-counselor/) to review your options.
