# VA home loan and property tax benefits for disabled veterans in Michigan

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 16, 2026

<div class="al-answer">

If you are a Michigan veteran with a service-connected disability, two benefits can stack when you buy. VA charges no funding fee to veterans receiving, or entitled to, disability compensation. Michigan exempts the home of a veteran rated 100% permanently and totally disabled or individually unemployable, or who received VA adapted housing help, from property tax. Both need paperwork.

</div>

<div class="al-takeaways">

**Key takeaways**

- **Funding fee:** VA charges no funding fee to veterans receiving compensation for a service-connected disability, or entitled to it, on purchase loans and VA refinances alike.
- **MCL 211.7b:** Michigan's exemption rests on one of three VA determinations: 100% permanent and total disability, individual unemployability, or a specially adapted housing grant. An unremarried surviving spouse keeps it.
- **Form 5107:** file it with your city or township assessor after January 1 and before December 31. Since 2025, a granted exemption stays in place without refiling.
- **Why it matters here:** without the exemption, the post-sale reset raised a typical Michigan home's taxable value about 50.5% in 2026, per [Align's 2026 Michigan Property Tax Uncapping Study](/research/michigan-property-tax-uncapping-study/).

</div>

## What VA home loan benefits do disabled veterans get?

Veterans with a service-connected disability get the same VA loan benefits as every eligible veteran, including no monthly mortgage insurance and no VA loan limit with full entitlement. Those receiving or entitled to disability compensation also pay no VA funding fee. Veterans with certain severe service-connected disabilities may also qualify for VA adapted housing grants.

The U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs sets each of these rules. VA guarantees part of the loan, and a private lender makes the loan and decides whether to approve it. For how a VA purchase works here, see [VA loans in Michigan](/product-loans/va-loans/). For the benefits every eligible borrower shares, see [VA loan benefits for every veteran](/learn/va-loan-benefits-for-veterans/).

| Benefit | Who gets it | Where VA sets it |
|---|---|---|
| No VA funding fee | Veterans receiving, or entitled to, compensation for a service-connected disability, plus the other groups in the next section | [VA funding fee and loan closing costs](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/funding-fee-and-closing-costs/) |
| Funding fee refund | Veterans whose disability award has an effective date before the loan closed | Same VA page |
| The same exemption on refinances | The same exempt groups, on a VA streamline refinance (IRRRL) or a VA cash-out refinance | Same VA page |
| No monthly mortgage insurance; no VA loan limit with full entitlement | Every eligible veteran | [VA home loan entitlement and limits](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/loan-limits/) |
| Specially Adapted Housing (SAH) and Special Housing Adaptation (SHA) grants | Veterans and service members with certain permanent and total service-connected disabilities | [Disability housing grants for Veterans](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/disability-housing-grants/) |

VA uses SAH and SHA grants to help a veteran buy, build or change a home to fit a disability. VA adjusts the maximum grant each fiscal year. For fiscal 2026 (October 2025 to September 2026), VA's [Federal Register notice for fiscal 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/18/2025-20047/loan-guaranty-assistance-to-eligible-individuals-in-acquiring-specially-adapted-housing) set the SAH maximum at $126,526 and the SHA maximum at $25,349. ## Who is exempt from the VA funding fee?

VA lists who is exempt: veterans receiving compensation for a service-connected disability, or entitled to it but receiving retirement or active-duty pay instead; surviving spouses receiving Dependency and Indemnity Compensation; service members with a proposed or memorandum rating before closing; and active-duty Purple Heart recipients. Your Certificate of Eligibility shows the exemption.

The funding fee is a one-time charge VA collects on most VA loans. The exemption applies to the fee on any VA-guaranteed loan, so it carries over if you later look at [refinancing a VA loan](/learn/benefits-of-refinancing-va-loan/). [VERIFY: VA funding fee page wording for the streamline and cash-out refinance exemption.]

Timing decides it. VA says that a service member who has a pre-discharge claim but no proposed or memorandum rating when the loan closes is not exempt. A claim still pending at closing generally means the fee is charged, with a refund possible later under the rule below. If you are not exempt, or you want to see what the fee would be, use the calculator: [Estimate my VA funding fee](/va-funding-fee-calculator/).

### Can you get the funding fee refunded if your rating comes later?

Possibly. VA says the test is the effective date of your disability award, not the date the decision reaches you. In VA's example, a loan that closes June 15 with an award effective June 1 may qualify for a refund, and an award effective July 1 generally would not. To ask, VA points veterans to their VA Regional Loan Center (877-827-3702) or their mortgage servicer, and VA pays refunds to the veteran directly, as VA News explains in [VA home loan funding fee refunds: Why timing matters](https://news.va.gov/150990/va-home-loan-funding-fee-refunds-timing-matters/). [VERIFY: refund rule, phone number and direct-payment rule against the VA funding fee page; record the VA News article's date.]

## Does Michigan exempt disabled veterans from property tax?

Yes. Under MCL 211.7b, the homestead of a qualifying disabled veteran is exempt from property taxes collected under Michigan's General Property Tax Act. Qualifying means a VA determination of 100% permanent and total disability, individual unemployability, or specially adapted housing assistance. An unremarried surviving spouse keeps it, even on a home bought later.

The law, [MCL 211.7b](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-7b), also requires a Michigan resident who was discharged under honorable conditions. The State Tax Commission reads that as an honorable discharge or a general discharge under honorable conditions. All three VA determinations must be for a service-connected disability. There is no income or asset test and no partial exemption: the home is fully exempt or not exempt. Michigan Treasury's [Disabled Veteran Exemption](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/exemptions/homeowner/disabled-veteran-exemption) page links the forms and guidance.

Two details catch people. A combined rating below 100% does not qualify by itself. Individual unemployability is a separate VA determination, which generally needs one disability rated 60% or more, or a combined 70% with one disability at 40% or more. And for a surviving spouse, Dependency and Indemnity Compensation alone does not qualify for the Michigan exemption, although it does exempt the spouse from the VA funding fee. The spouse qualifies only if the veteran did immediately before death, according to the State Tax Commission's [Disabled Veterans Exemption FAQ](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/-/media/Project/Websites/treasury/STC/2023/Disabled-Veterans-Exemption-FAQ-w-samples---Revised-1424.pdf) (updated June 13, 2024).

| Rule | What Michigan law and the State Tax Commission say |
|---|---|
| Who qualifies | A Michigan resident veteran, discharged under honorable conditions, with one of three VA determinations: 100% permanent and total, individually unemployable, or an SAH or SHA grant received |
| Surviving spouse | Keeps the exemption while unremarried, on any Michigan homestead the spouse owns |
| Proof | A letter or certificate from the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs; a county veterans office letter is not enough |
| Where to file | Form 5107 with the city or township assessor, not with Treasury |
| When to file | After January 1 and before December 31 of the year claimed |
| Refiling | Not required for an exemption granted for taxes levied on or after January 1, 2025 |
| Who must hold title | The veteran alone or with a spouse; the surviving spouse alone |
| What it covers | Property taxes under the General Property Tax Act; special assessments generally still apply |
| Income or asset test | None |

Source: MCL 211.7b; Michigan State Tax Commission, Bulletin 19 of 2023 and the Disabled Veterans Exemption FAQ (updated June 13, 2024); Form 5107 (Rev. 03-26).

### What does the exemption cover?

It covers property taxes collected under the General Property Tax Act on the home you own and live in, summer and winter bills alike. The State Tax Commission says special assessments are generally not property taxes, so they usually still apply unless the law behind the assessment exempts exempt property. It covers one homestead only. Contiguous vacant parcels do not qualify, and business use of the home other than farming disqualifies it.

### Does a land contract or a home held in a trust qualify?

A trust can qualify, depending on its form. The State Tax Commission says a trust with other current beneficiaries besides the veteran, the spouse or the unremarried surviving spouse does not qualify. Title matters for buyers too. The law requires legal title held by the veteran alone or jointly with a spouse, so putting a parent, an adult child or another co-buyer on the deed can cost the whole exemption. A life estate can qualify; a share in a housing cooperative cannot. The FAQ does not address land contracts, where the seller usually keeps legal title until payoff. [VERIFY: whether a land contract buyer meets the legal-title test in MCL 211.7b(4)(b); ask the State Tax Commission or the assessor.] Decide how you will take title before closing, and tell the title company.

## How do you file Michigan's disabled veterans exemption?

File Form 5107, the State Tax Commission Affidavit for Disabled Veterans Exemption, with the assessor of the city or township where the home sits, along with the VA letter showing your qualifying determination. File after January 1 and before December 31 of the year you claim. Since 2025, a granted exemption stays in effect without refiling.

The assessor grants or denies the claim. The March, July and December boards of review no longer hear new claims, though the July and December boards can correct certain qualified errors, including a delayed VA determination, according to the State Tax Commission's [Bulletin 19 of 2023](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/-/media/Project/Websites/treasury/STC/Bulletins/2023/Bulletin-19-of-2023---Disabled-Veterans-Exemption-Final.pdf). A December 30 postmark counts. For a home you just bought, the revised [Form 5107](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/-/media/Project/Websites/treasury/Forms/Local-Government/PSD/STC/5107.pdf) (Rev. 03-26) asks for your closing documents, because the first year's exemption is prorated from them.

1. Get your current VA benefit summary letter, or the VA certificate for an SAH or SHA grant, showing the qualifying determination.
2. Complete Form 5107 with the parcel number, the school district and the date you acquired the home.
3. File it with your city or township assessor, with the VA letter and a copy of your closing documents.
4. Keep copies of everything you file and the assessor's written decision.
5. Send the approval to your mortgage servicer and ask for a new escrow analysis.

If you sell, move out or stop qualifying, file Form 6054 with the assessor within 45 days. Local units audit granted exemptions, generally no more than once every three years.

## How do these benefits change your loan and budget in Michigan?

The funding fee exemption lowers your loan amount or cash to close, because there is no fee to finance or pay. The property tax exemption removes the tax line from your housing payment once the assessor grants it, which can change your debt-to-income ratio. Lenders may still escrow taxes until the exemption is on record.

[VERIFY: lender practice on escrow and on the debt-to-income ratio (DTI) before the assessor grants the exemption.]

The tax line is larger in Michigan than many buyers expect. The year after a home sells, its taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid. Align's Uncapping Study puts the statewide median homestead millage at 36.0 mills (2025 rates). Because taxable value resets to roughly half of market value, that works out to about 1.8% of market value a year after the reset. For the mechanics, see [how Michigan taxable value resets after a sale](/learn/michigan-taxable-value/).

**Hypothetical example, not an offer. Actual rates, costs and payments vary.** A veteran buys a Michigan home for $280,000, and the assessor's market value matches the price. The figures show property tax only, at the statewide median millage. They leave out the loan, insurance, special assessments and the first-year proration.

| Line | Without the exemption | With the exemption granted |
|---|---|---|
| Market value (assumed equal to the price) | $280,000 | $280,000 |
| Taxable value after the reset (half of market value) | about $140,000 | about $140,000 |
| Millage (statewide median, 2025) | 36.0 mills | Exempt |
| Property tax for a full year | about $5,040 | $0 |

Your own figure depends on the local millage and the assessor's value, so [estimate your Michigan property tax after you buy](/michigan-property-tax-calculator/) for the address. In the year you close, the exemption is prorated from your closing documents, and Michigan does not refund prior-year taxes you paid the seller at closing. If taxes were already paid through escrow, the State Tax Commission tells veterans to contact their mortgage company about the refund.

## Do disabled veterans still need to meet VA credit and income rules?

Yes. The benefits change the cost of the loan, not the approval test. VA disability compensation counts as income, and because it is tax-free, VA's handbook lets lenders gross up verified non-taxable income for the debt-to-income ratio. The loan still has to meet VA's residual income standard and the lender's approval.

VA's Lenders Handbook, [VA Pamphlet 26-7, chapter 4](https://www.knowva.ebenefits.va.gov/system/templates/selfservice/va_ssnew/help/customer/locale/en-US/portal/554400000001018/content/554400000330850/VA-Pamphlet-VAP26-7-Chapter-04-Credit-Underwriting), sets the income rules. Residual income, the money left each month after major expenses, is measured on actual income, not the grossed-up figure. [VERIFY: current chapter 4 text on grossing up and residual income, as applied to VA disability compensation.] Credit, assets, the appraisal and the property still go through the lender's review. For the full list, see [VA loan requirements](/learn/how-to-qualify-for-va-loan/). For the steps from offer to closing, see [how to buy a home with your VA benefit](/learn/buying-home-with-va-loan/).

## What if you do not qualify for the full Michigan exemption?

Veterans below the MCL 211.7b standard still get Michigan's Principal Residence Exemption like any owner who lives in the home, which exempts it from up to 18 mills of school operating tax. Veterans with a service-connected disability may also claim the homestead property tax credit on Form MI-1040CR-2, with a larger allowance at higher ratings.

File the Principal Residence Exemption affidavit with your assessor by June 1 for the summer levy or November 1 for the winter levy. See [Michigan's Principal Residence Exemption](/learn/homestead-exemption-benefits-and-filing-guide/) for the form and the rules. The credit is claimed with your Michigan income tax filing. For the 2025 tax year, Treasury's [MI-1040CR-2 booklet](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/-/media/Project/Websites/taxes/Forms/IIT/TY2025/MI-1040CR-2-Book.pdf) sets a taxable value allowance of $3,500 for ratings of 10% to 50%, $4,000 for 60% to 80% and $4,500 for 90% to 100%. Households with total household resources over $71,500, and homes with a taxable value over $165,400, cannot claim the 2025 credit. Treasury says to complete both the MI-1040CR and the MI-1040CR-2 and file the one that gives the larger credit.

## Where can Michigan veterans get help with the paperwork?

The Michigan Veterans Affairs Agency connects veterans with accredited Veteran Service Officers, who help with VA letters and benefit claims, through 1-800-MICH-VET or its county search. Your city or township assessor handles Form 5107. For a rating decision or an appeal, work with a VA-accredited representative.

The [Michigan Veterans Affairs Agency](https://www.michigan.gov/mvaa) (MVAA) runs the Michigan Veteran Resource Service Center at 1-800-642-4838, weekdays from 8 a.m. to 4:50 p.m. Its [Find a Veteran Service Officer](https://www.michigan.gov/mvaa/county-filter-search-locations) search lets you choose any Veteran Service Officer listed for your county, regardless of affiliation. ## Questions about VA benefits for disabled veterans in Michigan

### Who is exempt from paying the VA funding fee?

Veterans receiving VA compensation for a service-connected disability are exempt, as are veterans entitled to that compensation who receive retirement or active-duty pay instead. VA also exempts surviving spouses receiving Dependency and Indemnity Compensation, service members with a proposed or memorandum rating before closing, and active-duty Purple Heart recipients. The lender confirms the exemption on your Certificate of Eligibility.

### Do disabled veterans pay property tax in Michigan?

Not if they qualify under MCL 211.7b. A Michigan veteran whom the VA has rated 100% permanently and totally disabled or individually unemployable, or who has received a VA specially adapted housing grant, owes no property tax under the General Property Tax Act on the homestead once the assessor grants the exemption. Special assessments may still apply. Other veterans pay property tax like any owner.

### What disability rating do you need for the Michigan property tax exemption?

You need one of three VA determinations, all service-connected: permanent and total disability at the 100% rate, individual unemployability, or a certificate showing you received a specially adapted housing or special housing adaptation grant. A combined rating below 100% does not qualify by itself. No income or asset test applies, and the exemption is all or nothing.

### Do you have to file Form 5107 every year?

No, not since 2025. Under Michigan law, an exemption granted for taxes levied on or after January 1, 2025 stays in effect without a new application until you rescind it or the assessor denies it. File Form 6054 within 45 days if you sell, move out or stop qualifying. Your city or township may audit the exemption.

### Can a surviving spouse keep the Michigan disabled veterans exemption?

Yes, if the spouse does not remarry and the veteran qualified immediately before death. The exemption follows the surviving spouse to any Michigan homestead the spouse owns, including one bought later. Dependency and Indemnity Compensation alone does not qualify a spouse for the Michigan exemption, even though it does exempt a surviving spouse from the VA funding fee.

### Can you get a VA funding fee refund after a disability rating?

Sometimes. VA bases a refund on the effective date of your disability award. If that date falls before your loan closing date, you may qualify for a refund, even though the decision arrived later. If the effective date is after closing, you generally do not. Call your VA Regional Loan Center or your mortgage servicer to ask.

### Does a 100% disabled veteran need a down payment for a VA loan?

VA does not require a down payment from any eligible veteran, disabled or not, who has full entitlement and buys at or below the appraised value, according to VA's [entitlement and loan limits page](https://www.va.gov/housing-assistance/home-loans/loan-limits/). A lender may set its own requirements, and a low appraisal or partial entitlement can mean bringing cash to closing.
