# How DSCR loans work: the ratio, the rent and the Michigan tax math

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301
Updated November 16, 2026

For investors buying a 1–4 unit rental, a debt-service coverage ratio (DSCR) loan qualifies the property on its rent, not your income. The lender divides the rent by the full monthly housing cost: principal, interest, taxes, insurance and association dues. Many lenders look for 1.0 or more. In Michigan, a rental pays more property tax than an owner-occupied home.

`[VERIFY: "1.0 or more" describes common lender practice; no regulator sets a DSCR minimum]`

**Key takeaways**

- **The formula:** DSCR = gross monthly rent ÷ monthly PITIA (principal, interest, taxes, insurance and association dues). At 1.0 the rent exactly covers the housing cost.
- **What counts as rent:** the signed lease on a rented unit, or the appraiser's market rent estimate on a vacant one. Many lenders use the lower of the two.
- **Lenders set the minimum:** DSCR loans are written to each lender's own guidelines, not Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac rules, so the minimum ratio varies.
- **Michigan rentals pay non-homestead millage:** a rental gets no Principal Residence Exemption, so it pays up to 18 more mills of school operating tax, on a taxable value that resets the year after you buy.

## What is a DSCR loan?

A debt-service coverage ratio (DSCR) loan is a mortgage for investment property that qualifies on the property's rent compared with its housing cost, not on your tax returns or W-2s. Private lenders make DSCR loans under their own non-QM guidelines, not Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac rules. They are for rentals only, never a home you live in.

Non-QM is short for non-qualified mortgage. The industry uses it for loans written outside the federal qualified-mortgage standards and outside Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac rules. Federal Truth in Lending rules treat credit to buy or improve a rental that its owner does not live in as business-purpose credit ([12 CFR 1026.3(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/subpart-A/section-1026.3) and its official interpretation). That is a large part of why a DSCR lender can look at the property instead of your paycheck.

This guide covers the mechanics. For the program specifics, see [DSCR loan requirements in Michigan](/product-loans/dscr-loans-michigan/).

## How is the DSCR ratio calculated?

To calculate DSCR, divide the property's gross monthly rent by its full monthly housing cost, called PITIA: principal, interest, taxes, insurance and association dues. A ratio of 1.0 means the rent exactly covers the cost. Above 1.0, the rent covers it with room to spare. Below 1.0, the rent falls short of the cost.

> **DSCR = gross monthly rent ÷ monthly PITIA**
> PITIA = principal + interest + property taxes + insurance + association (HOA) dues

Some lenders, and most commercial lenders, run the ratio another way: annual [net operating income](/learn/what-is-net-operating-income-noi/) divided by annual debt service. That version subtracts operating costs from the rent first, so the same property shows a lower number. Ask which method a lender uses before you compare results.

## What DSCR do lenders require?

No regulation sets a minimum DSCR, so each lender sets its own. Many lenders look for a ratio of about 1.0 or higher, and some offer better pricing at about 1.20 to 1.25 and above. Some allow a ratio below 1.0 if you bring a larger down payment or more reserves. Guidelines change, so confirm the current figure.

`[VERIFY: no regulator publishes DSCR minimums; the figures describe common lender practice and match Align's DSCR page; confirm against current wholesale lender guidelines before publishing]`

A lender may also apply a higher minimum to some property types or loan sizes, such as short-term rentals `[VERIFY: lender matrices]`. A ratio just over the minimum leaves little room for error.

The ratio moves when its inputs move. A larger down payment lowers principal and interest, which raises the ratio. A lower insurance figure helps too. In Michigan, the tax line usually moves the other way after you buy.

## What counts as rent for a DSCR loan?

For a DSCR loan, lenders count the signed lease on a unit that is already rented, or the appraiser's market rent estimate on a vacant unit or a new purchase. Many lenders use the lower of the two when both exist. Some lenders accept short-term rental income, usually with a booking history or a market study.

`[VERIFY: lender practice on lease vs. market rent, "lower of the two" and short-term rental income]`

The appraiser's estimate usually comes on a comparable rent schedule. For a one-unit property that is Fannie Mae's Single-Family Comparable Rent Schedule (Form 1007). Appraisals of 2–4 unit properties use the Small Residential Income Property Appraisal Report (Form 1025). Fannie Mae's [rental income rules](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.1-08/rental-income) describe both. DSCR lenders often use the same forms, even though their loans are not Fannie Mae loans.

`[VERIFY: Selling Guide section number and URL (the site also links B3-3.8 rental income sections); selling-guide.fanniemae.com not reachable from the drafting session]`

In your own numbers, use the rent the unit will actually bring, not the highest listing nearby. Short-term rental income raises two questions: whether the lender counts it, and whether the city or township lets you operate. Read up on [short-term rental regulations](/learn/navigating-short-term-rental-regulations/) and [investing in short-term rentals](/learn/investing-in-short-term-rentals/) before you count nightly income.

## How do Michigan property taxes affect a DSCR loan?

A Michigan rental does not get the Principal Residence Exemption, so it pays the local school operating millage, up to 18 mills, that an owner-occupied home is exempt from. Its taxable value also resets the year after you buy to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value. Both raise the tax line in the ratio.

Michigan Treasury's [Principal Residence Exemption](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/principal-residence-exemption) page puts it this way: the exemption "exempts an owner’s principal residence from the local school operating millage, up to 18 mills." It covers only "the one place where an owner of the property has his or her true, fixed, and permanent home." A home you rent out pays the non-homestead rate. Treasury's [2025 millage rate list](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/estimator/related/millage-rates) shows both rates side by side for each township, city and school district. In most rows the gap is the full 18 mills; where a district levies less, it is smaller.

The second change is the reset. The year after a home sells, its taxable value resets to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid. [Align's 2026 Michigan Property Tax Uncapping Study](/research/michigan-property-tax-uncapping-study/) puts the typical jump in taxable value the year after a sale at 50.5%. The seller's tax bill tells you little about yours. Here is [how Michigan taxable value resets after a sale](/learn/michigan-taxable-value/).

## What else do DSCR lenders check?

Besides the ratio, DSCR lenders usually check your credit, cash for the down payment and closing costs, and reserves of several months of housing costs. They review the appraisal and its rent schedule, and some ask about your experience as a landlord. They also ask how you will hold title: in your own name or in an LLC.

`[VERIFY: typical lender checks; guidelines vary by lender]`

Each item is set by the lender, so two lenders can treat the same property differently. Expect the down payment to be larger than for a home you live in. Reserves are often counted for each financed property. Align's DSCR loan page, linked above, lists the current specifics.

## How does a DSCR loan compare with a conventional investment property loan?

A conventional investment property loan qualifies you on your personal income and tax returns under Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac rules, and usually costs less if you qualify. A DSCR loan skips personal income but usually costs more and may carry a prepayment penalty. A portfolio loan is kept by the lender, under its own rules.

| | DSCR loan | Conventional investment loan | Portfolio loan |
|---|---|---|---|
| How you qualify | The property's rent against its housing cost | Your income, debts and credit, with rent counted under agency rules | The lender's own standards |
| Documents | Lease or rent schedule, appraisal, assets, credit; no tax returns or W-2s | Tax returns, W-2s or other income records, assets, credit | What the lender asks for |
| Who sets the rules | The lender, under non-QM guidelines | Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, plus lender overlays | The lender that keeps the loan |
| Down payment | Larger than for a home you live in; set by the lender | Larger than for a home you live in; set by agency rules | Set by the lender |
| Prepayment penalty | Common | Uncommon | Depends on the note |
| Who it fits | Investors whose tax returns understate income, or who hold several financed properties | Borrowers whose documented income and debt-to-income ratio (DTI) support another mortgage | Unusual properties or files that fit neither of the other two |

*General comparison, not a commitment to lend. Each lender's guidelines differ and change.* `[VERIFY: prepayment penalty norms on each loan type]`

## What are the downsides of a DSCR loan?

A DSCR loan usually costs more than a conventional loan, many carry a prepayment penalty, and down payment and reserve requirements run larger. The biggest risk is a thin ratio: a vacancy, a rent cut or a tax reassessment can push it below 1.0. Run the numbers with conservative rent and the new owner's tax bill.

A prepayment penalty matters if you plan to sell or refinance in the first few years. Read how the penalty works in the note before you sign. If a later refinance is part of the plan, see [how to refinance an investment property](/learn/how-to-refinance-investment-property/). `[VERIFY: typical DSCR prepayment structures]`

A simple stress test is to rerun the ratio with lower rent. In the hypothetical example above, rent 10% lower ($1,800) drops the ratio from 1.17 to 1.06. If the property still clears the lender's minimum, it has some room.

## Do Michigan cities regulate rentals in ways that affect DSCR income?

Yes. Many Michigan cities require rental registration and periodic inspections before a unit can be rented, and some cities and townships limit short-term rentals through zoning or licensing. A unit that cannot legally be rented on your plan's terms cannot produce the income the ratio assumes, so check local rules before you count the rent.

Detroit, for example, requires rentals to be registered and to pass an inspection for a [Certificate of Compliance](https://detroitmi.gov/departments/buildings-safety-engineering-and-environmental-department-bseed/bseed-divisions/property-maintenance/tenant-rental-property/rental-certificate) from its Buildings, Safety Engineering and Environmental Department (BSEED). Grand Rapids runs a [rental certification program](https://www.grandrapidsmi.gov/departments/code-compliance/rental-properties/rental-certification-program/) for rental units. Ask the city or township what applies to the parcel, and how long an inspection takes to schedule, before you close.

## Questions about how DSCR loans work

### What is a good DSCR ratio?

It depends on the lender, but 1.0 is the usual floor, because at 1.0 the rent exactly covers the monthly housing cost. Many lenders price better at about 1.20 to 1.25 or higher. A ratio with room above the minimum also gives you a cushion for vacancy, repairs and a higher tax bill after the Michigan reset.

### Can you get a DSCR loan with no rental history?

Often, yes. On a vacant unit or a new purchase, lenders usually use the appraiser's estimate of market rent from a comparable rent schedule instead of a lease. Rental history matters more for short-term rental income, where many lenders want a booking record or a market study before they count nightly rent.

### Does a DSCR loan count toward your personal debt-to-income ratio?

It can. The DSCR loan itself is qualified on the property, but the mortgage still appears on your credit report. If you later apply for a loan that uses your personal income, that lender decides how to count the DSCR payment and the rent, under its own rules or agency rules.

`[VERIFY: typical treatment of a DSCR mortgage in a later personal-income loan]`

### Can you use a DSCR loan for a short-term rental?

Some lenders allow it, often with a higher minimum ratio and a booking history or a market study to support the income. Local rules matter as much as the lender's. Many Michigan cities and townships regulate short-term rentals through zoning, licensing or inspections, and a unit that cannot operate legally produces no qualifying income.

### Can you use a DSCR loan for a home you will live in?

No. A DSCR loan is for investment property only, and lenders usually ask you to sign a statement that you will not live in it. If you plan to live in one unit of a 2–4 unit building, look at loans for owner-occupied homes instead, which qualify you on your personal income.

`[VERIFY: occupancy or business-purpose statement is standard on DSCR loans]`

### Are DSCR loans only for experienced investors?

No, not always. Many lenders allow first-time investors, sometimes with conditions such as a larger down payment, more reserves or a higher minimum ratio. Some lenders limit first-time investors to certain property types or loan sizes. Ask about experience rules early, because they differ from lender to lender.
