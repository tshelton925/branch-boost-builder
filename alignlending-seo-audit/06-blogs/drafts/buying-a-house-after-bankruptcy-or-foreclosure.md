# Buying a house after bankruptcy or foreclosure: waiting periods by loan type

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 16, 2026

If a bankruptcy, foreclosure, short sale or deed in lieu is behind you, you can usually buy again after a set waiting period. It runs from about 12 months into a Chapter 13 plan for FHA and VA to 7 years after a foreclosure for a conventional loan. In Michigan, confirm your foreclosure's completion date first.

**Key takeaways**

- **FHA and VA have the shortest common waits:** generally about 2 years after a Chapter 7 discharge, and a purchase can be possible after 12 months of on-time Chapter 13 plan payments with court approval.
- **Conventional waits are the longest:** Fannie Mae generally requires 4 years after a Chapter 7 and 7 years after a foreclosure.
- **Extenuating circumstances can shorten most waits:** a documented, one-time event beyond your control can cut Fannie Mae's foreclosure wait from 7 years to 3.
- **Michigan's redemption period moves the date:** after a sheriff's sale, the most common redemption period is 6 months, and the completion date lenders use can fall at the end of it.

## How long after bankruptcy or foreclosure can you buy a house?

It depends on the event and the loan program. FHA and VA generally allow a purchase about two years after a Chapter 7 discharge, and after 12 months of on-time Chapter 13 plan payments with court approval. Fannie Mae's conventional rules generally require four years after Chapter 7 and seven years after a foreclosure.

Each period is a minimum, measured from a set date. Reaching it makes you eligible to apply, not approved: the lender still reviews your credit since the event, your income, your savings and the home. Lenders can also add their own stricter rules, called overlays.

## How long after Chapter 7 can you get a mortgage?

The Chapter 7 clock runs from the discharge date, not the filing date. FHA and VA generally look for about two years, USDA three years and Fannie Mae four years from discharge or dismissal. Documented extenuating circumstances can shorten each one, to as little as 12 months for FHA and two years for Fannie Mae.

HUD counts the two years at the date the lender assigns the FHA case number. VA is stricter about the first year: under its handbook, a discharge within the last 12 months generally rules out a finding of satisfactory credit. Between one and two years, VA looks for new credit paid as agreed and a cause beyond your control, such as a job loss or uninsured medical bills. USDA treats a Chapter 7 discharge in the past three years as a sign of unacceptable credit. `[VERIFY: USDA credit-exception path for a Chapter 7 discharged within 3 years]`

A foreclosure on the same mortgage changes the math. Fannie Mae applies the bankruptcy period only if the lender documents that the mortgage debt was discharged in the bankruptcy; otherwise it uses the longer of the two waits. USDA treats a foreclosure on a mortgage already wiped out by a Chapter 7 as an action against the property, not against you. VA starts its clock at the later of the discharge or the date title transferred.

If you already own a home and want to replace its loan, the rules differ, and Align's guide to [refinancing after bankruptcy](/learn/refinancing-after-bankruptcy/) covers them. For questions about the bankruptcy itself, talk with a bankruptcy attorney. This guide is not legal advice.

## Can you buy a house while in Chapter 13?

Yes, with FHA, VA or USDA, once 12 months of the plan have passed, every plan payment has been on time and the bankruptcy court or trustee approves the new mortgage in writing. Fannie Mae's conventional rules wait until two years after the discharge, or four years after a dismissal.

The written permission is the step people miss. The new mortgage is new debt, and a Chapter 13 plan usually needs the court's or trustee's approval before you take any on. Ask your bankruptcy attorney how your trustee handles that request and what it needs from you, such as the purchase price and the expected payment.

How the case ends also matters for a conventional loan. A discharge means the plan was completed, and Fannie Mae's wait is two years from that date, with no shorter period for extenuating circumstances. A dismissal means the plan ended early, and the wait is four years, or two with documented extenuating circumstances. More than one bankruptcy filing in the past seven years means five years from the most recent discharge or dismissal, or three with extenuating circumstances.

## How long after a foreclosure can you buy again?

Generally three years for FHA and USDA, about two years for VA and seven years for a Fannie Mae conventional loan. Each clock runs from the date the foreclosure was completed or title left your name. A deed in lieu or short sale carries a shorter conventional wait: four years, or two with extenuating circumstances.

Fannie Mae measures its seven years from the completion date reported on your credit report or in the foreclosure documents you provide. With documented extenuating circumstances, the wait drops to three years. Until seven years have passed, though, a loan can only be for a principal residence purchase or a limited cash-out refinance, and it carries a lower maximum loan-to-value ratio (LTV), which means a larger down payment.

HUD's rule says a borrower is generally not eligible for a new FHA-insured mortgage if a foreclosure or deed in lieu happened in the three years before the case number is assigned. The three years begin on the date title transferred from you. If the credit report does not show that date, the lender gets the deed or other legal documents. A file inside that window is also moved to manual underwriting, where an underwriter reviews it by hand.

USDA does not treat a foreclosure as adverse credit once it is more than 36 months before the application. Inside that window, a file needs a credit exception. VA's rules focus on whether you have re-established credit rather than a fixed countdown. `[VERIFY: VA foreclosure period, often described as 2 years, against VA Lenders Handbook ch. 4; FHA short-sale rule against current HUD 4000.1 (the 3-year rule found was in Mortgagee Letter 2009-52)]`

## When does the waiting period start after a Michigan foreclosure?

Most Michigan mortgages are foreclosed by advertisement, out of court, ending in a sheriff's sale and then a redemption period, six months in the most common case. Lenders count from the date the foreclosure was completed or title left your name, so get your sheriff's sale and deed dates from the county Register of Deeds.

The redemption period is your window to buy the home back after the sale. Under [MCL 600.3240](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-600-3240), it is six months for a home of one to four units when more than two-thirds of the original loan was still owed at the foreclosure notice. Otherwise the default is one year, and it can be as short as 30 days for an abandoned home. The Michigan State Housing Development Authority's (MSHDA) [guide to Michigan's foreclosure timeline](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/-/media/Project/Websites/mshda/homeownership/Counseling/Foreclosure-Timeline-212022.pdf) calls six months the most common period. `[VERIFY: MCL 600.3240 subsections read directly on legislature.mi.gov; this draft relied on search excerpts of that page because the host was blocked from the session. Also confirm the share of Michigan foreclosures done by advertisement.]`

That leaves two dates on the record. The sheriff's sale happens first. If you do not redeem, the buyer's sheriff's deed becomes final when the redemption period ends, under MCL 600.3236. `[VERIFY: MCL 600.3236]` Fannie Mae counts from the completion date reported on your credit report or foreclosure documents, and FHA counts from the date title transferred from you. In Michigan, those can be six months apart.

Hypothetical example, not an offer. Actual rates, costs and payments vary. A sheriff's sale on March 1 with a six-month redemption period ends on September 1. If a lender counts from September 1, a three-year FHA wait ends three years after that date, not three years after the sale.

Before you plan a purchase date, ask the lender which date it will use, and bring the sheriff's deed and the recorded dates. `[VERIFY: which date (sheriff's sale or end of redemption) lenders and agency guides treat as completion for a Michigan foreclosure by advertisement]`

### Does a Michigan property tax foreclosure count the same way?

It is a different process. A county treasurer, not a mortgage lender, forecloses for unpaid property taxes under the General Property Tax Act. [Michigan Treasury's timeline](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/forfeiture-foreclosure/county/foreclosure-process-timelines) shows unpaid taxes going delinquent on March 1, the property forfeiting a year later, and title vesting in the foreclosing government on March 31 of the year after that. The agency guides checked for this article do not say how a tax foreclosure is counted. Ask the lender before you assume either way, and bring the foreclosure judgment. `[VERIFY: Fannie Mae, HUD, VA and USDA treatment of a property tax foreclosure]`

## What counts as extenuating circumstances?

Fannie Mae defines extenuating circumstances as nonrecurring events beyond your control that caused a sudden, significant and prolonged drop in income or a catastrophic rise in financial obligations. Examples include a serious illness or the death of a wage earner. The lender must document the event and show you had no reasonable option other than default.

[Fannie Mae's Selling Guide B3-5.3-08](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.3-08/extenuating-circumstances-derogatory-credit) asks for three kinds of proof:

1. Documents that confirm the event, such as a divorce decree, medical reports or bills, or a layoff notice or severance papers.
2. Documents that show why you could not resolve it, such as insurance claim settlements, a listing agreement, a lease, or tax returns from before, during and after the loss of income.
3. Your written explanation, in a letter or an email, tying the documents to the event and showing that default was the only reasonable option.

FHA uses the same idea: an exception for a cause beyond your control, such as a serious illness or the death of a wage earner, once you have re-established good credit. Programs do not always agree on the details. A divorce decree is on Fannie Mae's list of supporting documents, but VA says divorce is generally not beyond the borrower's control. This is not a loophole. Lenders want to see that the cause is resolved and unlikely to happen again.

## What can you do during the waiting period to be ready?

Keep every payment on time, keep or add a few accounts in good standing, keep card balances low, and save for the down payment and reserves. Keep records of what caused the bankruptcy or foreclosure, and check all three credit reports for errors well before you apply, so the file is ready on day one.

1. **Collect your dates.** Keep the discharge or dismissal order, the sheriff's deed, and any short sale or deed in lieu closing papers.
2. **Check your credit reports.** Confirm the event shows the right dates and that discharged debts show a zero balance. You can get free reports at AnnualCreditReport.com. Disputes take time, so start early.
3. **Rebuild a record.** VA, for example, generally looks for about 12 months of satisfactory payments after the last problem. USDA treats even one rent or mortgage payment 30 or more days late in the last 12 months as significant. The [credit repair steps before you apply](/learn/credit-repair-strategies-for-home-buyers/) guide covers how.
4. **Save.** A down payment and reserves make a file stronger, and some programs require them.
5. **Know your score rules.** Fannie Mae's Desktop Underwriter no longer applies a fixed 620 minimum for case files created on or after November 16, 2025, and lenders may add overlays. See [what credit score you need](/learn/whats-good-credit-score-to-buy-house/) and the wider guide to [buying a home with bad credit](/learn/buying-home-with-bad-credit/).

## Can you get a mortgage sooner with a non-QM loan?

Sometimes. Non-QM loans follow each lender's own rules rather than Fannie Mae, FHA, VA or USDA guidelines, and some lenders accept a shorter time since a bankruptcy or foreclosure. The tradeoff is usually a larger down payment and higher costs, so compare it against waiting for an agency program.

Non-QM means the loan falls outside the federal qualified mortgage definition. The lender must still make a good-faith determination that you can repay it, under Regulation Z (12 CFR 1026.43). Because each lender writes its own guidelines, there is no single non-QM waiting period. `[VERIFY: Align to confirm the typical non-QM seasoning periods its wholesale lenders use, before any range is published]`

A shorter wait can cost more over the life of the loan. Weigh it against the date you become eligible for an agency program. Align's page on [non-QM loans in Michigan](/product-loans/non-qm-loans/) explains how these loans work.

## Does a loan modification or forbearance affect buying a home?

A completed loan modification or forbearance is not on the list of events with a bankruptcy-style waiting period in Fannie Mae's guide, but lenders still review your mortgage payment history after it. Late payments before or during the change can matter, and some programs look for a run of on-time payments before a new loan.

Fannie Mae's waiting-period section lists bankruptcy, foreclosure, deed in lieu, preforeclosure sale (a short sale) and a charged-off mortgage. A modification is not on that list. USDA's credit rules flag a housing payment 30 or more days late in the past 12 months, which is why the months around a hardship matter. `[VERIFY: HUD 4000.1 and VA ch. 4 treatment of a completed modification or forbearance; Fannie Mae's current post-forbearance eligibility rules]`

If you want to change the loan on a home you already own, see [refinancing after a loan modification](/learn/refinancing-after-loan-modification/) and [refinancing after forbearance](/learn/how-to-refinance-mortgage-in-forbearance/).

## Questions about buying after bankruptcy or foreclosure

### Can I buy a house 2 years after Chapter 7?

Often, with FHA or VA, which generally look for about two years from the discharge date. A Fannie Mae conventional loan generally needs four years, or two with documented extenuating circumstances, and USDA generally looks for three. Two years is the minimum, not an approval: the lender still reviews your credit since the discharge, your income and your savings.

### What is the FHA waiting period after foreclosure?

Three years. HUD's rule says a borrower is generally not eligible for a new FHA-insured mortgage if a foreclosure or deed in lieu happened in the three years before the case number is assigned, counted from the date title left the borrower's name. A lender can make an exception for documented extenuating circumstances when good credit has been re-established.

### Can you buy a house while in Chapter 13?

Yes, with some programs. FHA, VA and USDA can allow a purchase after 12 months of on-time plan payments if the bankruptcy court or trustee gives written permission for the new mortgage. Fannie Mae's conventional rules wait until two years after discharge. Ask your bankruptcy attorney how your trustee handles a request to take on new debt.

### Does a short sale have a waiting period?

Yes, for most programs. Fannie Mae's guide sets four years after a short sale, or two with documented extenuating circumstances. FHA generally sets three years if you were behind on the mortgage at the time of the sale, and USDA generally looks back three years. Check the rule for your program before you count on a date.

`[VERIFY: FHA and USDA short-sale rules against current HUD 4000.1 and HB-1-3555]`

### Is the waiting period counted from filing or discharge?

From the discharge or dismissal date for a bankruptcy, not the day you filed. For a foreclosure, the clock starts at completion: Fannie Mae uses the completion date on the credit report or your foreclosure papers, and FHA uses the date title transferred from you. In Michigan, that can be months after the sheriff's sale.

### Can you get a mortgage after bankruptcy with a low credit score?

It can be possible, but a low score narrows the choices. Each program and lender sets its own credit rules, and many lenders add overlays above the agency minimums. Fannie Mae's Desktop Underwriter has no fixed 620 floor for case files created on or after November 16, 2025. Rebuilding credit during the wait usually widens your options.

### Can you refinance after a loan modification?

Usually, yes, once the program's own seasoning rules and your payment history line up. Refinancing is a different question from buying, with its own waiting rules after a modification, forbearance or bankruptcy. Align's separate refinance guides cover those rules by program, so start there if you want to replace the loan on a home you already own.
