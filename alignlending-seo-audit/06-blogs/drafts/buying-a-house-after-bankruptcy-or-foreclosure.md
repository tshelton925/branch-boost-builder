# Buying a house after bankruptcy or foreclosure: waiting periods by loan type

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 16, 2026

If a bankruptcy, foreclosure, short sale or deed in lieu is behind you, you can usually buy again after a set waiting period. It runs from about 12 months into a Chapter 13 plan for FHA and VA to 7 years after a foreclosure for a conventional loan. In Michigan, confirm your foreclosure's completion date first.

**Key takeaways**

- **FHA and VA have the shortest common waits:** about 2 years after a Chapter 7 discharge, or 12 months into a Chapter 13 plan with court approval.
- **Conventional waits run longest:** Fannie Mae generally requires 4 years after Chapter 7 and 7 years after a foreclosure.
- **Extenuating circumstances can shorten them:** a documented, one-time event can cut Fannie Mae's foreclosure wait to 3 years.
- **Michigan's redemption period can move the date:** the most common period after a sheriff's sale is 6 months.

## How long after bankruptcy or foreclosure can you buy a house?

It depends on the event and the loan program. FHA and VA generally allow a purchase about two years after a Chapter 7 discharge, and after 12 months of on-time Chapter 13 plan payments with court approval. Fannie Mae's conventional rules generally require four years after Chapter 7 and seven years after a foreclosure.

Each period is a minimum. Reaching it makes you eligible to apply, not approved, and lenders can add stricter rules of their own, called overlays.

## How long after Chapter 7 can you get a mortgage?

The Chapter 7 clock runs from the discharge date, not the filing date. FHA and VA generally look for about two years, USDA three years and Fannie Mae four years from discharge or dismissal. Documented extenuating circumstances can shorten each one, to as little as 12 months for FHA and two years for Fannie Mae.

HUD counts FHA's two years at the date the lender assigns the case number. VA generally will not find satisfactory credit within 12 months of a discharge. Between one and two years, it looks for new credit paid as agreed and a cause beyond your control, such as a job loss. `[VERIFY: USDA credit-exception path inside 3 years]`

If the same mortgage was also foreclosed, Fannie Mae uses the longer of the two waits unless the lender documents that the bankruptcy discharged the mortgage debt. Replacing the loan on a home you already own is a different question: see [refinancing after bankruptcy](/learn/refinancing-after-bankruptcy/). For the bankruptcy itself, talk with a bankruptcy attorney.

## Can you buy a house while in Chapter 13?

Yes, with FHA, VA or USDA, once 12 months of the plan have passed, every plan payment has been on time and the bankruptcy court or trustee approves the new mortgage in writing. Fannie Mae's conventional rules wait until two years after the discharge, or four years after a dismissal.

The written permission is the step people miss. A mortgage is new debt, and a plan usually needs approval before you take any on, so ask your bankruptcy attorney how your trustee handles the request.

For Fannie Mae, extenuating circumstances can shorten the four-year wait after a dismissal to two years, but not the two-year wait after a discharge. Two or more filings in seven years mean five years from the latest discharge or dismissal, or three with extenuating circumstances.

## How long after a foreclosure can you buy again?

Generally three years for FHA and USDA, about two years for VA and seven years for a Fannie Mae conventional loan. Each clock runs from the date the foreclosure was completed or title left your name. A deed in lieu or short sale carries a shorter conventional wait: four years, or two with extenuating circumstances.

Fannie Mae counts from the completion date on your credit report or foreclosure documents. Between three and seven years, even with extenuating circumstances, the loan must be a principal residence purchase or a limited cash-out refinance, with a lower maximum loan-to-value ratio.

HUD counts FHA's three years from the date title transferred from you, and a file inside that window is underwritten by hand. USDA needs a credit exception for a foreclosure within 36 months of the application. `[VERIFY: VA's foreclosure period (2 years per VA training material) in Lenders Handbook ch. 4; FHA short-sale rule in current HUD 4000.1 (the 3-year rule found is from Mortgagee Letter 2009-52)]`

## When does the waiting period start after a Michigan foreclosure?

Most Michigan mortgages are foreclosed by advertisement, out of court, ending in a sheriff's sale and then a redemption period, six months in the most common case. Lenders count from the date the foreclosure was completed or title left your name, so get your sheriff's sale and deed dates from the county Register of Deeds.

Under [MCL 600.3240](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-600-3240), the redemption period is six months for a home of one to four units when more than two-thirds of the original loan was owed at the foreclosure notice. Otherwise it is generally one year, and as short as 30 days for an abandoned home. The Michigan State Housing Development Authority's (MSHDA) [foreclosure timeline guide](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/-/media/Project/Websites/mshda/homeownership/Counseling/Foreclosure-Timeline-212022.pdf) calls six months the most common.

If you do not redeem, the sheriff's deed becomes final when the period ends, so the sale date and the title date can be six months apart. Ask the lender which date it uses, and bring both. `[VERIFY: MCL 600.3240 subsections and MCL 600.3236 read directly on legislature.mi.gov (host blocked; this draft used search excerpts of that page); share of Michigan foreclosures by advertisement; which date lenders treat as completion in Michigan]`

### Does a Michigan property tax foreclosure count the same way?

It is a different process. A county treasurer forecloses for unpaid property taxes, and [Michigan Treasury's timeline](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/forfeiture-foreclosure/county/foreclosure-process-timelines) shows title vesting in the foreclosing government on March 31, about three years after the taxes went unpaid. The agency guides checked here do not say how lenders count it, so ask before you assume. `[VERIFY: Fannie Mae, HUD, VA and USDA treatment of a property tax foreclosure]`

## What counts as extenuating circumstances?

Fannie Mae defines extenuating circumstances as nonrecurring events beyond your control that caused a sudden, significant and prolonged drop in income or a catastrophic rise in financial obligations. Examples include a serious illness or the death of a wage earner. The lender must document the event and show you had no reasonable option other than default.

[Fannie Mae's Selling Guide B3-5.3-08](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.3-08/extenuating-circumstances-derogatory-credit) asks for papers that confirm the event, such as medical bills or a layoff notice, papers showing why you could not resolve it, such as tax returns from before and after, and your written explanation.

FHA allows a similar exception once you have re-established good credit. The programs differ on details: VA says divorce is generally not beyond the borrower's control. Lenders want the cause resolved and unlikely to recur.

## What can you do during the waiting period to be ready?

Keep every payment on time, keep or add a few accounts in good standing, keep card balances low, and save for the down payment and reserves. Keep records of what caused the bankruptcy or foreclosure, and check all three credit reports for errors well before you apply, so the file is ready on day one.

1. **Collect your dates:** the discharge or dismissal order, the sheriff's deed, and any short sale or deed in lieu papers.
2. **Check your reports:** confirm the dates and that discharged debts show a zero balance. Free reports are at AnnualCreditReport.com, and disputes take time.
3. **Rebuild a record:** VA generally looks for about 12 months of satisfactory payments after the last problem. See [credit repair steps before you apply](/learn/credit-repair-strategies-for-home-buyers/).
4. **Know the score rules:** Fannie Mae's Desktop Underwriter no longer applies a fixed 620 minimum for case files created on or after November 16, 2025, and lenders may add overlays. See [what credit score you need](/learn/whats-good-credit-score-to-buy-house/) and [buying a home with bad credit](/learn/buying-home-with-bad-credit/).

## Can you get a mortgage sooner with a non-QM loan?

Sometimes. Non-QM loans follow each lender's own rules rather than Fannie Mae, FHA, VA or USDA guidelines, and some lenders accept a shorter time since a bankruptcy or foreclosure. The tradeoff is usually a larger down payment and higher costs, so compare it against waiting for an agency program.

Non-QM loans fall outside the federal qualified mortgage definition, but the lender must still decide in good faith that you can repay (Regulation Z, 12 CFR 1026.43). There is no single non-QM waiting period. ## Does a loan modification or forbearance affect buying a home?

A completed loan modification or forbearance is not on the list of events with a bankruptcy-style waiting period in Fannie Mae's guide, but lenders still review your mortgage payment history after it. Late payments before or during the change can matter, and some programs look for a run of on-time payments before a new loan.

USDA, for example, flags a housing payment 30 or more days late in the past 12 months. To change the loan on a home you own, see [refinancing after a loan modification](/learn/refinancing-after-loan-modification/) and [refinancing after forbearance](/learn/how-to-refinance-mortgage-in-forbearance/). `[VERIFY: HUD 4000.1 and VA ch. 4 treatment of a completed modification or forbearance; Fannie Mae's current post-forbearance rules]`

## Questions about buying after bankruptcy or foreclosure

### Can I buy a house 2 years after Chapter 7?

Often, with FHA or VA, which generally look for about two years from the discharge date. A Fannie Mae conventional loan generally needs four years, or two with documented extenuating circumstances, and USDA three. Reaching two years makes you eligible to apply, not approved.

### What is the FHA waiting period after foreclosure?

Three years. HUD generally bars a new FHA-insured mortgage when a foreclosure or deed in lieu happened in the three years before the case number is assigned, counted from the date title left your name. Documented extenuating circumstances can allow an exception.

### Can you buy a house while in Chapter 13?

Yes, with FHA, VA or USDA, after 12 months of on-time plan payments and written permission from the bankruptcy court or trustee. Fannie Mae's conventional rules wait until two years after discharge. Ask your bankruptcy attorney how your trustee handles a request for new debt.

### Does a short sale have a waiting period?

Yes, for most programs. Fannie Mae sets four years, or two with documented extenuating circumstances. FHA generally sets three years if you were behind on the mortgage at the sale, and USDA generally looks back three years. Check your program's rule before counting on a date.

`[VERIFY: FHA and USDA short-sale rules against current HUD 4000.1 and HB-1-3555]`

### Is the waiting period counted from filing or discharge?

Bankruptcy waits run from the discharge or dismissal date, not the day you filed. Foreclosure waits run from completion: Fannie Mae uses the completion date on your credit report or papers, and FHA the date title left your name. In Michigan, that can be months after the sheriff's sale.

### Can you get a mortgage after bankruptcy with a low credit score?

It can be possible, but a low score narrows the choices. Each program and lender sets its own credit rules, often with overlays above agency minimums, and Fannie Mae's Desktop Underwriter has no fixed 620 floor for case files created on or after November 16, 2025.

### Can you refinance after a loan modification?

Usually, once the program's seasoning rules and your payment history line up. Refinancing has its own waiting rules after a modification, forbearance or bankruptcy, separate from buying. Align's refinance guides cover them by program, so start there to replace the loan on a home you own.
