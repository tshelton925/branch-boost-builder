# What disqualifies you from a home equity loan or HELOC?

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 16, 2026

**The short answer.** If you own a Michigan home and want to borrow against it, the usual deal-breakers are too little equity once the lender applies its combined loan-to-value cap, a debt-to-income ratio that is too high, and credit history. In 2025, Michigan lenders denied about 30% of HELOC applications, against 15.5% for purchase loans. Most of these can be checked before you apply.

**Key takeaways**

- Michigan lenders denied 30.1% of the home equity line of credit (HELOC) applications they decided in 2025 and 27.3% of closed-end home equity loan applications, against 15.5% for purchase loans ([Align's Michigan Mortgage Market Report](/research/michigan-mortgage-market-report/), 2025 HMDA data).
- Combined loan-to-value (CLTV), not the equity you see on paper, sets how large a line or loan can be. Each lender sets its own cap.
- Debt-to-income ratio and credit history were the two reasons lenders cited most on denied Michigan home equity applications in 2025. Collateral came third.
- A land contract, unpaid property taxes or a home listed for sale can stop a file no matter how strong the credit is.

## What disqualifies you from a home equity loan or HELOC?

Seven things stop most home equity applications: too little equity after the lender's combined loan-to-value cap, a high debt-to-income ratio, a low credit score or recent late payments, income that is unstable or hard to document, the property type or condition, title or lien problems, and a recent bankruptcy or foreclosure. Lenders weigh them together.

1. **[Not enough equity after the cap.](#how-much-equity-do-you-need-for-a-heloc)** Lenders lend against a share of the home's value, never all of it.
2. **[A high debt-to-income ratio.](#what-debt-to-income-ratio-do-you-need-for-a-home-equity-loan)** The new payment is added to the debts you already carry.
3. **[Credit score or recent late payments.](#what-credit-score-do-you-need-for-a-heloc-or-home-equity-loan)** A recent late mortgage payment can weigh more than the score itself.
4. **[Income that is unstable or hard to document.](#what-can-you-fix-before-you-apply-and-how-fast)** The lender has to see that you can repay, on paper.
5. **[The property type or condition.](#which-properties-and-ownership-situations-disqualify-a-home-equity-loan)** Some lenders exclude certain manufactured homes, investment properties or homes listed for sale.
6. **[Title or lien problems.](#which-properties-and-ownership-situations-disqualify-a-home-equity-loan)** A land contract, an unsettled estate, unpaid property taxes or a contractor's lien can block a new lien.
7. **[A recent bankruptcy or foreclosure.](#does-a-recent-bankruptcy-or-foreclosure-disqualify-you)** Lenders set waiting periods.

This guide covers who gets turned down and why. For how the products themselves work, see Align's pages on a [home equity line of credit in Michigan](/product-loans/heloc/) and [home equity loans in Michigan](/product-loans/home-equity-loan/).

## How often are home equity loans denied in Michigan?

In 2025, Michigan lenders denied 30.1% of the HELOC applications they decided and 27.3% of closed-end home equity loan applications, about twice the 15.5% share for purchase loans. Debt-to-income ratio and credit history were the reasons lenders cited most, with collateral third, according to Align's Michigan Mortgage Market Report (2025 HMDA data).

Why the gap? A purchase application usually follows a preapproval, so much of the sorting has already happened. A home equity application often does not. People apply to see what happens, and the first answer they get is the lender's. Demand stayed high anyway: Michigan homeowners took out 66,247 home equity loans and lines in 2025, with a median of $85,000 for a line and $55,000 for a loan. The report counts applications, not people, and one borrower can apply with more than one lender.

## How much equity do you need for a HELOC?

Enough to stay under the lender's combined loan-to-value cap. Combined loan-to-value (CLTV) is every mortgage on the home, the new line included, divided by the appraised value. Each lender and program sets its own cap, so the most you can borrow is the cap times the value, minus what you owe, not your full equity.

> **CLTV = (first mortgage balance + new line or loan) ÷ appraised value**
>
> **Largest line under the cap = (appraised value × the lender's CLTV cap) − what you already owe**

The CFPB's [home equity line of credit booklet](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_heloc-brochure.pdf) shows the same method with a 75% cap, and notes that the lender also looks at your ability to repay before it sets the final limit. Many lenders cap combined borrowing somewhere around 80% to 90% of value [VERIFY: typical CLTV caps for HELOCs and closed-end seconds; no GSE or regulator source located. If none is found, replace the range with "many lenders set caps above that" and no number]. Fannie Mae's Selling Guide uses the same [combined loan-to-value ratio](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-1.2-02/combined-loan-value-cltv-ratios) when a first mortgage has a second lien behind it.

**Hypothetical example, not an offer. Actual rates, costs and payments vary.** [COMPLIANCE REVIEW]

| Step | Figure |
|---|---|
| Appraised value | $300,000 |
| × the lender's CLTV cap (85%, for illustration) | $255,000 |
| − first mortgage balance | $180,000 |
| = largest line under the cap | $75,000 |

The same home at an 80% cap allows $60,000: $240,000 minus $180,000. The figures are the appraised value and the first mortgage balance only. They leave out closing costs and every other test the lender runs, so the result is a ceiling, not an amount anyone has offered. The owner has $120,000 of equity on paper and can borrow, at most, $75,000 of it.

### Can you borrow 100% of your home equity?

Rarely. A cap at 100% would leave the lender nothing to absorb a drop in value or the cost of a sale. Texas, where Align is also licensed, writes its own limit into the [Texas Constitution](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/CN/htm/CN.16.htm): all debt on a homestead, the new loan included, is held to 80% of fair market value. This guide covers Michigan.

### What if the appraisal comes in low?

The cap applies to the appraised value, not to an online estimate. A lower value shrinks the line, and it can end the application when what you owe is already near the cap. On smaller lines some lenders order a [drive-by appraisal](/learn/drive-by-appraisal/), which looks at the outside of the home only. Value matters after closing too. Regulation Z ([12 CFR 1026.40](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/subpart-E/section-1026.40)) lets a lender freeze or reduce an open line while the home's value is significantly below the appraised value the line was based on.

## What debt-to-income ratio do you need for a home equity loan?

Your debt-to-income ratio (DTI) is your monthly debt payments, the first mortgage and the new home equity payment included, divided by your gross monthly income. Limits vary by lender. DTI was one of the two reasons lenders cited most on denied Michigan home equity applications in 2025, so it is the number to check first.

The math is simple, and the details matter: which debts count, and how income is averaged. Align's guide to [how debt-to-income ratio is calculated](/learn/debt-to-income-ratio-dti/) walks through both.

A HELOC adds a twist. Some lenders figure the qualifying payment on the full line, not only on what you plan to draw, so a larger line can push your DTI over the limit even if you never use it [VERIFY: lender practice for HELOC qualifying payments; the CFPB booklet does not state a rule]. Asking for a smaller line is sometimes the whole fix.

For context, 43% is often quoted as a hard limit. It is not one. In 2025, 30% of the purchase loans Michigan lenders made had a DTI above 43%, most of them between 44% and 49%, and denials piled up at 50% or more (Align's report). Those are purchase-loan figures. A home equity lender may hold you to a tighter limit.

## What credit score do you need for a HELOC or home equity loan?

No federal rule sets a minimum credit score for a HELOC or a home equity loan. Each lender sets its own, and the minimum can change with the size of the loan and the CLTV. Recent late mortgage payments, collections or a fresh bankruptcy can weigh more than the score. Credit history was the other reason cited most in Michigan's 2025 denials.

Lenders read the whole report, not only the number at the top. The CFPB booklet lists credit history next to income and debts as what a lender weighs. A score that is fine for a small line at a low CLTV may not be enough for a larger one.

### Can you get a home equity loan with a 520 credit score?

Unlikely. At that score, many home equity lenders will decline the application whatever the equity. There are two realistic paths. Wait and rebuild, starting with [credit repair steps before you apply](/learn/credit-repair-strategies-for-home-buyers/). Or compare a cash-out refinance, which replaces your first mortgage and follows its own program's credit rules.

## Which properties and ownership situations disqualify a home equity loan?

Lenders usually decline or restrict homes that are listed for sale, some investment properties, certain manufactured homes, homes held in some trusts or still in an estate, and homes bought on a land contract, where the buyer holds equitable title and the seller keeps the deed. Unpaid property taxes and assistance liens add two more Michigan checks.

- **Land contracts.** On a Michigan land contract, the seller keeps the deed until the contract is paid off. Home equity lenders generally lend only to the owner of record, so the usual path is to refinance out of the contract first. See [how a Michigan land contract works](/learn/michigan-land-contract/).
- **Unpaid property taxes.** In Michigan, taxes still unpaid on March 1 of the year after they are levied are returned as delinquent to the county treasurer ([MCL 211.78a](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-78a)). After 12 months of delinquency, the property forfeits to the county treasurer on March 1 ([MCL 211.78g](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-78g)). If a foreclosure judgment enters and the taxes are not paid by the following March 31, title passes to the county or state and existing liens, mortgages included, are wiped out ([MCL 211.78k](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-78k)). That is why a lender wants delinquent taxes paid first. The [Michigan Department of Treasury](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/forfeiture-foreclosure) explains the process.
- **Down payment assistance liens.** The [MSHDA MI 10K DPA loan](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/pathway-to-housing/mi-10k-dpa-loan) is a second mortgage from the Michigan State Housing Development Authority (MSHDA), with repayment deferred until the first mortgage is paid off, the home is sold or refinanced, or it is no longer owner-occupied. A new line would sit behind it, and some lenders will not take that position. Whether MSHDA, or a city program, allows a new lien is set by its own documents [VERIFY: MSHDA and city DPA policy on new subordinate liens and subordination requests].
- **Contractor liens.** A contractor or supplier who was not paid can record a construction lien against the home. Expect the lender to want it paid and released first.
- **A home listed for sale.** For comparison, Fannie Mae requires a home listed for sale to be taken off the market by the disbursement date of a [cash-out refinance](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-1.3-03/cash-out-refinance-transactions). Home equity lenders set their own rules, and many decline a listed home outright.
- **Trusts and estates.** Lenders differ on which trusts they accept. A home still in a probate estate usually has to pass to the heirs before anyone can borrow against it [VERIFY: typical lender trust and estate eligibility].
- **Manufactured homes and investment properties.** Some lenders exclude manufactured homes, especially those titled as personal property, and some programs lend only on a primary residence. Align's report found purchase applications on manufactured homes were denied 57% of the time in 2025, against 8% for site-built homes.

## Does a recent bankruptcy or foreclosure disqualify you?

Usually, for a period of time. Lenders set waiting periods after a bankruptcy, foreclosure or short sale, measured from when it ended, and home equity lenders are often stricter than first-mortgage programs. A second lien is repaid only after the first mortgage if the home is sold, so the lender carries more risk.

For a comparison point, [Fannie Mae's Selling Guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-5.3-07/significant-derogatory-credit-events-waiting-periods-and-re-establishing-credit) sets a 4-year wait after a Chapter 7 bankruptcy for a conventional first mortgage, or 2 years with documented extenuating circumstances. A home equity lender can ask for longer.

## What can you fix before you apply, and how fast?

Pay down revolving balances to lower your DTI, correct errors on your credit reports, gather your income documents, clear any delinquent property taxes or title issues, and get a realistic value before you apply. Most of these show up in a first conversation, before any credit pull, so you can decide what to fix first.

| Problem | What to do | When it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| DTI too high | Pay down card balances; skip new debt; ask about a smaller line | After the creditor reports the lower balance, usually on the next statement cycle |
| Error on a credit report | Dispute it with the credit bureau and the creditor | After the correction posts |
| Recent late payment | Bring every account current and keep it current | The late stays on the report; its weight fades as it ages |
| Income hard to document | Gather tax returns, W-2s, pay stubs or award letters | As soon as the documents are complete |
| Delinquent property taxes | Pay the county treasurer | When the payment posts |
| Land contract, estate or contractor lien | Refinance out of the contract, settle the estate, or get the lien released | After the new deed or release is recorded with the county register of deeds |
| Value lower than expected | Use a realistic value; consider a smaller line | Before you apply |

## If you are turned down, what are your options?

Read the adverse action notice first. Under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act's Regulation B, a lender that turns down a completed application must tell you within 30 days and either state the specific reasons or tell you how to ask for them within 60 days. Then compare a smaller line, a cash-out refinance, waiting to rebuild, or a personal loan.

The rule is [12 CFR 1002.9](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1002/section-1002.9) [VERIFY: confirm the 1002.9 text is unchanged by the Regulation B amendments effective June 30, 2026]. The reasons tell you which fix comes first. A denial for DTI points to a smaller line or paying down debt. A denial for collateral points to the value or the cap.

- **A smaller line.** A lower amount can bring both DTI and CLTV inside the limits.
- **A cash-out refinance.** It replaces your first mortgage, so it follows different rules and costs. Compare [HELOC vs. home equity loan vs. cash-out refinance](/refinance/cash-out-vs-home-equity/).
- **Wait and rebuild.** Time helps with late payments, and paying down balances helps DTI.
- **A personal loan for a small amount.** It is unsecured, so the home is not at stake. See [personal loan or home equity](/learn/personal-loan-vs-home-equity/).

## Questions about home equity loan approval

### What will disqualify you from a home equity loan?

Usually one of three things: too little equity once the lender applies its combined loan-to-value cap, a debt-to-income ratio above the lender's limit, or credit history, such as recent late payments. Title problems, the property type, unpaid property taxes and a recent bankruptcy can also stop a file. In Michigan in 2025, debt-to-income and credit history were the reasons lenders cited most.

### Can you borrow 100% of your home equity?

Rarely. Lenders cap the combined balance of every loan on the home at a share of its appraised value, so part of your equity always stays unborrowed. The cap protects the lender if values fall or the home has to be sold. Each lender sets its own cap, and Texas sets an 80% limit on homestead borrowing in its constitution.

### What credit score do you need for a HELOC?

There is no federal minimum. Each lender sets its own score requirement, and it can change with the size of the line and the combined loan-to-value ratio. A higher score helps, but lenders also weigh recent late payments, collections and bankruptcies, which can matter more than the number itself.

### Can you get a home equity loan with a 520 credit score?

It is unlikely. With a score that low, many home equity lenders will decline the application whatever the equity, so the realistic options are to rebuild credit first or to compare a cash-out refinance, which follows its own program's credit rules. Correcting report errors and paying down card balances are the usual first steps.

### What is the downside of taking a home equity loan?

Your home secures the debt, so missed payments can lead to foreclosure. There are closing costs, most HELOCs carry a variable rate, and federal rules let a lender freeze or reduce a line in specific cases, such as a significant drop in the home's value or a material change in your finances. Weigh those against what the money is for.

### How long after buying a house can you get a HELOC?

It depends on the lender. Some lenders will open a line soon after closing as long as the combined loan-to-value fits their cap, and others want you to have owned the home for a set time first. Because a recent purchase leaves little equity unless you put a lot down, the cap is usually the real limit.

### Can you get a HELOC on a house you are buying on a land contract in Michigan?

Usually not until you hold the deed. On a Michigan land contract, the seller keeps the deed until the contract is paid off, and most home equity lenders lend only to the owner of record. The common path is to refinance out of the land contract into a mortgage first, then borrow against the equity later.
