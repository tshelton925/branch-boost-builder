# Rent-to-own, land contract or mortgage: three ways to buy a Michigan home

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 16, 2026

<div class="al-answer">

Rent-to-own makes you a tenant with an option to buy. A Michigan land contract makes you the buyer, paying the seller instead of a bank. A mortgage makes you the owner, with a lender's lien. For a buyer weighing a seller's offer, three things differ most: who holds title, what a missed payment costs, and when your property tax resets.

</div>

<div class="al-takeaways">

**Key takeaways**

- **Title:** with rent-to-own, the owner keeps the deed and you hold a lease and an option. With a land contract, you hold equitable title and the seller keeps the deed until payoff. With a mortgage, the deed is yours at closing.
- **Default:** a Michigan tenant can face eviction 7 days after a written demand for unpaid rent. A land contract buyer gets 15 days after a forfeiture notice, then 90 days or 6 months after a judgment. A mortgage borrower usually gets 6 months or 1 year to redeem after the sheriff's sale.
- **Property tax:** a land contract uncaps taxable value for the year after it is signed. Rent-to-own usually uncaps it only when the deed transfers, unless the lease has a "bargain purchase option". A purchase with a mortgage uncaps it the year after the sale.
- **The way to a mortgage:** Fannie Mae counts a rent credit only up to the difference between the rent you paid and the appraiser's market rent. Paying off a land contract is often financed as a refinance.

</div>

## What is the difference between rent-to-own, a land contract and a mortgage?

With rent-to-own, the owner keeps title, you pay rent to a landlord, and a missed payment can mean eviction and losing your option money. With a land contract, the seller keeps legal title, you pay the seller, and default can end in forfeiture. With a mortgage, you hold title, you pay a lender, and default can end in foreclosure.

The table sets the three side by side. Each cell is the usual Michigan rule. Your contract can change some of them, which is one more reason to have it reviewed before you sign.

| | Rent-to-own (lease-option) | Land contract | Mortgage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who holds legal title | The owner, until you exercise the option and close | The seller, until the contract is paid off | You, from closing |
| Who holds equitable title | The owner. You hold a lease and an option | You, the buyer (vendee) | You. The lender holds a lien |
| Who you pay | The landlord: rent, plus an option fee | The seller, under the contract | The lender or its servicer |
| Who pays property tax | The owner, though the lease may pass costs to you | Whoever the contract names, usually you | You, often through an escrow account |
| Can you claim the Principal Residence Exemption? | No, not while you are a tenant | Yes, under a written land contract | Yes, if it is your principal residence |
| When taxable value uncaps | When the deed transfers, unless the lease has a bargain purchase option | The year after the contract is signed | The year after the sale |
| What you can lose on default | The home, after an eviction, plus the option fee and rent credits if the contract says so | The home and, usually, the payments made, after forfeiture or foreclosure | The home, after foreclosure and the redemption period |
| How you get to a mortgage | Exercise the option and buy with a purchase loan | Pay off the contract, often with a refinance | You already have one |
| Recorded with the county Register of Deeds? | Ask your attorney whether to record a memorandum of the option | Not required, but recording the contract or a memorandum is recommended | The deed and the mortgage are recorded at closing |

*Sources: MCL 211.27a, 211.7dd, 600.5714, 600.5728, 600.5744 and 600.3240; Michigan Department of Treasury. Your contract decides who pays costs such as taxes, insurance and repairs.*

## How does rent-to-own work in Michigan?

Rent-to-own is a lease with a right to buy the home at a set price by a set date. You usually pay an option fee up front, and part of each rent payment may be credited toward the price. The fee is often nonrefundable, so not buying usually means losing it. A lease-purchase version can legally obligate you to buy.

Two different contracts sit under the same label. A lease-option lets you walk away at the end; a lease-purchase commits you to buy, and the [Federal Trade Commission warns](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2016/11/what-you-need-know-about-rent-own-home-deals) that breaking it can lead to legal action by the seller. Our guide to [lease to own vs. rent to own](/learn/lease-to-own-vs-rent-to-own-whats/) explains the two contracts and their typical terms.

Two Michigan rules follow from being a tenant. Until you exercise the option, the [Michigan Department of Treasury](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/property/principal/principal-residence-exemption-pre-guidelines-and-faqs/ownership-requirement) treats a lease with an option to buy as a rental agreement, not a land contract. And because the lease is a residential rental agreement, the [Truth in Renting Act](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-554-633) bars clauses that waive the landlord's repair duties under Michigan's fitness and habitability rules or your right to notice and procedure in court.

## How does a land contract work in Michigan?

A land contract is a home sale the seller finances. You pay the seller in installments, usually with a balloon payment of the remaining balance due on a set date. You move in and usually pay the taxes and insurance, while the seller keeps legal title until the contract is paid off, often with a new mortgage.

Michigan's tax law counts a person "purchasing property under a land contract" as an owner ([MCL 211.7dd](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-7dd)), which is why a land contract buyer can claim the Principal Residence Exemption and a tenant cannot. Treasury expects the contract to be in writing and signed by the seller. For each stage from signing to the deed, see [how a Michigan land contract works](/learn/michigan-land-contract/). A land contract is one kind of seller financing, and [seller financing pros and cons](/learn/pros-and-cons-of-seller-financing/) covers the wider picture.

## What happens if you miss a payment on each one?

A rent-to-own tenant can be evicted and lose the option fee and rent credits. A land contract buyer faces forfeiture or foreclosure, with 90 days or 6 months to catch up after a forfeiture judgment. A mortgage borrower faces foreclosure, usually by advertisement, followed by a redemption period of 6 months or 1 year for most homes.

**Rent-to-own.** A landlord can start an eviction case 7 days after serving a written demand for unpaid rent ([MCL 600.5714](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-600-5714)). After a judgment, the court generally waits 10 days before ordering you out, and paying the judgment amount and costs in that time stops the order ([MCL 600.5744](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-600-5744)). Whether you keep any option fee or rent credits depends on the contract.

**Land contract.** Forfeiture is available only if the contract provides for it ([MCL 600.5726](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-600-5726)). The seller serves a written notice of forfeiture, and you have 15 days to pay or cure, unless the contract allows longer ([MCL 600.5728](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-600-5728)). If the seller wins a judgment, the order to leave waits 90 days if you have paid less than half the price, or 6 months if you have paid half or more. Pay the judgment amount and cure other breaches in that window and the contract continues. If you do not, the order ends your right to redeem, and the payments you made are usually lost. The seller can instead foreclose through the circuit court, a longer process.

**Mortgage.** After a foreclosure sale, you can redeem the home by paying the sale amount. For a home of up to 4 units, the redemption period is 6 months if more than two-thirds of the original loan was still owed when foreclosure began and 1 year in most other cases. It can be as short as 30 days if the home is abandoned ([MCL 600.3240](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-600-3240)).

## How do property taxes work under each option in Michigan?

Under a land contract, the buyer usually pays the taxes, and signing the contract uncaps taxable value the next year. Under rent-to-own, the owner pays, and taxes usually uncap when the deed transfers. With a mortgage, taxable value resets the year after the sale to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price.

Michigan treats a land contract as a transfer of ownership when it is signed. Taxable value resets for the calendar year after the contract is entered into, and it does not reset again when the deed is recorded at payoff ([MCL 211.27a](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-27a)). One rent-to-own detail matters too. The same law counts a lease as a transfer of ownership if it runs more than 35 years with renewals, or if it gives you a "bargain purchase option": a right to buy at the end of the lease for no more than 80% of the home's projected true cash value. Ask your attorney to check the option price against that test.

The reset is large. In 2026 it raised a typical Michigan home's taxable value about 50.5% the year after the sale, according to [Align Lending's 2026 Michigan Property Tax Uncapping Study](/research/michigan-property-tax-uncapping-study/).

After a land contract or a purchase, the buyer files a Property Transfer Affidavit with the local assessor within 45 days. Land contract buyers and owners can then claim the Principal Residence Exemption, filed by June 1 for the summer levy or November 1 for the winter levy. For the math behind the reset, see [how Michigan taxable value resets after a sale](/learn/michigan-taxable-value/).

## Do rent credits or land contract payments count toward a mortgage?

Rent credits count only under the lender's rules. Fannie Mae limits a credit to the difference between the rent you paid and the market rent the appraiser sets, and it needs the written lease-option agreement. Land contract payments build equity you can use when you refinance, and lenders ask for the contract and proof of every payment.

Under Fannie Mae's [Selling Guide B3-4.3-12](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-4.3-12/rent-related-credits), the lease must have an original term of at least 12 months and state the monthly rent and the credit, and the appraisal must show the market rent. Since an August 2024 update, more than 12 months of credit can count toward the down payment. FHA sets its own rent-credit rule in [HUD Handbook 4000.1](https://www.hud.gov/hud-partners/single-family-handbook-4000-1). A seller's promise that "your rent goes toward the house" does not bind any lender.

For land contracts, Fannie Mae treats paying off a contract signed more than 12 months before the loan application as a limited cash-out refinance, valued on a new appraisal ([Selling Guide B2-1.3-05](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-1.3-05/payoff-installment-land-contract-requirements)). Your equity is roughly the appraised value minus the payoff. Other programs set their own rules.

## How do you move from rent-to-own or a land contract to a mortgage?

Start a lender review well before the option deadline or the balloon date. The lender looks at your credit, income, payment history and an appraisal of the home. A land contract payoff may be financed as a purchase or a refinance, depending on the loan program and how long ago the contract was signed.

1. **Find your real deadline.** Locate the option expiration date or the balloon date in the signed contract.
2. **Check your readiness well before it.** That leaves time to work on credit, income documents or savings. If credit is the obstacle, start with the [credit repair steps before you apply](/learn/credit-repair-strategies-for-home-buyers/).
3. **Build the paper trail.** Keep the signed contract, proof of every payment, and the seller's written statement of rent credits or the payoff amount. <!-- EDITOR: once /learn/how-to-get-pre-approved-for-a-mortgage/ is live, add the anchor "how to get preapproved for a mortgage" here. -->
4. **Apply, then order the appraisal and title work** with enough time to close before the deadline.

## What should you ask before you sign a rent-to-own or land contract deal?

Have a Michigan real estate attorney review the agreement before you sign. Confirm the seller owns the home and whether a mortgage on it could be called due. Record the land contract or a memorandum of it. Get the price, any credits, the balloon or option date and the default terms in writing.

1. Does a title search show the seller owns the home, free of liens and other claims?
2. Does the seller still have a mortgage, and could this deal let that lender demand payment in full? `[VERIFY: due-on-sale rule, 12 U.S.C. 1701j-3]`
3. Will the land contract, or a memorandum of it, be recorded with the county Register of Deeds?
4. Who pays for repairs, including major ones such as a roof or a furnace?
5. Who insures the home, and is your interest named on the policy?
6. When is the balloon payment or the option deadline, and what happens if you cannot get a mortgage by then?
7. Is the purchase price fixed now, or set later?
8. How will the seller document option fees and rent credits, in a form a lender will accept?

The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau has warned](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/consumer-advisory-help-is-available-for-people-facing-housing-problems-because-of-a-contract-for-deed/) that contract-for-deed buyers can carry the costs of owning a home, such as taxes, insurance and repairs, without holding the deed. The FTC also warns of scammers who advertise a home they do not own as rent-to-own, so check the owner's name against county property records before you pay anything. Michigan Legal Help, a free legal-information site, publishes a guide to leases with an option to buy.

## Is renting, rent-to-own or a land contract cheaper than buying with a mortgage?

It depends on the price, the terms and how long you stay. Compare the full cost of each path, including option fees, the land contract's interest, taxes, insurance and repairs, against a mortgage you could qualify for. Use your own numbers, because a figure from someone else's deal tells you little about yours.

Three costs rarely show up in a monthly figure: an option fee you may not get back, a price set today for a sale years away, and the cost of refinancing by the balloon date. ## Questions about rent-to-own and land contracts in Michigan

### Is a land contract better than rent-to-own?

It depends on what you need. A land contract makes you the buyer from the start: you hold equitable title, can claim the Principal Residence Exemption and build equity, but you take on taxes, repairs and a balloon date. Rent-to-own keeps you a tenant, and a lease-option lets you walk away, usually at the cost of the option fee. Have a Michigan real estate attorney compare the actual contracts.

### Who pays property taxes on a land contract in Michigan?

Usually the buyer, because the contract says so. Taxable value uncaps for the calendar year after the contract is signed, not when the deed is recorded at payoff. The buyer should file a Property Transfer Affidavit within 45 days and can claim the Principal Residence Exemption. The contract itself decides who sends the payment to the treasurer.

### Can you claim the homestead exemption on a land contract in Michigan?

Yes. Michigan's tax law counts a person purchasing property under a land contract as an owner for the Principal Residence Exemption, often called the homestead exemption. The contract should be in writing and signed by the seller. File the affidavit with your local assessor by June 1 for the summer levy or November 1 for the winter levy.

### Do rent-to-own payments count toward a down payment?

Only part of them, and only under the lender's rules. Fannie Mae counts no more than the difference between the rent you paid and the market rent the appraiser sets, and it needs a written lease-option agreement with an original term of at least 12 months. The rest of your payment is rent. Ask a lender to review the agreement before you sign it.

### What happens at the end of a land contract if you cannot get a mortgage?

The balloon payment is still due. You can ask the seller for a written extension, sell the home and pay off the contract, or keep working toward a loan. If you miss the balloon, the seller may start forfeiture, which gives you 15 days after the notice and then 90 days or 6 months after a judgment to pay what is owed.

### Is rent-to-own a good idea with bad credit?

It can buy time, but it costs money if the plan fails. The option fee and rent credits can be lost if you cannot get a mortgage by the deadline. Before you sign, find out how far your credit actually is from a mortgage. A readiness review shows what to work on first and roughly how long it may take.

### Do you need a lawyer for a land contract in Michigan?

Yes, have one review it before you sign. A Michigan real estate attorney can check the title, the forfeiture and balloon terms, and the seller's existing mortgage, and can record the contract or a memorandum of it.
