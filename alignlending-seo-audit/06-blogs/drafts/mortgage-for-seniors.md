# Can you get a mortgage at 60, 70 or 80? How lenders treat retirement income

Written by Samantha Shelton NMLS 1647301 · Updated November 16, 2026

<div class="al-answer">

Yes. Federal law bars lenders from turning you down or cutting your loan term because of your age, so at 60, 70 or 80 you can take a 30-year mortgage if your income and credit qualify. Lenders count Social Security, pensions, retirement withdrawals and, in some programs, savings. Michigan movers should budget for the property tax reset.

</div>

<div class="al-takeaways">

**Key takeaways**

- The Equal Credit Opportunity Act and Regulation B bar lenders from using your age against you.
- Fannie Mae counts retirement account withdrawals that can continue for at least 3 years from the note date.
- The loan term is your choice at any age. No rule ties it to life expectancy.
- In Michigan, taxable value jumped a typical 50.5% the year after a sale in 2026.

</div>

`[VERIFY: takeaways 1 and 2 rest on 12 CFR 1002.6 and Fannie Mae B3-3.4-03, flagged in the sections below; 50.5% is from Align's 2026 Uncapping Study]`

## Can a lender deny you a mortgage because of your age?

No. The Equal Credit Opportunity Act and its rule, Regulation B, bar lenders from discriminating because of age, so a lender cannot turn you down or limit your loan term because you are older. Age can be considered only in narrow, permitted ways. The lender still checks your income, credit and the property, as it would for anyone.

The narrow uses: a statistically tested scoring system may use age but cannot score an applicant 62 or older lower for it, and any system may use age to favor that applicant. `[VERIFY: 12 CFR 1002.6(b)(2)(ii) and (iv); 1002.2(o), CFPB]`

A lender also may not discount income because it is a pension, an annuity or another retirement benefit, though it may weigh how long it will last. `[VERIFY: 12 CFR 1002.6(b)(5)]` Read the [CFPB's text of Regulation B, section 1002.6](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/6/). If you think a lender treated you differently because of age, [submit a complaint to the CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/).

## Can you get a 30-year mortgage at 70 or 75?

Yes. The loan term is your choice, not a function of your age. Some older borrowers choose a shorter term to finish paying sooner. Others prefer the lower required payment of a longer term and plan to pay extra or sell later. Both are allowed, and the lender qualifies you on the same income and credit rules.

A shorter term usually means a larger required payment and less total interest. A longer term keeps more cash free each month. The guide to the [15-year vs. 30-year mortgage](/learn/15-year-vs-30-year-mortgage-comparison/) compares the two. `[COMPLIANCE REVIEW: D7 term names]`

If you will sell one home and buy another, the order of the two closings shapes the loan, so [plan a buy-before-you-sell move](/purchase/buy-before-you-sell/) before you make an offer.

## How do lenders count Social Security and retirement income?

Lenders count Social Security retirement, pensions and annuities when you document them with an award letter, statements or tax returns. Withdrawals from retirement accounts count if they can continue for at least three years. Non-taxable income, such as part of your Social Security, may be grossed up, meaning increased for qualifying under each program's rules.

`[VERIFY: gross-up methods, Fannie Mae B3-3.1-01 and HUD 4000.1 II.A.4]`

| Income type | What lenders usually ask for | Continuance or special rule |
|---|---|---|
| Social Security retirement | Award letter or tax returns, plus proof of receipt | On your own work record, no extra proof it will continue; on another person's record, 3 years |
| Social Security disability (SSDI) | Award letter or tax returns | Same as retirement benefits |
| Pension | Award letter or pension statement | Lender checks for an end date |
| Annuity | Contract or statement | At least 3 years from the note date |
| IRA or 401(k) withdrawals | Account statements | At least 3 years from the note date |
| Interest and dividends | Tax returns or statements | The assets must remain to keep paying |
| Rental income | Leases and tax returns | Program rules set how much counts |

Sources: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-3.4-03, B3-3.4-15 and B3-3.1-01; HUD Handbook 4000.1 for FHA loans. `[VERIFY each row against Fannie Mae B3-3.4-03, B3-3.4-08, B3-3.4-15 and HUD 4000.1]`

Disability income is covered in the guide to buying a home on SSI or disability income. `[LINK WHEN LIVE: /learn/buying-a-home-on-ssi-or-disability-income/]` To turn your figures into a price range, try the [home affordability calculator](/home-affordability-calculator/).

## Can you qualify for a mortgage using your savings instead of income?

Sometimes. Fannie Mae lets lenders count income from certain employment-related assets, such as retirement accounts, under set rules. Non-QM asset depletion loans, which fall outside the standard agency rules, divide eligible assets over a set period to create qualifying income. Terms, costs and requirements on these loans differ from standard loans.

Under [Fannie Mae's employment-related assets rule](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.4-06/employment-related-assets-qualifying-income), the lender divides your net documented assets by the loan's term in months. Only assets from work count, such as a 401(k), an IRA or a severance payout. An inheritance or the proceeds of a home sale do not. `[VERIFY: Fannie Mae B3-3.4-06 eligibility, loan-to-value and transaction limits]`

Non-qualified mortgage (non-QM) loans are not sold to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, so each lender sets its own method. Align's page on [asset depletion loans in Michigan](/product-loans/asset-based-income-loans/) explains that path.

## What are the mortgage options for people over 60?

People over 60 have the same options as any borrower: conventional loans, FHA loans, VA loans for eligible veterans and USDA loans in eligible areas. Some also buy with cash, use a bridge loan or a buy-before-you-sell plan, or, as a separate product, take a reverse mortgage, which is for homeowners 62 and older.

- **Conventional loans** follow Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac rules, and lenders may add overlays.
- **FHA loans** are insured by HUD and made by FHA-approved lenders, with more room on credit history in exchange for longer-lasting mortgage insurance.
- **VA loans** serve eligible veterans; **USDA loans** serve eligible rural areas, with income limits.
- **[Bridge loans](/learn/bridge-loans/)** let some sellers buy first and repay from the sale.

A reverse mortgage (HECM) is a different product with HUD-required counseling; see [HUD's HECM page](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/housing/sfh/hecm/hecmabou). `[VERIFY: HUD HECM age rule, 62 and older]` `[DECISION D1 PENDING: link to /learn/reverse-mortgages-how-they-work/ only if Align confirms it brokers reverse mortgages]`

For features such as a step-free entry, see the guide to [buying a home for aging in place](/learn/buying-home-for-aging-in-place/).

## What happens to your property taxes when you downsize in Michigan?

Buying a new Michigan home resets its taxable value the next year to the state equalized value, half of the assessor's market value, not automatically half the price you paid. Because your old home's taxable value was capped for years, a smaller home can cost as much or more in property tax.

While one owner keeps a home, [Michigan law](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-27a) caps the yearly rise in taxable value at inflation or 5%, whichever is lower. A sale ends the cap. Align's [2026 Michigan Property Tax Uncapping Study](/research/michigan-property-tax-uncapping-study/) puts the typical statewide jump the year after a sale at about 50.5%. See [how Michigan taxable value resets after a sale](/learn/michigan-taxable-value/).

**Hypothetical example, not an offer. Actual rates, costs and payments vary.**

You sell a home whose taxable value had been capped at about $90,000 and buy a $260,000 condo. If the assessor's market value matches the price, the condo's taxable value after the reset is about $130,000. Both use 36.0 mills, the 2025 statewide median homestead millage.

| Property tax only | Old home | New condo, after the reset |
|---|---|---|
| Taxable value | about $90,000 | about $130,000 |
| Millage (statewide median homestead, 2025) | 36.0 mills | 36.0 mills |
| Annual property tax | about $3,240 | about $4,680 |

Annual tax = taxable value ÷ 1,000 × millage. Taxes only, no loan figures. Your local units set your actual millage, and homestead millage assumes you file the Principal Residence Exemption. Source: Align's 2026 Uncapping Study.

Before an offer, [estimate your Michigan property tax after you buy](/michigan-property-tax-calculator/).

## What Michigan property tax help is there for seniors?

There is no automatic senior property tax cut in Michigan. Older homeowners may qualify for the homestead property tax credit on the state income tax return, a deferment that lets some homeowners 62 and older pay summer taxes later, and, with low income, a poverty exemption granted by the local board of review.

- **Homestead property tax credit.** Claimed on Form MI-1040CR under state income and taxable value limits; claimants 65 or older can receive a larger share. See [Treasury's credit page](https://www.michigan.gov/taxes/iit/tax-guidance/credits-exemptions/hptc) and Align's guide to the Michigan homestead property tax credit. `[LINK WHEN LIVE: /learn/michigan-homestead-property-tax-credit/]` `[VERIFY: senior share and current limits, MI-1040CR instructions]`
- **Summer tax deferment.** Homeowners 62 or older with prior-year household income of $40,000 or less can ask the local treasurer to defer the summer bill to February 14 without penalty, under [MCL 211.51](https://legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-211-51). `[VERIFY: income limit, other eligible groups, September 14 or 15 filing deadline, Treasury]`
- **Poverty exemption.** Homeowners of any age with low income can apply to the local board of review (Forms 5737 and 5739) for a full or partial reduction. See [Treasury's poverty exemption fact sheet](https://www.michigan.gov/documents/treasury/Poverty_Exemption_Taxpayer_Fact_Sheet_728331_7.pdf). `[VERIFY: MCL 211.7u, one-year term, local guidelines]`

All three are separate from [Michigan's Principal Residence Exemption](/learn/homestead-exemption-benefits-and-filing-guide/), which exempts your home from up to 18 mills of school operating tax. File your own by June 1 or November 1.

## Should you pay off your mortgage before you retire?

It depends on your income, savings, taxes and the interest rate on your loan. Paying it off removes a monthly payment, while keeping it can leave savings available for health care, repairs or other needs. There is no single right answer, so compare your own numbers and talk with a tax or financial professional.

Three questions usually decide it:

- **Where would the payoff money come from?** A large withdrawal from a pre-tax retirement account can raise that year's taxable income.
- **How much cash do you want on hand?** Home equity is harder to reach later; borrowing against it means qualifying again.
- **What continues either way?** Property taxes and insurance, paid directly after a payoff.

## Questions about mortgages for older homebuyers

### Can a 75-year-old get a 30-year mortgage?

Yes. Under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act and Regulation B, a lender cannot refuse you or offer only a shorter term because you are 75. You still qualify on income, credit and the property, like any borrower. The right term depends on your plans and budget.

### Is 55 too old to buy a house?

No. There is no upper age limit for buying a home or getting a mortgage, and lenders may not use your age against you. What matters is whether your income, savings and credit support the loan, and whether the home fits your plans.

### Can you buy a house on Social Security alone?

Yes, if the benefit covers the payment and your other debts within the lender's limits. Social Security retirement and disability income count with an award letter or tax records, and the non-taxable part may be grossed up. Savings or a co-borrower can also help.

### What mortgage options do people over 70 have?

The right option depends on your income, savings, equity and plans. People over 70 can use conventional, FHA, VA (if eligible) or USDA (in eligible areas) loans, buy with cash, or use a bridge loan. Homeowners 62 and older can also look at HUD's separate reverse mortgage program.

### Do lenders count retirement account withdrawals as income?

Yes, under set rules. Fannie Mae counts withdrawals from an IRA or 401(k) when you document them and show they can continue for at least three years from the note date. Some programs can also count eligible retirement savings as income.

### Do property taxes go down when you turn 65 in Michigan?

No. Michigan has no automatic property tax cut at 65. Older homeowners may qualify for a larger share of the homestead property tax credit, a summer tax deferment from age 62 with an income limit, and, with low income, a poverty exemption. Each requires a claim or application.

### Is it smart to pay off your house when you retire?

It depends on your situation. Paying off the loan removes a payment, but it can use savings you may need later, and pre-tax withdrawals can raise your taxable income. Compare your numbers with a tax or financial professional; this is general education, not advice.
